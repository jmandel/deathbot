# Maryland (MD)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** an advance directive appointing a "health care agent", under the Health Care Decisions Act (Md. Code, Health–General §§ 5-601 to 5-626). Part I of the statutory form "Maryland Advance Directive: Planning for Future Health Care Decisions" (Health–General § 5-603) is "Selection of Health Care Agent." The form is optional, and other forms are also valid.
- **Official copy:** the Attorney General's Health Policy Division publishes the form: https://www.marylandattorneygeneral.gov/Health%20Policy%20Documents/adirective.pdf. The Department of Health posts an accessible version based on the May 2024 form.
- **Signing:** the directive must be dated and signed by the person, or at their express direction. Two witnesses must sign in the person's physical or electronic presence (§ 5-602(c)(1)). No notary is needed. An electronic advance directive is allowed.
- **Who may witness:** any competent adult, including the person's physician, NP, PA or facility employee (§ 5-602(c)(2)), except that:
  - the health care agent may not witness, and
  - at least one witness must not knowingly be entitled to any part of the estate or to any financial benefit from the person's death.
- **Video directive:** the state health information exchange (CRISP) may accept an unwitnessed, dated video directive stored with an electronic advance directives service the Maryland Health Care Commission recognizes (§ 5-602(c)(3)).
- **Oral directive:** valid if made before the attending physician, PA or NP and one witness, and documented in the medical record (§ 5-602(d)).
- **Who cannot be the agent** (§ 5-602(b)(3)):
  - An owner, operator or employee of a health care facility where the person is receiving care, or their spouse, parent, child or sibling. The bar does not apply if that person would qualify as a surrogate under § 5-605, or was appointed before the person began receiving care from the facility.
  - Someone subject to a protective order protecting the person.
  - A spouse after a separation agreement or a divorce filing, unless the person indicates otherwise.
- **When it takes effect:** unless the document says otherwise, when the attending physician and a second physician certify in writing that the person cannot make an informed decision (§ 5-602(e)).
- **Agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker** (Health–General § 5-605(a)), in this order:
  1. A guardian.
  2. The spouse or domestic partner.
  3. An adult child.
  4. A parent.
  5. An adult brother or sister.
  6. A friend or other relative who gives the attending physician an affidavit showing regular contact with the person and familiarity with their activities, health and beliefs.

  Someone subject to a protective order, or a spouse after a separation agreement or divorce filing, is excluded.

## Written instructions (living will)
- **Form:** Part II of the same statutory form, "Treatment Preferences ('Living Will')" (§ 5-603). It covers three situations: terminal condition, persistent vegetative state, and end-stage condition. The person may fill in Part I, Part II or both. The signing rules are the same as above.
- **Tube feeding:** the form offers separate choices for or against "nutrition and fluids by tube or other medical means."
- **Pregnancy:** there is an optional pregnancy line where the person writes instructions for the agent. Leaving it blank does not affect validity. No statutory pregnancy override was found.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** MOLST (Maryland Medical Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment), under Health–General § 5-608.1. The form is developed by the Department of Health with MIEMSS and the Board of Physicians. It is not an advance directive.
- **Who signs:** a physician, nurse practitioner or physician assistant. The patient does not sign. The practitioner certifies that the orders follow a discussion with, and the informed consent of, the patient, the agent or the surrogate. The patient or decision maker must get a copy within 48 hours.
- **Required in facilities:** hospitals, nursing homes, assisted living, home health, hospice and dialysis centers must complete or accept a MOLST at admission or at discharge to another facility.
- **Getting the form:** the public can download it at https://marylandmolst.org/pages/MolstForm.html. Not confirmed: the copy posted there is the 2013 "MM3" version, and no newer version was found.
- **Copies:** the original, a copy and a fax are all valid. A MOLST does not expire.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- MOLST replaced the separate EMS/DNR form on January 1, 2013. Prior EMS/DNR forms are still honored.
- A MOLST "no CPR" order has the same effect as an EMS DNR order (§ 5-608(a)(4)). EMS may follow only an EMS DNR order, a MOLST, or an oral DNR order from the online medical command physician or from a physician, PA or NP physically present (§ 5-608(c)).
- **Bracelets:** a paper bracelet insert comes with the MOLST, and plastic bracelets are available from MIEMSS (410-706-4367). Metal bracelets or necklaces come from MedicAlert (800-432-5378), which requires a copy of the order.

## Registries
- **Advance directive:** MyDirectives, an electronic advance directives service recognized by the Maryland Health Care Commission and linked to CRISP. Uploading is free and residents file themselves (§ 5-623).
- **POLST:** no dedicated registry. Some MOLST forms flow to CRISP.
- **EMS DNR:** none.

## Documents from other states
- An advance directive, an EMS DNR order or a life-sustaining treatment order made in another state is valid if it complies with Maryland law or the law of the state where it was made. It is given effect as far as Maryland law permits (Health–General § 5-617).

## Also in this state
- **Mental health:** an advance directive for mental health services (Health–General § 5-602.1) follows the same signing rules as § 5-602.
- **Organ donation:** the statutory form points to a separate organ donor form.
