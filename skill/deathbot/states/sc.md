# South Carolina (SC)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Health Care Power of Attorney (S.C. Statutory Form)", S.C. Code 62-5-503 and 62-5-504. A health care power of attorney signed on or after January 1, 2007 must be substantially in the statutory form, printed in full in 62-5-504 (https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t62c005.php).
- **Signing:** dated and signed by the person (or by another in their presence and at their direction) and signed by two witnesses who saw the signing or heard the person acknowledge it (62-5-503). The form has a notary block; notarizing is not required by the text.
- **Who may not witness:** relatives by blood, marriage or adoption (spouse, descendants, ancestors, siblings and their descendants, and their spouses); anyone financially responsible for the person's care; heirs, will beneficiaries and life insurance beneficiaries; anyone with a claim against the estate; the agent or successor agent; the person's physician or the physician's employee. In a health facility, at most one witness may be a facility employee.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** anyone under 18 or not of sound mind; a health care provider, or provider employee, with a provider-patient relationship with the person when the document is signed; an employee of a nursing care facility where the person lives; the spouse of any of these. Relatives are excepted (62-5-503(a)(4)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** S.C. Code 44-66-30 (Adult Health Care Consent Act): guardian; attorney-in-fact; spouse (unless separated by court order or agreement); majority of adult children; parent; majority of adult siblings; majority of grandparents; another adult relative with a close relationship; a person authorized by other statute; last, someone with an established relationship who is not a paid caregiver or provider.
- **Tube feeding:** the person initials one of three options in paragraph 8. If none is initialed, the agent cannot direct withdrawal of nutrition and hydration needed for comfort or pain relief (62-5-503(b)).
- **Pregnancy:** life-sustaining procedures may not be withheld or withdrawn under the power of attorney during a diagnosed pregnancy (62-5-507).

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Declaration of a Desire for a Natural Death", Death With Dignity Act, S.C. Code 44-77-10 to 44-77-160; must be substantially in the form in 44-77-50. A separate document, not combined with the health care power of attorney.
- **Signing:** signed and dated before an officer authorized to administer oaths (notary) and two witnesses. The notary may also serve as one of the two witnesses. The witnesses sign an affidavit, and at least one swears to it before the notary (44-77-40). The same people are barred from witnessing as for the power of attorney.
- **Ombudsman witness:** if the person is a hospital patient or nursing care facility resident when signing, one of the two witnesses must be an ombudsman designated by the State Ombudsman (44-77-40(3)).
- It applies only after two physicians certify a terminal condition or permanent unconsciousness (generally 90 days). The person initials whether tube nutrition and hydration count as life-sustaining procedures; with no instruction they are given for comfort. A declaration has no effect during a diagnosed pregnancy (44-77-70).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "South Carolina Physician Orders for Scope of Treatment" (SC POST), POST Act, S.C. Code 44-80-10 to 44-80-120 (effective May 24, 2019). The current form is DPH 4061 (07/2024) from the Department of Public Health, which shows a printable sample. Signed by a physician, an APRN under a practice agreement, or a PA within scope (44-80-110, 44-80-120). The signature of the patient or legal representative is also required on the form. A copy, fax or electronic version of a completed form is legal (44-80-30(B)). Use is limited to serious illness.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "South Carolina EMS Do Not Resuscitate Order", EMS Do Not Resuscitate Order Act, S.C. Code 44-78-10 to 44-78-65; form in 44-78-30. Only for a patient with a terminal condition. Requested by the patient, the surrogate under the Adult Health Care Consent Act, the health care agent, or a parent or guardian of a terminally ill child; signed by the requester and a physician (MD or DO). The physician affixes a bracelet reading "South Carolina Do Not Resuscitate EMS" or gives an order form for an approved vendor; the patient or family pays. EMS withholds resuscitation when the order or bracelet is presented (44-78-25). 2026 Act No. 146 moved oversight from DHEC to the Department of Public Health.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: unclear. The My Life My Choices eRegistry is for clinicians only, and its current use is unconfirmed. Mind My Health offers free consumer storage; it is not a state service.
- POLST registry: same eRegistry, unclear.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- A POST-type form made in another jurisdiction under its law is a valid expression of the patient's wishes (44-80-40(A)). A declaration from another state is valid if it complies with the law of the state of domicile and expresses substantially the same intent as the SC form (44-77-20, 44-77-30). Not confirmed: whether an out-of-state health care power of attorney is honored; 62-5-502(b) says one that does not meet SC requirements is not governed by that section. Not confirmed: recognition of out-of-state EMS DNR orders.
