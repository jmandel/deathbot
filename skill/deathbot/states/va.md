# Virginia (VA)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Advance Medical Directive," Virginia Health Care Decisions Act, Code of Virginia Title 54.1, Chapter 29, Article 8 (§ 54.1-2981 et seq.). The suggested form in § 54.1-2984 is optional ("may, but need not"). One document can name an agent, give instructions, and make an anatomical gift (§ 54.1-2983). The Virginia Health Information (VHI) registry page points to forms from the Virginia State Bar and Honoring Choices Virginia: https://vhi.org/advance-care-planning-registry/
- **Signing:** signed by the person in front of two adult witnesses (§ 54.1-2983). No notary is needed. Any adult may witness, including a spouse or blood relative. Employees of health care facilities and physician offices acting in good faith may also witness (§ 54.1-2982, "Witness"). A person with a terminal diagnosis may make an oral directive in front of the attending physician and two witnesses.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the Act sets no restriction beyond the agent being an adult (§ 54.1-2982).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** § 54.1-2986, in order: guardian; spouse (unless a divorce action is filed and not final); adult child; parent; adult sibling; other relatives in descending order of blood relationship; then any adult who has shown special care and concern and knows the person's values and religious beliefs. That last adult may not be staff of a provider caring for the person, and may not decide on withholding or withdrawing life-prolonging procedures. If members of one class disagree, the physician may follow the majority.

## Written instructions (living will)
- Part of the same Advance Medical Directive: one document can both give instructions and name an agent (§ 54.1-2983). Same two-witness rule.
- **Decisions over protest:** an agent may override a later objection by the person only if the directive explicitly allows it and, when the directive was signed, a physician, PA, APRN, clinical psychologist, professional counselor or clinical social worker who knows the person attested in writing that the person understood. This cannot be used to withhold or withdraw life-prolonging procedures (§ 54.1-2986.2).
- VHI lists optional amendments for life-prolonging treatment during pregnancy and for the agent's power to authorize care over objection.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- The National POLST Form (eff. April 2023) is approved in Virginia. The older "Virginia Physician Orders for Scope of Treatment" (POST, rev. 5/2017) is still recognized, but moving to POLST is recommended. In the regulations, a POST or POLST counts as a Durable DNR Order (12VAC5-66). The form is signed by a physician and by the patient or the patient's authorized representative. APRN and PA signatures count wherever the law requires a physician's (§ 54.1-2957.02, § 54.1-2952.2). Clinicians complete it.
- An original or a legible photocopy is valid (12VAC5-66).

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "Durable Do Not Resuscitate Order" (DDNR), § 54.1-2987.1 and 12VAC5-66. It is issued on the Board of Health form by a physician (APRN and PA signatures count, as above) with the consent of the patient or the person authorized to consent for them. The physician and the patient (or that authorized person) both sign the form (12VAC5-66-10). It is valid until revoked. Before withholding CPR, EMS must find an intact original or photocopy of the DDNR, POST or POLST form, or approved jewelry, and confirm the patient's identity by photo ID or by someone who knows them (12VAC5-66). Only the patient can revoke an order they consented to themselves. EMS and licensed facilities may follow it.
- Alternate Durable DNR jewelry (bracelet or necklace) is available from sellers approved by the VDH Office of EMS. Photocopies are valid. The form and bracelet instructions are at https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/emergency-medical-services/durable-dnr/

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: Advance Health Care Planning Registry, run by VHI and stored in the U.S. Living Will Registry database. It is free, and the signer files their own documents (§ 54.1-2994 et seq.).
- POLST registry: the statute allows filing a POST/POLST in the same registry (§ 54.1-2995). Not confirmed: whether the registry accepts these in practice.
- EMS DNR registry: the statute allows filing a DDNR in the same registry. Not confirmed: whether it is accepted in practice.

## Documents from other states
- An advance directive made in another state is valid if it met Virginia law or the law of the state where it was made. Virginia law governs how it is read (§ 54.1-2993).
- A DNR or other order about life-prolonging procedures made under another state's law is valid in Virginia (§ 54.1-2987.1(C)).

## Also in this state
- A directive may address "any or all forms of health care" (§ 54.1-2983), including mental health care. Some Virginia health systems add a "Virginia Advance Directive Supplement for Mental Health Conditions."
- Trained "qualified advance directive facilitators" and providers may help people complete the § 54.1-2984 form without it being the practice of law (§ 54.1-2988.1).
- A directive may let an agent approve taking part in IRB-approved research (§ 54.1-2983.1).
