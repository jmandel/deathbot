# Vermont (VT)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Advance Directive," 18 V.S.A. Chapter 231 (§§ 9700–9731). One document can name a health care agent and give instructions (§ 9702). There is no required statutory form. The Department of Health points to optional forms from the Vermont Ethics Network (VEN): the "Appointment of a Health Care Agent" form, the "Advance Directive, Short Form," the "Advance Directive, Long Form," and a PREPARE easy-read form. No third party may demand a different form (§ 9717). Download: https://www.healthvermont.gov/systems/advance-directives/create-register-and-make-changes-advance-directive
- **Signing:** dated and signed by the person, or by someone else in their presence at their direction, and signed by two or more adult witnesses. The witnesses affirm the person seemed to understand and was free of pressure (§ 9703(b)). No notary is needed. These people may not witness: the agent, the person's spouse, parent, adult sibling, adult child or adult grandchild (§ 9703(c)). A health care provider may witness. A remote witness (by audio-video or phone) is allowed if the two know each other and the person writes the witness's name, contact details and relationship on the directive.
- **Extra rule for hospitals and nursing homes:** if the person is being admitted to, or is in, a nursing home or residential care facility, the directive is not effective unless someone explains it and signs a statement saying so (§ 9703(d)). That person may be an ombudsman, clergy member, Vermont attorney, Probate Division designee, hospital-designated explainer, mental health patient representative, a trained unpaid volunteer, or a clinician not employed by the facility. For hospital patients the list is narrower: ombudsman, clergy, attorney, Probate designee, hospital designee or mental health patient representative (§ 9703(e)). The explainer may also be one of the witnesses.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the person's health care provider; and, unless related by blood, marriage, civil union or adoption, an owner, operator, employee, agent or contractor of a residential care, health care or correctional facility where the person lives when signing (§ 9702(c)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required. The person may state in the directive that the agent has accepted (§ 9702(a)(2)).
- **Default decision-maker:** Vermont has no general priority list. A surrogate is provided only for consent to a DNR or COLST order (§ 9731). The person may name a surrogate by telling their clinician. Otherwise the clinician tries to notify all reasonably available "interested individuals," and one who is willing to decide according to the patient's wishes serves (§ 9731(c)). Only one surrogate acts at a time. Health care providers and facility staff cannot serve unless related.

## Written instructions (living will)
- Part of the same Advance Directive. Instructions may cover life-sustaining treatment, tube feeding and hydration (§ 9702(a)(7)), and what the person wants if pregnant (§ 9702(a)(8)). Same signing rules.
- Copies and faxes are relied on like the original (§ 9717).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "Clinician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment" (COLST), issued on the Department of Health's combined "Vermont DNR/COLST form" (§ 9708(b)). The form was last updated June 2022. The patient's clinician (MD, DO, APRN or PA) signs it. The form names the patient, agent, guardian or surrogate who gave consent (§ 9708(e)). The public can download it, but a clinician must complete it: https://www.healthvermont.gov/systems/advance-directives/do-not-resuscitate-dnrcolst
- Copies: the form itself states "Photocopies and faxes of signed DNR/COLST order are legal and valid." Verbal orders are allowed with a follow-up clinician signature. The Department advises keeping the original on the refrigerator at home and carrying copies.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- The same "Vermont DNR/COLST form," signed by the clinician (§ 9708(d)). The patient's or surrogate's signature is "if available; not required" on the form; it names the patient, agent, guardian or surrogate who consented, or instead carries a certification by the clinician and a second named clinician that resuscitation would not prevent imminent death (§ 9708(d)(3)). A clinician who issues a DNR must authorize a DNR identification (§ 9708(h)). Approved vendors: MedicAlert and StickyJ. EMS honors a displayed DNR/COLST or DNR ID.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: Vermont Advance Directive Registry (VADR), run by VEN on the U.S. Advance Care Plan Registry platform. It is free, and the person files it themselves online or by mail, fax or email. Mail or fax filings need the registration agreement form. All hospitals can search it, and facilities must check it for patients without capacity (§ 9709(b)(3)).
- POLST registry: none. A COLST is probably not registrable.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- An advance directive made in another state under that state's law stays enforceable (§ 9716).
- Providers must honor out-of-state DNR orders, orders for life-sustaining treatment and DNR IDs in good faith (§ 9708(k)). Out-of-state clinicians count as clinicians for this (§ 9708(a)).

## Also in this state
- A directive may include mental health instructions. It may also include a clause letting the agent authorize or refuse care over the person's later objection (§ 9702(a)(6), § 9707(h)). The VEN Long Form and the Disability Rights Vermont form cover this.
- The directive may also cover anatomical gifts, disposal of remains, and a guardian nomination (§ 9702(a)(14)–(18)).
