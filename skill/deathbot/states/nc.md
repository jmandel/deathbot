# North Carolina (NC)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Health Care Power of Attorney", N.C.G.S. Chapter 32A, Article 3 (32A-15 to 32A-27); statutory form at 32A-25.1. The form is optional, but any document must substantially meet the Article (32A-16(3)). The Secretary of State's Advance Health Care Directives page links to the statutory forms (sosnc.gov, "Download Forms").
- **Signing:** both two qualified witnesses and a notary. The person signs in front of the witnesses, and the signature is acknowledged before a notary (32A-16(3)). A qualified witness believes the person to be of sound mind and states that they are not: related within the third degree to the person or the person's spouse; an expected heir or will beneficiary; the attending physician or mental health treatment provider, or a licensed health care provider who is that provider's paid employee; a paid employee of a health facility where the person is a patient, or of a nursing home or adult care home where they live; or someone with a claim against the estate (32A-16(6)). The notary may be an employee of those facilities. The form tells the person not to sign until both witnesses and the notary are present.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** anyone providing health care to the person for pay (32A-18). The agent must be a competent adult.
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** N.C.G.S. 90-21.13(c), in order: court-appointed guardian of the person (but a valid health care agent keeps their authority unless the clerk suspends it); health care agent; another agent with health care powers; spouse; a majority of reasonably available parents and adult children; a majority of reasonably available adult siblings; an individual with an established relationship who acts in good faith and can reliably convey the person's wishes. If no one is available, the attending physician may treat with a second physician's confirmation. The same order governs withholding life-prolonging measures without a living will. That requires the attending physician and a second physician to find the person permanently lacks capacity and is terminally ill or permanently unconscious (90-322).

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Advance Directive for a Natural Death ('Living Will')", N.C.G.S. 90-320 to 90-323; statutory form at 90-321(d1). It is separate from the health care power of attorney. Same signing rule: two qualified witnesses (same disqualifications) and proof before a notary or a clerk or assistant clerk of superior court (90-321(c)). It applies to the conditions the person initials: incurable or irreversible condition leading to death soon; permanent unconsciousness; or advanced dementia or other irreversible substantial loss of cognitive ability. A second physician must confirm the condition (90-321(b)).
- **Tube feeding:** artificial nutrition and hydration are life-prolonging measures (32A-16(4)). Under the form they are withheld with other measures unless the person initials an exception to keep both, only fluids, or only nutrition. The law has no pregnancy clause.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "Medical Order for Scope of Treatment" (MOST), N.C.G.S. 90-21.17; the form is from DHHS's Office of Emergency Medical Services. It is authorized by a physician, PA or NP and signed by the patient or the patient's representative (the 90-322 order). If the representative cannot sign the original, they sign a copy that goes in the record, and the original is marked "on file". It must be printed on "Astrobrights Pulsar" pink paper. Forms are downloadable only by physicians' offices and licensed facilities, not the public. The original must travel with the patient. Copies are for the medical record only (North Carolina Medical Society MOST FAQ).

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "Portable DNR order", N.C.G.S. 90-21.17, on the OEMS form printed on goldenrod paper. It is issued with consent of the patient, a minor's parent or guardian, or the representative. The statute names the physician. Not confirmed: whether a PA or NP may sign the portable DNR form (secondary sources say yes). EMS and other providers are immune when they rely on an original form (90-21.17(d)). Forms come from clinicians and facilities only. Not confirmed: a current state-approved DNR bracelet.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: Advance Health Care Directive Registry (Secretary of State). Active; the signer files notarized documents for $10. Access is by wallet card with file number, password and (since February 2022) a QR code.
- POLST registry: none for MOST.
- EMS DNR registry: none; portable DNR on goldenrod paper.

## Documents from other states
- A health care power of attorney or similar document made elsewhere is valid if it appears to meet that jurisdiction's or North Carolina's requirements (32A-27). Not confirmed: a parallel section for out-of-state living wills, or for out-of-state POLST and DNR forms.

## Also in this state
- "Advance Instruction for Mental Health Treatment", N.C.G.S. 122C-71 to 122C-77 (form at 122C-77). It needs two qualified witnesses and a notary (122C-72(1)). A witness may not be the attending physician or mental health provider or their employee, an owner, operator or employee of the facility where the person lives or is a patient, or a relative within the third degree (122C-72(7)). It may be combined with a health care power of attorney if each part follows its own statute (122C-73(f)).
- The statutory health care power of attorney form lets the agent decide organ donation and disposition of remains only if the person initials those powers.
