# Oregon (OR)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Oregon Advance Directive for Health Care" (form OHA 3905), adopted under the Oregon Health Care Decisions Act, ORS 127.505 to 127.660, and enacted as amended in Senate Bill 199 (2021). The Oregon Health Authority's Advance Directive Adoption Committee keeps it. OHA also publishes a separate "Form for Appointment of Health Care Representative and Alternate" and a user guide (OHA 3942). Any form used must be substantially the same as the OHA form. Free download, in English and many other languages plus large print, from the OHA ADAC Forms page: https://www.oregon.gov/oha/PH/ABOUT/Pages/ADAC-Forms.aspx
- **Signing:** signed by the person and either witnessed by at least two adults or notarized (ORS 127.515). A witness may not be the person's attending physician or attending health care provider, or the named health care representative or alternate. If the person is a patient in a long-term care facility, one witness must be someone the facility designates under Department of Human Services rules.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** unless related by blood, marriage or adoption, the attending physician or attending health care provider or their employee, and an owner, operator or employee of a facility where the person is a patient or resident (unless appointed before admission). Also a parent or former guardian whose rights a court removed (ORS 127.520).
- **The agent's acceptance:** required. The health care representative accepts by signing the acceptance of appointment on the form, or by telling a third party they accept (ORS 127.525). The appointment takes effect only when the representative accepts it (ORS 127.510(2)(d)).
- **Default decision-maker:** ORS 127.635: guardian with health care authority; spouse; an adult designated by the others on this list if none object; majority of adult children; either parent; majority of adult siblings; any adult relative or adult friend. If no one is available, the attending provider may decide. Withdrawal of life support needs a medically confirmed terminal condition, permanent unconsciousness, severe pain with no benefit, or advanced progressive fatal illness.

## Written instructions (living will)
- Part of the same OHA 3905 Advance Directive (health care instructions section); same signing rules.
- **Tube feeding:** an appointed representative cannot withhold or withdraw artificially administered nutrition and hydration needed to sustain life unless the person authorized it or ORS 127.580 applies (ORS 127.540). The representative also cannot consent to convulsive treatment, psychosurgery, sterilization or abortion.
- A representative may withdraw life support only if given that authority or the person meets one of the medical conditions above (ORS 127.540).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "Oregon POLST" (Portable Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment), ORS 127.663 to 127.684, run by the Oregon POLST Program at OHSU. Signed by a physician (MD or DO), nurse practitioner, physician associate or naturopathic doctor, completed with the patient or, if the patient cannot speak for themselves, their health care decision-maker. A POLST can never be required by a clinician, facility or health system. The signed form must be in English; translated versions are for education only.
- Clinicians order forms from the program; the public may download a sample to prepare. Not confirmed: the form's wording on copies and faxes; a secondary source quotes the 2023 form as saying photocopies, faxes and electronically signed forms are valid.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- No separate state EMS DNR form. EMS follows Oregon POLST and can query the Oregon POLST Registry.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none; registry bills failed.
- POLST registry: Oregon POLST Registry (OHSU), active since 2009. Clinicians must submit each POLST unless the patient opts out in Section D (ORS 127.666). EMS can query it around the clock, and since December 2025 it is retrievable nationwide through MyDirectives' A|D Vault Exchange. No cost to the patient.
- EMS DNR registry: none separate; POLST registry serves EMS.

## Documents from other states
- A nonresident may sign an Oregon form while in Oregon. A directive signed by an adult living in another state is valid if it complies with the law of that state, the state where the person is located, or Oregon (ORS 127.515). Not confirmed: an Oregon statute or program statement on honoring out-of-state POLST forms.

## Also in this state
- Declaration for Mental Health Treatment, ORS 127.700 to 127.737 (form in ORS 127.736). Covers psychoactive medication, short-term admission, convulsive treatment and outpatient services, and can name a representative, who signs an acceptance. Notarized or signed by two adult witnesses who personally know the person and are not relatives or the named representative. It lasts three years, or longer while the person stays incapable.
