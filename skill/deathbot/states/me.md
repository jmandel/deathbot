# Maine (ME)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Power of Attorney for Health Care", under the Maine Uniform Health Care Decisions Act (18-C M.R.S. §§ 5-801 to 5-818). It is Part 1 of the optional statutory "Advance Health Care Directive" form (18-C M.R.S. § 5-805). The form also covers instructions, organ donation and a primary physician. No particular form is required.
- **Official copy:** the form text is in the statute at https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/18-C/title18-Csec5-805.html
- **Signing:** the person and 2 witnesses must sign (§ 5-803(2)). No notary is needed.
  - Signatures must be made in person, not electronically.
  - The statute lists no witness disqualifications.
  - Exception for isolation: when a person in a hospital or residential health care facility has an infectious disease and isolation keeps signers out of the room, the person may direct someone elsewhere in the facility to sign for them, using 2-way audiovisual technology for the direction and the witnessing. The signed original goes into the medical record and a copy goes to the person (§ 5-803-A).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** an owner, operator or employee of a residential long-term health care institution where the person is receiving care. This bar does not apply if the agent is related by blood, marriage or adoption (§ 5-803(2)).
- **When it takes effect:** unless the document says otherwise, the agent acts only after a finding that the person lacks capacity, made by the primary physician, a court, or (for mental health treatment directives) an examiner under 34-B M.R.S. § 3863 (§ 5-803(3)–(4)).
- **Agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Divorce:** divorce or legal separation revokes a spouse's appointment as agent (§ 5-804(4)).
- **Default decision-maker** (18-C M.R.S. § 5-806(2)), in this order:
  1. The spouse, unless legally separated.
  2. An adult in a spouse-like emotional, physical and financial relationship with the person.
  3. An adult child.
  4. A parent.
  5. An adult brother or sister.
  6. An adult grandchild.
  7. An adult niece or nephew.
  8. An adult aunt or uncle.
  9. Another adult relative familiar with the person's values.
  10. An adult who has shown special concern for the person (§ 5-806(3)).

  A surrogate may not refuse lifesaving, medically necessary surgery or procedures. A surrogate may withhold or withdraw life-sustaining treatment only when the person is in a terminal condition or a persistent vegetative state (§ 5-806(1)).

## Written instructions (living will)
- **Form:** "individual instruction", which is Part 2 of the same statutory Advance Health Care Directive form (§§ 5-803(1), 5-805). A written instruction needs no witnesses. Two witnesses are needed only for the power of attorney part.
- **Oral instructions:** valid only if made to a health care provider or to someone who could serve as surrogate.
- **Tube feeding:** the form has a box to require artificial nutrition and hydration "regardless of my condition." An agent can decide on artificial nutrition unless the person limits that.
- **Pregnancy:** the statute has no pregnancy clause.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** Maine POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment). The Maine State POLST Program is housed with the Maine Hospice Council: https://mainehospicecouncil.org (207-626-0651). No POLST statute was found.
- **Who signs:** a physician, nurse practitioner or physician assistant. The patient or surrogate also signs. Not confirmed: the signer rules come from a nurse practitioner association fact sheet and from Maine EMS guidance, not from a program page that could be opened.
- **Getting the form:** clinicians get it.
- **EMS:** a 2021 Maine EMS clinical bulletin says EMS should honor a POLST/MOLST or other written order from the patient's physician, PA or NP if the crew can understand it. The bulletin also dropped the old one-year date limit.
- **Copies:** Not confirmed: no statement on whether copies are valid was found.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Program:** Maine EMS Comfort Care/DNR Program, with two forms:
  - **Comfort Care/DNR Directive:** for an adult 18 or older who can make the decision. The person signs, and so does a physician, nurse practitioner or physician assistant who attests to the person's capacity; the form is not valid until both sign. The clinician is not obliged to sign.
  - **Comfort Care/DNR Order:** for a patient without capacity. The patient's physician and the guardian or surrogate sign.
- **Where to get it:** https://www11.maine.gov/ems/protocols-resources/comfort-care-dnr-program
- **Jewelry and older forms:** Maine EMS-approved Comfort Care/DNR jewelry (such as MedicAlert) is honored in place of the form. The pre-2007 orange forms, wallet cards and jewelry are still honored but no longer issued.
- **Copies:** copies may be given to the local EMS agency and hospital, but EMS withholds resuscitation only on seeing the original form or the jewelry.
- **POLST:** EMS also honors a POLST, as described above.

## Registries
- **Advance directive:** none. HealthInfoNet, the state's health information exchange, ran a pilot in 2022.
- **POLST:** none.
- **EMS DNR:** none.

## Documents from other states
- An advance health care directive is valid in Maine if it complies with Maine law, wherever it was signed, or if it is valid under the law of the state where it was made (18-C M.R.S. § 5-803(8)).
- A copy has the same effect as the original (§ 5-813).
- Not confirmed: no Maine EMS statement on out-of-state POLST or DNR forms was found.

## Also in this state
- **Mental health treatment:** an advance health care directive can direct mental health treatment (§ 5-803(9), read with 34-B M.R.S. §§ 3831 and 3862). Capacity for that part can be decided by an examiner qualified under 34-B M.R.S. § 3863.
- **Mental health institutions:** an agent or surrogate cannot consent to admission to a mental health institution unless the directive expressly allows it (§ 5-814(5)).
- **Organ donation:** Part 3 of the statutory form is an organ and tissue donation section.
- **Military directives:** military advance medical directives are recognized (§ 5-817).
