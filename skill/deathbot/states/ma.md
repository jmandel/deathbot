# Massachusetts (MA)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Health Care Proxy", which names a "health care agent", under M.G.L. c. 201D.
- **Required content:** the statute has no mandatory form. A proxy must identify the person and the agent, state that the agent is to make health care decisions, describe any limits, and say the agent's authority starts when the person is found to lack capacity (c. 201D § 4).
- **Official copy:** mass.gov posts the Massachusetts Health Care Proxy form at https://www.mass.gov/doc/download-the-massachusetts-healthcare-proxy-form/download. The form is published by Massachusetts Health Decisions, and the Massachusetts Medical Society hosts it too.
- **Alternate agent:** a person may name one (§ 2).
- **Signing:** the person signs, or someone signs at their direction, in the presence of two adult witnesses. The witnesses sign and affirm in writing that the person appeared to be at least 18, of sound mind and under no constraint or undue influence (c. 201D § 2). No notary is needed.
- **Who may not witness:** a person named as agent (or alternate) in the proxy. No other witness restriction is in the statute.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** an operator, administrator or employee of a facility where the person is a patient or resident, or has applied for admission, when the proxy is signed. The bar does not apply if that person is related by blood, marriage or adoption (c. 201D § 3).
- **When it takes effect:** when the attending physician determines in writing that the person lacks capacity (§ 6).
- **Agent's authority:** the agent may make any health care decision the person could make, including about life-sustaining treatment, unless the proxy limits it. The agent's decision outranks anyone acting under a durable power of attorney (§ 5).
- **Agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** Massachusetts has no statutory surrogate consent list. Without a proxy, providers may rely on "the informed consent of responsible parties ... to the extent permitted by law" (c. 201D § 16). In practice that is family, and some decisions need a court-appointed guardian.

## Written instructions (living will)
- Massachusetts has no living will statute and no statutory living will form.
- A person can write instructions or limits into the health care proxy, which the agent must follow (§ 5).
- A separate written statement of wishes has no statutory force. It still guides the agent and clinicians.
- **Statutory limits:** none in c. 201D on pregnancy or tube feeding. The agent decides on artificial nutrition like any other treatment, unless the proxy limits it.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program now:** MOLST (Massachusetts Medical Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment), a bright pink form.
- **Who signs:** a clinician (physician, nurse practitioner or physician assistant) and the patient. If the patient cannot sign, the guardian or activated health care agent signs instead.
- **Getting the form:** clinicians complete it. Not confirmed: the instructions say photocopies, faxes and electronic copies of a properly signed MOLST are valid, in any color, if the patient section (D) and the clinician section (E) are complete. That comes from the program's EMS guidance as quoted in search results; mass.gov refused direct fetches.
- **Change coming:** Massachusetts is moving from MOLST to MassPOLST, the National POLST form, in spring 2027. The ePOLST Registry is set to go live on April 6, 2027, and no new MOLST forms are to be created after that date. Physicians (MD, DO), NPs and PAs may sign a POLST.
  - Until the switch, providers keep using MOLST.
  - MOLST forms made before go-live stay valid.
  - After go-live, POLSTs are created in the ePOLST Registry, and only registry-created POLSTs are presumed valid. The clinician prints the completed form for the patient to keep where emergency responders can find it. A POLST is never to be required.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Form:** the Comfort Care/DNR Order Verification form (Department of Public Health, Office of Emergency Medical Services). It remains valid, and EMS honors it alongside MOLST under the OEMS "MOLST and Comfort Care/DNR Order Verification Protocol."
- **Bracelet:** a Comfort Care/DNR bracelet exists. Not confirmed: current details on getting a bracelet were not found on an openable page.

## Registries
- **Advance directive:** none.
- **POLST:** an ePOLST registry run with the Massachusetts eHealth Institute is in pilot, going statewide April 2027. Patients will be able to view their forms.
- **EMS DNR:** none. EMS uses the Comfort Care/DNR verification protocol.

## Documents from other states
- A health care proxy or similar document made in another state under that state's law is enforceable in Massachusetts (c. 201D § 11). A provider who refuses on moral or religious grounds must arrange a transfer (§§ 14–15).
- Not confirmed: no OEMS or program statement on out-of-state POLST forms was found.

## Also in this state
- **No default surrogate:** Massachusetts has neither a living will statute nor a family-consent list. A health care proxy is the main way to make sure the right person can decide.
