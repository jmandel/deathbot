# Washington (WA)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Durable Power of Attorney for Health Care," made under the Washington Uniform Power of Attorney Act, RCW 11.125. Health care authority is set out in RCW 11.125.400. Washington has no statutory form for it. Not confirmed: a state agency page that publishes an official copy.
- **Signing:** signed and dated by the person, and either acknowledged before a notary or signed by two competent witnesses in the person's presence (RCW 11.125.050). A witness may not be a home care provider for the person, a care provider at the adult family home or long-term care facility where they live, or anyone related to the person or to the agent by blood, marriage or state registered domestic partnership.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the person's physicians, the physicians' employees, and owners, administrators or employees of the health care or long-term care facility where the person lives or gets care. The exception is a spouse or registered domestic partner, parent, adult child or sibling (RCW 11.125.400(3)). The agent's health care authority has the same limits as a guardian's under RCW 11.130, except where a mental health advance directive consents to inpatient admission or electroconvulsive therapy (RCW 11.125.400(3)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** RCW 7.70.065, in order: guardian; health care power-of-attorney agent; spouse or state registered domestic partner; adult children; parents; adult siblings; then adult grandchildren, adult nieces and nephews, and adult aunts and uncles, each only if familiar with the patient; then a close friend who signs a sworn declaration. The friend may not be the patient's physician or their employee, facility staff, or a paid caregiver. No one may consent if a higher-priority person has refused, or if members of the same class do not all agree (RCW 7.70.065(1)(b)).

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Health Care Directive," RCW 70.122.030. The statutory form there is optional and is separate from the power of attorney. It covers terminal and permanently unconscious conditions. It has a checkbox for whether the person wants artificially provided nutrition and hydration.
- **Signing:** notarized, or signed in front of two witnesses. Witnesses may not be related by blood or marriage, entitled to any part of the estate, the attending physician or their employee, an employee of a health facility where the person is a patient, or anyone with a claim against the estate.
- The current statutory form has no pregnancy clause.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "Washington POLST" (Portable Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment). The Washington State Medical Association (WSMA) distributes the form (2021 version). It must be signed by a physician (MD/DO), ARNP or PA-C, and by the patient or their legal decision-maker. Clinicians usually supply it. The public can download it or get one by sending WSMA a stamped, self-addressed envelope: https://www.wsma.org/polst
- Copies, digital images and faxes are valid, and so is a form printed on paper other than green.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- No separate EMS DNR form in use. Washington paramedics follow only the POLST form. Bracelets, necklaces and wallet cards may alert responders, but medics cannot act on them alone (WSMA). Not confirmed: whether the older "EMS-No CPR" form was formally retired.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none. The DOH Health Care Declarations Registry stopped accepting filings on July 1, 2011. Its statute, RCW 70.122.130, is still on the books until June 30, 2027.
- POLST registry: none. Registry bills, including HB 1724 in 2025, have not been enacted.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- A power of attorney signed in another state is valid in Washington if its signing met the law of the state named in it, or else the state where it was signed (RCW 11.125.060(3), 11.125.070). A photocopy or electronically sent copy has the same effect as the original (RCW 11.125.060(4)).
- A health care directive made in another jurisdiction is valid to the extent Washington law allows (RCW 70.122.030(3)).
- POLST: Washington EMTs will try to honor another state's form if it has the basic elements of the Washington form. An Oregon POLST without the patient's signature may be harder to honor (WSMA).

## Also in this state
- Mental health advance directive, RCW 71.32. It can name an agent for mental health decisions. Under RCW 11.125.400(2), if more than one agent is named for mental health decisions, the most recently appointed one controls.
