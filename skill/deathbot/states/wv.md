# West Virginia (WV)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Medical Power of Attorney" (MPOA), or the "Combined Medical Power of Attorney and Living Will," under the West Virginia Health Care Decisions Act, W. Va. Code § 16-30-1 et seq. Execution rules are in § 16-30-4. The WV Center for End-of-Life Care (WVCEOLC) distributes the state forms and runs the state registry: https://wvendoflife.org/meet-the-forms/advance-directives/
- **Signing:** in writing, dated, signed by the person (or by another person at their direction), signed in front of two adult witnesses, and the witnesses' signatures must be acknowledged before a notary. West Virginia needs both witnesses and a notary (§ 16-30-4(a)). A witness may not be the person who signed for the principal; a relative by blood or marriage; someone entitled to part of the estate under a will (a witness who did not know they were a beneficiary does not invalidate it); someone directly financially responsible for the person's medical care; the attending physician; or the named representative or successor (§ 16-30-4(b)).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** a treating health care provider; that provider's employee unless related; the operator of a health care facility serving the person; and that operator's employee unless related (§ 16-30-4(c)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** the attending physician or APRN selects a "health care surrogate" in writing (§ 16-30-8). They choose from: spouse; adult children; parents; adult siblings; adult grandchildren; close friends; then others the Department of Health designates by rule. Within a level, the clinician picks the best-qualified person and may pass over a higher-ranked person, giving reasons in the chart.

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Living Will," § 16-30-4. It can stand alone or be part of the Combined MPOA/Living Will, the form most West Virginians use. Same signing rules: two witnesses plus a notary.
- The attending physician must confirm and document a terminal condition before it is acted on (§ 16-30-19).
- EMS cannot honor CPR or DNR wishes in an advance directive. Those must be converted into a POST or DNR card.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "WV POST" (Portable Orders for Scope of Treatment), § 16-30-25. It is part of the National POLST program. The form is bright pink, and the current version took effect January 1, 2026; older versions remain valid. It is signed by a physician, APRN or PA, and by the patient or their guardian, MPOA representative or surrogate (§ 16-30-25(b)). Clinicians complete it. WVCEOLC distributes it: https://wvendoflife.org/meet-the-forms/wv-post/
- POST forms cannot be mailed to patients for completion; the clinician completes it with the person.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "Do Not Resuscitate" order on the orange DNR card, under the West Virginia Do Not Resuscitate Act, § 16-30C. An attending physician, PA or APRN issues it with the consent of the person, representative or surrogate (§ 16-30C-6). Clinicians must complete it with the patient; blank cards are not mailed to the public. The current version is dated 2020, and older versions are still valid.
- Providers must honor the card, DNR identification under § 16-30C-13, or a POST with a DNR order (§ 16-30C-7).

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: WV e-Directive Registry, run by WVCEOLC. It is free. Forms go in by fax or mail with an opt-in form. Clinicians view it through WVHIN, and EMS reaches it through MEDCOM.
- POLST registry: the same registry accepts POST forms.
- EMS DNR registry: the same registry accepts the orange DNR card.

## Documents from other states
- A living will, MPOA, mental health advance directive, POST, DNR card or similar form made in another state is valid if it was executed under West Virginia law or the law of the state where it was made (§ 16-30-21).

## Also in this state
- WV Mental Health Advance Directive (MHAD), distributed by WVCEOLC, for stating psychiatric treatment preferences ahead of a crisis.
- Adult status includes emancipated and "mature" minors (§ 16-30-3), who may make directives.
