# North Dakota (ND)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Health Care Directive", N.D.C.C. chapter 23-06.5. One document can name a health care agent (Part I), give health care instructions (Part II), make an anatomical gift (Part III) and be signed (Part IV). The statutory form at 23-06.5-17 is optional; any document that meets 23-06.5-05 is valid (23-06.5-16). The official text is in the Century Code chapter PDF (ndlegis.gov/cencode/t23c06-5.pdf). The form ends with a "Principal's Statement" that the person has read the attached written explanation of what appointing an agent means.
- **Signing:** written, dated, naming the person, and signed by them or by someone they authorize in their presence. The signature is verified by a notary or by at least two adult witnesses (23-06.5-05). The notary and every witness are bound by the same bars. None may be the agent or alternate, the spouse or an heir, a relative by blood, marriage or adoption, someone entitled to part of the estate by will, deed or law, a creditor of the estate, someone directly financially responsible for the person's medical care, or the attending physician. At least one witness must not be a health care or long-term care provider giving direct care, or an employee of one. A notary may be such an employee.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the person's health care provider or long-term care services provider, or a nonrelative employee of either (23-06.5-04).
- **Agent limits:** an agent cannot consent to admission to a mental health facility or state institution for more than 45 days without a court proceeding. Nor can an agent consent to psychosurgery, abortion or sterilization without court approval (23-06.5-03(6)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** N.D.C.C. 23-12-13, in order: health care agent (including one named under another state's law); guardian or custodian; then, each only if they have kept significant contact with the person: spouse; adult child; parent (including stepparent); adult sibling; grandparent; adult grandchild; close relative or friend. Last comes an interdisciplinary team of at least three health care professionals, one not directly involved in the person's care. The person must be certified incapacitated by the attending physician, psychiatrist or psychologist.

## Written instructions (living will)
- Health care instructions are Part II of the same Health Care Directive; same signing rules. A directive with only instructions and no agent is valid if Part II(B) is completed.
- **Pregnancy:** despite contrary instructions, care must continue for a pregnant person. The exception is when the attending physician and an examining obstetrician certify it will not allow live birth, or will be physically harmful or unreasonably painful, or will prolong severe pain medication cannot relieve (23-06.5-09(5)).
- **Tube feeding:** nutrition and hydration are given, withheld or withdrawn as the directive says. If it is silent, they may be withheld only if the physician finds the person cannot assimilate them or they would cause harm or unreasonable pain (23-06.5-09(6)).
- A directive that withholds care does not apply to emergency treatment in a prehospital situation (23-06.5-13(13)); EMS follow a POLST as a medical order (Honoring Choices ND).
- A copy of a directive has the same effect as the original (23-06.5-13(8)).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "North Dakota POLST" (Physician Order for Life Sustaining Treatment, as Honoring Choices ND spells it), coordinated by Honoring Choices ND (acpnd.org); North Dakota has no POLST statute. The 2016 ND POLST form (green trim) is completed and signed by a physician, NP or PA, and signed and dated by the patient or health care agent. EMS follow it as a medical order. Patients get it from their clinician; contact the Honoring Choices ND POLST coordinator for the form. The patient keeps the original, which travels with them between home, facility and hospital; copies go to the hospital and family (Honoring Choices ND FAQ). Not confirmed: whether EMS accept a photocopy or fax.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- North Dakota has no separate out-of-hospital DNR form; the ND POLST serves that role. Not confirmed: a state statute or EMS rule naming it.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: ND Health Care Directive Registry, run by NDHIN (the state health information exchange); authorized by 23-06.5-19. Active; residents upload their own directive.
- POLST registry: POLST forms are accepted in the same registry.
- EMS DNR registry: none; there is no separate form.

## Documents from other states
- A health care directive or similar document made in another state in compliance with that state's law stays enforceable (23-06.5-11). An agent named under another state's law ranks first under 23-12-13.
- Not confirmed: a statute or program statement on out-of-state POLST forms.
