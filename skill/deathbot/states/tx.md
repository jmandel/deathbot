# Texas (TX)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Medical Power of Attorney: Designation of Health Care Agent" (MPOA), Health and Safety Code Ch. 166, Subchapter D. The form in § 166.164 must be used "in substantially" that form. It takes effect when the person's physician certifies in writing that the person cannot make their own decisions. Free copy from Texas Health and Human Services (HHS): https://fhb.hhs.texas.gov/forms/advance-directives
- **Signing:** either two witnesses or a notary (§ 166.154). Both witnesses must be competent adults. At least one witness must not be any of these (§ 166.003): the named agent; a relative by blood or marriage; someone entitled to part of the estate by will or by law; the attending physician or their employee; a facility employee giving the person direct care, or an officer, director, partner or business-office employee of the facility; anyone with a claim against the estate. Digital or electronic signatures that meet § 166.011 are allowed. If the person cannot sign, another person may sign their name in their presence at their direction.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** while serving in that role, the person's health care provider or residential care provider, or an employee of either unless a relative (§ 166.153).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** for life-sustaining treatment decisions (§ 166.039): the attending physician together with the guardian or MPOA agent; if neither exists, one person in this order: spouse, reasonably available adult children, parents, nearest living relative. If no one is available, a second physician not involved in the person's care, or an ethics committee representative, must concur. For other treatment (§ 313.004, for patients in hospitals, nursing homes, home health or jails): spouse, adult children, parents, nearest living relative. These surrogates may not consent to voluntary inpatient mental health care or electroconvulsive therapy.

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Directive to Physicians and Family or Surrogates" (§§ 166.032–166.033). It is a separate form from the MPOA; HHS posts both. The same two-witness or notary rule applies (§ 166.032). The directive may also name a person to make decisions.
- **Pregnancy:** life-sustaining treatment may not be withheld or withdrawn from a pregnant patient under the directive provisions (§ 166.049).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- None. Texas has no state POLST program or statute.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "Out-of-Hospital Do-Not-Resuscitate (OOH-DNR) Order," on the standard state form (Health and Safety Code Ch. 166, Subchapter C; 25 Tex. Admin. Code § 157.25). Download it from HHS or from the DSHS EMS page. The person signs, and the attending physician must also sign. Witnesses or a notary follow the same rule as above (§ 166.082). An agent, guardian, proxy or qualified relative may sign for a person who cannot. Photocopies and complete facsimiles are valid (§ 166.083(d)). For a person who cannot sign and has no agent, guardian or proxy, the attending physician and a qualified relative in the § 166.039(b) order may sign, before two witnesses (§ 166.088). Not confirmed: whether EMS will act on a Directive to Physicians or MPOA in the field; the DSHS program page speaks only of the OOH-DNR form and ID device.
- **ID device (optional):** a plastic bracelet, or a metal bracelet or necklace inscribed "Texas Do Not Resuscitate - OOH" from a DSHS-approved maker (DSHS lists American Medical Identifications, MedicAlert Foundation, Caring Advocates, StickyJ Medical ID and Empower Hope), obtained only after the form is fully signed, at the person's expense. EMS must honor it as if the signed form were present (§ 166.090).

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none. The DSHS "Advance Directives Registry" lists providers willing to accept transfers; it does not hold documents.
- POLST registry: none (no program).
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- An advance directive validly made in another state has the same effect as a Texas one, but it cannot authorize anything Texas law forbids (§ 166.005).
- Out-of-state DNR or POLST forms: Chapter 166 has no provision for them; the OOH-DNR rules (§ 166.083) require the Texas standard form. Not confirmed: whether § 166.005 covers an out-of-state POLST.
