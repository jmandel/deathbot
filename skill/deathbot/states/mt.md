# Montana (MT)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** Montana has two ways to name someone.
  - **Durable health care power of attorney** (MCA 72-5-501 and 72-5-502).
    - It must be a written designation of an "attorney-in-fact or agent."
    - It must contain words such as "This health care power of attorney is not affected by subsequent disability or incapacity of the principal or lapse of time," or "This health care power of attorney becomes effective upon the disability or incapacity of the principal."
    - Montana's Uniform Power of Attorney Act does not cover health care powers (MCA 72-31-303(2)), and its statutory financial form cannot be used for health care.
    - The person may also nominate a guardian in it (72-5-501(2)).
  - **Designee in a living will Declaration** (MCA 50-9-103(1), (3)). The person may name an adult "to make decisions governing the withholding or withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment." An alternate may also be named. The statutory form in 50-9-103(3) does this.
- **Official copy:** the statutory forms are in the statute: https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0500/chapter_0090/part_0010/section_0030/0500-0090-0010-0030.html
- **Signing:**
  - A Declaration must be signed by the person (or by someone else at their direction) and witnessed by two individuals (50-9-103(1)). The statute lists no witness disqualifications and no notary requirement.
  - Not confirmed: no statutory witness or notary rule was found for a health care power of attorney under 72-5-501. Having two witnesses sign, as for a Declaration, also meets the Declaration rule.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the statute names no category.
- **Agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker in hospitals and skilled nursing facilities** (MCA 50-5-1311, enacted 2025). When a patient lacks decisional capacity, the decision-maker is chosen from these available people, in order:
  1. A guardian with medical authority.
  2. An agent under a medical durable power of attorney, or a designee in an advance directive.
  3. The spouse.
  4. An adult child.
  5. A parent.
  6. A sibling.
  7. A "trusted decisionmaker" (see below).
  8. A close friend with a deep understanding of the patient's wishes.
  9. Another proxy under Title 50, chapter 5, part 13.
- A hospital patient may name a "trusted decisionmaker" by telling their provider, with a third-party witness present. The provider then documents it and notifies the family. The designation lasts until the patient signs an advance directive or medical durable power of attorney, names someone else, or is discharged (50-5-1310).
- **Withholding life-sustaining treatment without a Declaration** (MCA 50-9-106). A terminally ill person's spouse, then adult children (by majority), then parents, then adult siblings (by majority), then nearest adult relative may consent. The consent must be in writing and witnessed by two individuals.

## Written instructions (living will)
- **Form:** the "Declaration", under the Montana Rights of the Terminally Ill Act (MCA 50-9-101 to 50-9-111). There are two optional statutory forms in 50-9-103: one directs the physician or APRN, and the other appoints a designee. Signing is as above.
- **When it applies:** the Declaration is operative once it is communicated to the attending physician or APRN, and that clinician finds the person terminal and unable to decide (50-9-105).
- **Pregnancy:** life-sustaining treatment cannot be withheld under a Declaration, or under family consent, from a woman known to be pregnant "so long as it is probable that the fetus will develop to the point of live birth" (50-9-202(3), 50-9-106(7)).
- **Comfort care:** providers must still give nutrition and hydration needed for comfort or pain relief (50-9-202(2)).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** Montana POLST (Provider Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment), overseen by the Board of Medical Examiners.
- **Who signs:** a Montana-licensed physician, APRN or PA (mandatory), and the patient, or the legal decision-maker (surrogate, medical power of attorney agent or guardian) if the patient cannot decide (mandatory; the form says it must be signed by both to be valid). Verbal orders are acceptable with a follow-up provider signature.
- **Getting the form:** from a provider, or download it at polst.mt.gov.
- **Copies:** the January 2024 form says photocopies and faxes of a signed POLST are legal and valid; the original, on "Terra" green paper, is strongly encouraged and stays with the patient. The board says the form must be transferred with the individual, and a facility may keep a duplicate. Earlier versions stay valid until revised or revoked.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Law:** MCA 50-10-101 to 50-10-106 (Do Not Resuscitate -- Notification). EMS must follow the DNR protocol when shown any of these (50-10-103):
  - DNR identification: a state-approved card, form, necklace or bracelet
  - an oral DNR order given directly by a physician or APRN
  - a written DNR order on the department's form
- **Who signs:** a DNR order is a directive from a licensed physician or APRN (50-10-101(7)). DNR identification signifies that the person is a qualified patient under the Rights of the Terminally Ill Act or that their physician or APRN has issued a DNR order (50-10-101(6)).
- **Comfort One:** the old Comfort One program is still honored by EMS. POLST is now the main form, and EMS follows a POLST.

## Registries
- **Advance directive:** the End-of-Life Registry run by the Department of Justice (https://app.mt.gov/registry/). It is active, free, and filed by mail on paper. The person chooses a privacy level.
- **POLST:** unclear whether the End-of-Life Registry accepts POLST forms.
- **EMS DNR:** none separate.

## Documents from other states
- A declaration executed in another state "in a manner substantially similar to 50-9-103" and in compliance with that state's law is effective in Montana (MCA 50-9-111).
- Not confirmed: no Montana statement on out-of-state health care powers of attorney or POLST forms was found.

## Also in this state
- **Mental health care advance directive:** separate law, MCA 53-21-1301 to 53-21-1335. An adult, or a minor aged 16 or older with capacity, may make one (53-21-1304). Not confirmed: the current witness rules for it.
- **Conscience objections:** a provider may decline on conscience grounds under 72-17-216, but must comply with the transfer duties (50-9-203, 50-10-103(2)).
