# Delaware (DE)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Advance Health-Care Directive", 16 Del. C. ch. 25. This is Delaware's Uniform Health-Care Decisions Act, effective September 30, 2025, which replaced the old law. The agent is named in a "power of attorney for health care" (§ 2508). The optional statutory form is § 2511, and using it is not required.
  - The Division of Services for Aging and Adults with Physical Disabilities posts the form at https://dhss.delaware.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2026/09/DE-Advanced-Health-Care-Directive.9.14.26.pdf.
  - Statute: https://delcode.delaware.gov/title16/c025/index.html.
  - Directives made before September 30, 2025 stay valid if they met this chapter or the law in force when signed (§ 2529).
- **Signing:** a power of attorney for health care must be in a record, signed by the person, and signed by one adult witness (§ 2508(d)). No notary is needed.
  - The witness may not be the agent, or the agent's spouse, domestic partner or cohabitant.
  - If the person lives in or receives care from a nursing home or long-term care facility, the witness may not be its owner, operator, employee or contractor.
  - Relatives are otherwise allowed.
  - The witness must be present physically, by real-time audio-video, or by real-time audio with identity verification (§ 2508(e)).
- **Who cannot be the agent** (for a person who lacks capacity, § 2508(b)):
  - an owner, operator, employee or contractor of a nursing home or long-term care facility where the person lives or receives care, unless a family member, cohabitant or their descendant;
  - a person a court finds dangerous to the individual;
  - a person subject to a pending protection-from-abuse petition or order, or to a no-contact order.
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** 16 Del. C. § 2512(b), in order:
  1. an adult the person named outside a power of attorney;
  2. the spouse or domestic partner, unless separated, divorcing or deserted;
  3. an adult child or a parent;
  4. a cohabitant;
  5. an adult sibling;
  6. an adult grandchild or grandparent;
  7. an adult who provided supported decision-making in the prior 6 months;
  8. an adult stepchild the person actively parented;
  9. an adult who has shown special care and concern and knows the person's values.
  - A clinician may require a declaration under penalty of perjury (§ 2512(c)).

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Health-care instruction", § 2507. It is Part B of the § 2511 form, which covers life-sustaining treatment, food and liquids, and pain relief. An instruction alone needs no witness and may even be oral (§ 2502(14)). The clinician records it in the chart.
- **Tube feeding:** § 2519. For a person with a long-term disability who routinely uses artificial nutrition, hydration or ventilation, an agent or surrogate generally may not stop it. Exceptions: the person expressly authorized stopping it, or there has been a major, unrecoverable loss of function.
- There is no pregnancy clause in the chapter.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** DMOST, "Delaware Medical Orders for Scope of Treatment" (16 Del. C. ch. 25A; 16 Del. Admin. Code 4304). SB 274 (85 Del. Laws c. 485, signed and effective Sept 8, 2026) renames it "Delaware Portable Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment (POLST)". Existing DMOST forms stay valid. The Health Care Commission page (https://dhss.delaware.gov/dhcc/dmost/) still uses the DMOST name and links the form at https://dhss.delaware.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/DMOSTforms.pdf.
- **Who signs:** the patient and a physician (MD/DO), APRN or PA. If the patient lacks capacity, an authorized representative signs, in this order: a guardian with the authority, the most recently appointed agent, then a default surrogate (Reg. 4304-4.9.2). Under SB 274, a representative may sign only after the practitioner documents the patient's lack of capacity in the medical record, and any authorized practitioner (not only a physician) may make that finding.
- Not confirmed: the text of 16 Del. C. ch. 25A itself; on the check date the delcode page showed only the chapter heading and the 85 Del. Laws c. 485 note. Signing and copy rules here come from the regulation.
- **Copies:** a copy of a signed DMOST "is legal and valid to the same extent as the original." Only the watermarked form in the regulations is recognized (Reg. 4304-4.5).
- It is for a patient with serious illness or frailty whose practitioner "would not be surprised if the patient died within the next year" (Reg. 4304-4.2).

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- DMOST serves as Delaware's out-of-hospital DNR order (EMS regulation, 19 DE Reg 922, 2016). EMS honors a DMOST that declines CPR.
- Not confirmed: any separate state DNR bracelet.

## Registries
- **Advance directive registry:** none. MyDirectives made a vendor proposal in June 2025, but no registry exists.
- **POLST registry:** the DMOST Registry, run by DHIN (the state health information exchange), is active. Enrolled organizations submit forms.
- **EMS DNR registry:** DMOST serves as the out-of-hospital DNR, and it is in the DHIN registry.

## Documents from other states
- § 2516(a): an out-of-state directive is valid if it complies with the law of the state it names, or the state where it was made, or this chapter.
- Not confirmed: whether Delaware EMS honors out-of-state POLST forms in place of DMOST.

## Also in this state
- **Advance mental health-care directive:** § 2509. A person can make a directive that covers mental health care only.
  - It may say the directive cannot be revoked during a specified psychiatric episode. If so, it must be a separate record, signed by the person and at least two adult witnesses who are physically present.
  - The § 2511 form also has optional special powers: voluntary mental health admission (up to 72 hours) and nursing home placement over 100 days.
- Part D of the § 2511 form covers organ donation.
