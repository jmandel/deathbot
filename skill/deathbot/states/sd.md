# South Dakota (SD)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Durable Power of Attorney for Health Care", SDCL 59-7-2.1 to 59-7-2.8. South Dakota has no statutory form for it; any document meeting 59-7-2.1 works. The South Dakota Association of Healthcare Organizations (SDAHO) links an SD Advance Directives brochure from its MOST page.
- **Signing:** signed by the person, or by another adult at their direction and in their presence, and either witnessed by two adults or notarized (SDCL 59-7-2.1). Not confirmed: any statutory list of people who may not witness; none was found in 59-7.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the statute lets the person name "any other person". No exclusions were found.
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** SDCL 34-12C-3, when no agent or guardian is available: spouse (if not legally separated); adult child; parent; adult sibling; grandparent or adult grandchild; adult aunt, uncle, cousin, niece or nephew; close friend.
- **Tube feeding:** an agent may never withhold comfort care. The agent may withhold artificial nutrition or hydration only if it is not needed for comfort and death is near, the body cannot absorb it, its burden outweighs its benefit, there is clear and convincing evidence of the person's wishes, or the document says so or expressly authorizes it (SDCL 59-7-2.7).
- **Pregnancy:** whatever the agent decides, life-sustaining treatment and artificial nutrition and hydration are given to a pregnant woman unless the attending physician and one other examining physician certify that they will not allow live birth, will physically harm her, or will prolong severe pain that medication cannot relieve (SDCL 59-7-2.8).

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Living Will Declaration", SDCL 34-12D; the suggested form in 34-12D-3 is entirely optional. Separate from the power of attorney. Signed by the person (or another at their direction) and attested by two adult witnesses, or notarized (34-12D-2). The suggested form has blocks for both.
- It must state the person's preference on artificial nutrition and hydration; if it does not, the law that applies without a declaration governs those decisions (34-12D-2). The form has separate initialed choices for life-sustaining treatment and for tube or IV food and water, both tied to imminent death or permanent unconsciousness.
- **Pregnancy:** life-sustaining treatment, nutrition and hydration continue for a pregnant woman unless the attending physician and one other examining physician record that it will not allow live birth, will physically harm her, or will prolong severe pain that medication cannot relieve (34-12D-10).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "Medical Orders for Scope of Treatment" (SD MOST), SDCL 34-12H (Senate Bill 118, signed March 27, 2019). Only for patients with a terminal condition. Signed and dated by the patient or authorized representative (the representative only when the patient lacks capacity) and by the medical provider, meaning a physician, physician assistant or certified nurse practitioner (34-12H-1, 34-12H-4). The Department of Health sets the standard form; SDAHO posts it in English and Spanish. A MOST overrides conflicting instructions in a power of attorney or living will. Not confirmed: whether copies are valid.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "Comfort One", the EMS cardiopulmonary resuscitation directive, SDCL 34-12F; EMS rules ARSD 44:05:06. Signed by or for the person and by a physician, physician assistant or certified nurse practitioner. Clinicians request forms from the Department of Health (605-773-3361); completed forms go to the Department in Pierre. An optional bracelet ($45.50) can stand in for the form; it needs a fully completed, signed form, and its number is recorded in the state Comfort One database.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none.
- POLST registry: none.
- EMS DNR registry: Comfort One database kept by the SD Department of Health. Enrollment is through a signed Comfort One form; the bracelet costs $45.50.

## Documents from other states
- A MOST-type document executed in another state that meets that state's requirements is valid in South Dakota (34-12H-6). Not confirmed: a South Dakota section recognizing out-of-state living wills or health care powers of attorney; none appears in 34-12D's section list.
