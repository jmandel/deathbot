# Arkansas (AR)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** Arkansas Healthcare Decisions Act, Ark. Code §§ 20-6-101 to -118, with execution rules in § 20-6-103. The person names a "health care agent". There is no required statutory form: a person may use these forms "or any form of their own choosing." The Arkansas Department of Health (ADH) posts two:
  - "Appointment of Health Care Agent": https://healthy.arkansas.gov/wp-content/uploads/AppointmentHealthCareAgentForm.pdf
  - "Advance Care Plan", which combines an agent with treatment instructions: https://healthy.arkansas.gov/wp-content/uploads/AdvanceCarePlanForm.pdf
  - Both are linked from https://healthy.arkansas.gov/programs-services/certificates-records/polst/, and Spanish versions are there too.
  - A durable power of attorney for health care under Ark. Code § 20-13-104 is an older alternative.
- **Signing:** § 20-6-103. An adult, married minor or emancipated minor may sign. The directive is either notarized or signed by two witnesses.
  - Each witness must be a competent adult who is not the agent.
  - At least one witness may not be related to the person by blood, marriage or adoption, and may not be entitled to any part of the estate under an existing will or codicil or by operation of law.
  - A witnessed directive must include an attestation clause saying the witnesses meet these rules (§ 20-6-103(c)(3)).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** § 20-6-103 sets no restriction beyond the witness rules. For default surrogates, the treating provider, the provider's employees, and the operator or employees of the health care institution are ineligible unless related by blood, marriage or adoption, and so is anyone under an order to avoid contact with the patient (§ 20-6-105(c)(2)(B), (d)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** Ark. Code § 20-6-105. A surrogate must be an adult who has shown special care and concern, knows the patient's values, and is reasonably available and willing. The provider may consider, in descending order of preference: the spouse (unless legally separated), an adult child, a parent, an adult sibling, any other adult relative, then any other qualifying adult (§ 20-6-105(c)(4)). ADH has an "Acceptance of Surrogate" form at https://healthy.arkansas.gov/wp-content/uploads/AcceptanceofSurrogateForm.pdf. If no surrogate is available, the supervising provider decides after consulting an ethics committee or ethics officers, or with the concurrence of a second physician not involved in the patient's care (§ 20-6-105(c)(5)).

## Written instructions (living will)
- The ADH "Advance Care Plan" form carries treatment instructions under the Healthcare Decisions Act. It includes a separate yes/no choice for "Tube feeding/IV fluids" and an optional organ donation line. It uses the same signing rules as above.
- **Declaration:** the separate living will "declaration" under the Arkansas Rights of the Terminally Ill or Permanently Unconscious Act is Ark. Code § 20-17-202. A declaration is valid if two witnesses sign, or if it is notarized without witnesses, or if it meets the Healthcare Decisions Act.
- **Pregnancy:** Ark. Code § 20-17-206(c). A declaration has no effect on a known-pregnant patient while it is possible the fetus could develop to live birth with continued life-sustaining treatment.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** AR POLST, "Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment". It is created by the Arkansas Physician Order for Life-Sustaining Treatment Act, Ark. Code § 20-6-301 et seq. (Act 504 of 2017). The State Board of Health prescribes the form (§ 20-6-304).
- **Who signs:** both a physician and the patient or legal representative must sign; "Both signatures are required." The current ADH form has only a physician signature line, and an AANP fact sheet (October 2025) says nurse practitioners may not sign it. Not confirmed: whether Act 431 of 2025 (HB1167, which changed APRN and PA signature authority in § 17-80-120) reaches the POLST form.
- **Where to get it:** the public can download it from ADH, at https://healthy.arkansas.gov/wp-content/uploads/POLST_Form_and_Directions.pdf (Spanish version on the same page).
- **Copies:** the original is encouraged, but "photocopies and faxes are legal and valid under Arkansas law."

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Form:** "Emergency Medical Services Do Not Resuscitate Order" (EMS/DNR), Ark. Code § 20-13-901 et seq. (Act 1101 of 1993) and ADH EMS/DNR rules. Only the patient's attending physician may issue it. The form has a signature line for the patient, health care proxy or legal guardian, and the attending physician's order.
- Any form approved by the Board, or a physician's form that meets the rules, works. It must say "DNR" or "No Code" and carry the physician's signature and date. Physicians get forms through local health units, hospitals and ambulance services.
- A POLST marked "Do Not Attempt Resuscitation/DNR" is also a portable medical order.
- The EMS/DNR rules do not provide for a bracelet or wallet card. Copies of the form may be given to other providers for information.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none.
- POLST registry: none. Arkansas POLST comes from Act 504 of 2017.
- EMS DNR registry: none. The attending physician alone signs the EMS DNR.

## Documents from other states
- Ark. Code § 20-6-103(h): an advance directive executed outside Arkansas is given effect if it complies with Arkansas law or the law of the state where it was signed.
- Not confirmed: the text of § 20-17-212 (out-of-state living will declarations); secondary summaries describe the same rule.
- Not confirmed: any Arkansas rule on out-of-state POLST forms.
