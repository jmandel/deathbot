# Utah (UT)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Advance Health Care Directive," under Utah's Uniform Health Care Decisions Act, Utah Code Title 75A, Chapter 9 (S.B. 134, 2025, in effect since January 1, 2026). The act replaced the old Title 75A Chapter 3 rules. Using a form is optional. The optional statutory form is in § 75A-9-110. A copy of the state-approved form (version 1.7) is posted by the Utah Commission on Aging: https://ucoa.utah.edu/coreissues/directives/
- **Signing:** the person signs. A part that names an agent (a "power of attorney for health care") also needs one adult witness (§ 75A-9-107(4)). The witness may not be the agent, the agent's spouse or partner, or, for someone living in or getting care at a nursing home or assisted living facility, its owner, operator, employee or contractor. The witness must believe the signing is voluntary. They may be present in person, by live audio-video, or by phone if they know the person or can verify who they are. No notary is needed. Electronic directives and signatures are valid (§ 75A-9-115(3)). A form that only gives instructions needs no witness.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** someone a court finds is a danger to the person, and an owner, operator, employee or contractor of the person's nursing home or assisted living facility unless they are family, the person's partner, or the partner's descendant (§ 75A-9-107(2)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** § 75A-9-111, in this order: an adult the person identified (other than in a power of attorney) to decide for them; spouse (not if divorce or separation is filed or decreed, or after desertion of more than a year); adult child or parent; partner the person lives with; adult sibling; adult grandchild or grandparent; an adult who routinely helped with supported decision-making in the past six months; an adult stepchild the person raised; an adult who has shown special care and knows the person's values; then a designated physician. A clinician may require a sworn declaration on a state form. A person may disqualify anyone from serving, even after losing capacity (§ 75A-9-113).

## Written instructions (living will)
- Health care instructions (§ 75A-9-106) are part of the same Advance Health Care Directive form (Part B, "Health Care Instructions"). An instruction may be in the same record as the power of attorney.
- The old Utah pregnancy rule (§ 75A-3-306) was repealed with S.B. 134. The new chapter has no pregnancy clause.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "Order for Life-Sustaining Treatment" (OLST, formerly Utah POLST), Utah Code § 26B-2-802 and Utah Admin. Code R432-31. It is prepared with a physician, APRN or PA, or under their supervision by a nurse, PA, mental health professional or other designated provider. The physician, APRN or PA must sign it. The patient (or an adult the patient directs) also signs; for an adult without capacity, the highest-priority default surrogate under § 75A-9-111 (or a majority of that class) signs; for a minor, a parent or guardian signs (§ 26B-2-802(3)). An order to withhold or withdraw treatment from a minor also needs two physicians' certification (§ 26B-2-802(4)). A surrogate's or parent's signature may be replaced by a verbal confirmation witnessed and signed by a second licensed provider when signing in person or electronically would be very hard (§ 26B-2-802(11)). It must be on the form designated by the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS). Clinicians complete it. The 2026 form is posted at ucoa.utah.edu.
- The form says photocopies and faxes of a signed OLST are valid. The Uniform Electronic Transactions Act applies to its signatures. If the OLST conflicts with the advance directive, the OLST controls (§ 26B-2-802(7)).

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- No separate EMS DNR form. The OLST's "Do NOT attempt resuscitation" box is the out-of-hospital DNR, and EMS providers are covered when they follow it (§ 26B-2-802(6)). The statute mentions a bracelet only as "other evidence" of an OLST that can be destroyed to revoke it (§ 26B-2-802(8)). Not confirmed: a state-approved bracelet vendor.

## Documents from other states
- A directive made outside Utah is valid if it meets the law of the state it names or where it was made, or meets Utah's chapter (§ 75A-9-115(1)). Directives made before 2026 stay valid if they met the law at the time (§ 75A-9-128).
- Not confirmed: a Utah statute or DHHS statement on honoring out-of-state POLST forms.

## Also in this state
- Advance mental health care directive (§ 75A-9-108): it may cover only mental health care. It can name an agent just for mental health decisions and state preferences on medications, facilities, admission and crisis care.
- The statutory form has an organ donation part (Part D).
