# Wisconsin (WI)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Power of Attorney for Health Care" (POA-HC), Wis. Stat. ch. 155. The statutory form is in § 155.30. The Department of Health Services (DHS) publishes it as form F-00085 ("Power of Attorney for Health Care – Letter and Form"), with large-print and translated versions. Free: https://www.dhs.wisconsin.gov/forms/advdirectives/adformspoa.htm
- **Signing:** in writing, dated, and signed by the person in front of two adult witnesses (§ 155.10(1)). No notary option. A witness may not be: related by blood, marriage, adoption or domestic partnership; someone who knows they are entitled to or have a claim on the estate; someone directly financially responsible for the person's health care; a health care provider serving the person, that provider's employee, or an employee of an inpatient facility where the person is a patient (chaplains and social workers are exempt); or the named agent (§ 155.10(2)). Remote witnessing by live audio-video is allowed only under an attorney's supervision with an affidavit of compliance (§ 155.10(3)).
- **When it takes effect:** only after 2 physicians, or a physician and an advanced practice clinician (psychologist, nurse practitioner, or qualified PA), personally examine the person and sign a statement of incapacity. Neither may be a relative or an heir (§ 155.05(2)).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the person's health care provider, an employee of that provider, an employee of a facility where the person is a patient or resident, or the spouse of any of these, unless a relative (§ 155.05(3)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** Wisconsin has no general family-consent law. Without a POA-HC, a court guardianship is usually needed. One narrow exception, 2025 Wisconsin Act 115 (§ 50.06(8), from June 1, 2026): for a hospital inpatient found incapacitated, a "patient's representative" may consent to admission straight to a nursing home or community-based residential facility. The order is spouse or domestic partner, adult child, parent, adult sibling, grandparent, adult grandchild, adult close friend (§ 50.06(3)). It uses DHS forms F-03435 and F-03436.

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Declaration to Health Care Professionals (Wisconsin Living Will)," § 154.03, DHS form F-00060. It is separate from the POA-HC. Two adult witnesses, with the same kinds of exclusions as above (§ 154.03(1)). It covers terminal condition and persistent vegetative state only.
- **Pregnancy:** the statutory form says it has no effect during a known pregnancy (§ 154.03(2)).
- **Tube feeding:** the declaration cannot refuse food or fluids taken by mouth unless medically contraindicated. An agent may refuse a feeding tube only if the POA-HC specifically authorizes it, and not if the attending health care professional advises that withholding it would cause pain or reduce comfort (§ 155.20(4)). An agent may make decisions during a known pregnancy only to the extent the POA-HC authorizes (§ 155.20(6)).
- If the two documents conflict, the POA-HC controls (§ 155.20(5)).
- **Agent limits:** an agent may not admit the person to a mental health facility, intermediate care facility for persons with intellectual disability, or state treatment facility. Admission to a nursing home or community-based residential facility is allowed only under the conditions in § 155.20(2)(c), for example a stay of up to 3 months for recuperative care straight from a hospital. An agent may not consent to electroconvulsive treatment, psychosurgery or experimental mental health research (§ 155.20(3)).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- None statewide. Wisconsin has no state POLST program or statute. Not confirmed: whether some health systems use POLST forms locally.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "Emergency Care DNR Order," DHS form F-44763, Wis. Stat. §§ 154.17–154.29. It is issued only by the attending health care professional (physician, PA or APRN) for a "qualified patient" age 18 or older. The patient must request it and sign it (§ 154.19(1)). § 154.225 sets out when a guardian or health care agent may request and sign it for a patient.
- **Bracelet:** the order works through a standardized DNR bracelet, either a plastic one put on by the provider or a metal one from DHS-approved vendor StickyJ. EMS does not follow the order if the bracelet looks tampered with or removed, or if they know the patient is pregnant (§ 154.19(3)). Older MedicAlert bracelets are still recognized.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none statewide. A person or their agent may file a POA-HC, for a fee, with the Register in Probate of their county for safekeeping (§ 155.65). The same county filing is available for a Declaration to Health Care Professionals (§ 154.13); research/state-registries.md mentions only the POA-HC.
- POLST registry: none.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- A health care agent document validly made in another state is enforceable to the extent it grants powers a Wisconsin agent may have (§ 155.70(10)).
- A living will from another state is valid to the extent it is consistent with Wisconsin law (§ 154.11(9)).
- Not confirmed: whether Wisconsin EMS honors out-of-state DNR or POLST forms without a Wisconsin bracelet.

## Also in this state
- DHS also publishes an "Authorization for Final Disposition" (F-00086), which names who decides about a person's remains.
