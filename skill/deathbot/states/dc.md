# District of Columbia (DC)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Durable Power of Attorney for Health Care", D.C. Code § 21-2205. The sample form is § 21-2207, "offered as a sample form only". Any written form that meets § 21-2205 may be used. Official text: https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/21-2207.
- **Signing:** dated and signed by the person and by two adult witnesses. The witnesses affirm that the person was of sound mind and free from duress. No notary is required.
  - Neither witness may be the agent, the person's health care provider, or an employee of that provider.
  - At least one witness may not be related by blood, marriage or adoption, and may not be entitled to any part of the estate by a current will or by law (§ 21-2205(c)-(d)).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** § 21-2205 lists no disqualification for the attorney in fact.
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required. The sample form has no acceptance block.
- **Default decision-maker:** D.C. Code § 21-2210. It applies when there is no health care power of attorney and the patient's incapacity is certified under § 21-2204. In order:
  1. a court-appointed guardian or conservator, within the scope of the appointment;
  2. a court-appointed intellectual disability advocate;
  3. the spouse or domestic partner;
  4. an adult child;
  5. a parent;
  6. an adult sibling;
  7. a religious superior, if the patient is in a religious order or is a diocesan priest;
  8. a close friend;
  9. the nearest living relative.
  - For everyone from the spouse or domestic partner down (statute paragraphs (a)(2) through (6)), at least one witness must be present when consent is given, refused or withdrawn (§ 21-2210(c)).
  - The order is a rebuttable presumption: a person on the list can challenge a higher-ranked person's decision in Superior Court (§ 21-2210(e)-(f)).
  - A close friend may not act if they are the treating provider, or an owner, operator, administrator or employee of that provider (§ 21-2210(g)).

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Declaration", the Natural Death Act, D.C. Code § 7-622. The model form is in the same section, and it applies only to a terminal condition certified by two physicians, one of them the attending physician. It is separate from the durable power of attorney.
- **Signing:** two or more witnesses, each at least 18. A witness may not be any of the following:
  - the person who signed for the declarant;
  - related by blood, marriage or domestic partnership;
  - entitled to the estate by will or intestacy;
  - directly financially responsible for the person's medical care;
  - the attending physician, the physician's employee, or an employee of the facility where the person is a patient.
- The declarant must tell the attending physician, who puts it in the medical record.
- § 7-622 has no pregnancy clause.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** DC MOST, "Medical Orders for Scope of Treatment", D.C. Code § 21-2221.01 et seq., run by the DC Health EMS Division. The fillable form is linked from https://dchealth.dc.gov/most ("MOST Fillable Form V2 08182021").
- **Who signs:** an "authorized health care professional", defined as a licensed physician or advanced practice nurse responsible for the patient's care (§ 21-2221.01(4)). PAs are not included. The professional completes the form, and the patient (18 or older) or authorized representative signs it (§ 21-2221.05).
- **Electronic:** an electronic MOST (eMOST) is created and viewed through the CRISP DC health information exchange via MyDirectives for Clinicians (§ 21-2221.14a). Paper forms can be scanned and uploaded at MyDirectives.com.
- **Review:** at least once a year, after a significant change in the patient's condition, and within 72 hours after a transfer.
- Any incomplete section of the form means full treatment for that section.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- The District's Comfort Care Order law was repealed. The DC MOST form is the current out-of-hospital order on CPR. DC Fire and EMS can view advance care plans through the health information exchange.
- Not confirmed: any current DC DNR bracelet.

## Registries
- **Advance directive registry:** CRISP DC advance care planning, through MyDirectives, is active. Resident accounts are free. DC Fire and EMS and hospitals can view it.
- **POLST registry:** DC MOST, via eMOST through CRISP DC, is active.
- **EMS DNR registry:** none. The Comfort Care Order law was repealed, and MOST is the current order.

## Documents from other states
- MOST: "EMS personnel and other health care professionals shall recognize a MOST Form or similar instrument executed in another state as if the instrument were executed in accordance with the laws of that state" (§ 21-2221.09).
- Not confirmed: a D.C. Code provision on out-of-state health care powers of attorney or declarations. § 21-2212 and § 7-622 do not address it.
