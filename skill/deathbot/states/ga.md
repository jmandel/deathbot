# Georgia (GA)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Georgia Advance Directive for Health Care", O.C.G.A. § 31-32-4 (Georgia Advance Directive for Health Care Act, ch. 31-32). It is one statutory form in four parts. Part One names a health care agent and a backup, Part Two gives treatment preferences (it replaced the old living will), Part Three nominates a guardian, and Part Four holds the signatures and must be completed for any other part to take effect. The form is optional, and other forms may be used. The Division of Aging Services posts it at https://aging.georgia.gov/document/document/georgia-advance-directive-health-care-07pdf/download.
- **Signing:** the person must be 18 or older, or an emancipated minor. Someone else may sign for the person in the person's presence and at their express direction. Two witnesses sign. No notary is needed. Both witnesses must be of sound mind and at least 18. They do not have to be together or watch the person sign; each witness attests that the person signed in their presence or acknowledged signing to them (§ 31-32-5; form Part Four). A copy of a validly signed form has the same effect as the original.
- **Who cannot witness:** the health care agent or backup agent; anyone who knowingly inherits from the person or otherwise gains financially from the death; anyone directly involved in the person's health care. Only one witness may be an employee, agent or medical staff member of the hospital, skilled nursing facility, hospice or other facility where the person is receiving care.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** a physician or health care provider directly involved in the person's care.
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required. An agent has no duty to act. An agent may not consent to psychosurgery, sterilization, or involuntary hospitalization or treatment under Title 37.
- **Default decision-maker:** O.C.G.A. § 31-9-2(a) sets this order: the health care agent; the spouse; a guardian; then, if none of those is available, an adult child, a parent, an adult sibling, a grandparent, an adult grandchild, or an adult niece, nephew, aunt or uncle related in the first degree. Last comes an "adult friend" who has shown special care, knows the person's views, and signs the facility's acknowledgment form. A physician must examine the person and record that the person cannot consent (§ 31-9-2(c)). Not confirmed: whether 2025 amendments (S.B. 153) changed this order. The text checked here may be from before 2025.

## Written instructions (living will)
- Part Two of the same form (§ 31-32-4), signed the same way. It covers a terminal condition and a state of permanent unconsciousness. It includes choices about nourishment and hydration, which the law treats separately from other life-sustaining procedures.
- **Pregnancy:** Part Two generally has no effect if the person is pregnant, unless the fetus is not viable and the person initialed the pregnancy line asking for Part Two to be carried out.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** Georgia POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment), O.C.G.A. § 31-1-14. The Department of Public Health (DPH) publishes the form in English and Spanish at https://dph.georgia.gov/POLST, and the public can download it.
- **Who signs:** the patient with capacity and the attending physician. An authorized person may not sign for a patient who has capacity. If the patient lacks capacity, an authorized person signs, in this order: the health care agent, spouse, court-appointed guardian, adult child, parent, adult sibling. If someone other than the health care agent signs a "do not attempt resuscitation" order, a concurring physician must also sign. The current DPH form has signature lines only for physicians, not APRNs or PAs.
- **Copies:** the form states that copies of the original POLST are valid. It may be written when the physician expects death within 365 days, or at any time after a diagnosis of dementia or another progressive degenerative brain disease.
- Anyone who follows a POLST in good faith is protected (§ 31-1-14(d)).

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Form:** "Do Not Resuscitate Order", O.C.G.A. ch. 31-39. The attending physician signs it on a form substantially like the one in the statute. The form shows the patient's name, the physician's printed name and phone number, and the date. The patient, the health care agent, or a listed family member (spouse, guardian, adult child, parent, adult sibling) consents. When a family member rather than the patient or agent consents, the patient must be a "candidate for nonresuscitation" and a second physician must concur. A POLST marked "do not resuscitate" signed by the health care agent counts as consent, with no concurring physician needed.
- **Bracelet:** optional. A person at home may wear a bracelet on the wrist or ankle, or a necklace, saying "DO NOT RESUSCITATE ORDER". It must show the patient, the authorized person and the physician, with phone numbers, and the date of the order. A person with an order at home must also post a prominent notice in the home.
- EMS technicians and caregivers may carry out the order.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none found.
- POLST registry: none.
- EMS DNR registry: none. The bracelet and a notice posted in the home stand in for one.

## Documents from other states
- O.C.G.A. § 31-32-5(b): a document executed in another state that is valid there is treated as a Georgia advance directive for health care. § 31-1-14 also covers a POLST from another state that is valid there and substantially similar to Georgia's form.
