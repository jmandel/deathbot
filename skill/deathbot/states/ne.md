# Nebraska (NE)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Power of Attorney for Health Care", Neb. Rev. Stat. 30-3401 to 30-3432. The statute gives a form that may be used (30-3408(1)); it is not required, and the power may sit inside another document if it meets 30-3404 (30-3408(2)). The official text of the form is on the Legislature's page for 30-3408. The form has optional lines for instructions on life-sustaining treatment and on artificially administered nutrition and hydration.
- **Signing:** in writing and dated, then either signed before two adult witnesses (who see the signing or the person's acknowledgment of it) or acknowledged before a notary (30-3404). The notary may not be the agent or successor agent. A witness may not be the person's spouse, parent, child, grandchild, sibling, presumptive heir, known devisee, attending physician, mental health treatment team member, romantic or dating partner, the agent, or an employee of the person's life or health insurer. No more than one witness may be an administrator or employee of a health care provider caring for the person (30-3405).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the attending physician or a member of the mental health treatment team; an unrelated employee of either; an unrelated owner, operator or employee of a health care provider where the person is a patient or resident; an unrelated person already serving as agent for ten or more people (30-3406).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required; the statutory form has no acceptance block.
- **Default decision-maker:** Health Care Surrogacy Act, 30-601 to 30-619. If there is no agent or guardian and the person has not designated a surrogate, the order is: spouse (not if legally separated or a divorce, annulment or separation is pending); adult child; parent; adult sibling (30-604(2)). An adult who has shown special care and concern and knows the person's values may also act (30-604(3)). The primary health care provider must first find the person incapable. Same-class members who cannot agree are disqualified, and the provider may go to the next class. A surrogate's authority ends on transfer to another facility (30-604). A person can disqualify anyone, including family, in a signed and dated writing or by telling the primary health care provider in front of a witness (30-604(7)).

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Declaration" under the Rights of the Terminally Ill Act, Neb. Rev. Stat. 20-401 to 20-416. Separate document; the form in 20-404 is optional. Signed by the person (or another at their direction) before two adult witnesses or a notary. No more than one witness may be an administrator or employee of a treating health care provider, and a witness may not be an employee of the person's life or health insurer; these limits do not apply when a notary is used (20-404(1)). The form covers an "incurable and irreversible condition" or a "persistent vegetative state".
- **Pregnancy:** treatment is not withheld or withdrawn under a declaration while it is probable the fetus will develop to live birth (20-408(3)).
- **Comfort care:** the Act does not remove the duty to give comfort care and pain relief, including nutrition and hydration for comfort (20-408(2)).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- Nebraska has no POLST statute. polst.org lists a state program, "POLST - Provider Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment", run through the Office of Healthcare Ethics at Nebraska Medicine and the Nebraska Hospice and Palliative Care Association (nehospice.org, 402-552-3647). Not confirmed: the current form name and signer rules. Some Nebraska hospitals refer to a "Nebraska Emergency Treatment Order" (NETO) signed by a physician. A person gets any such form from their clinician.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- None statewide. Nebraska has no out-of-hospital DNR statute, form, bracelet or EMS DNR program; EMS follows local medical-director protocols. Not confirmed: how individual EMS services treat a Nebraska POLST.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none (a 2019 registry bill, LB365, was not enacted).
- POLST registry: none; an ePOLST registry was only proposed.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- A health care power of attorney made in another state and valid there is valid in Nebraska according to its terms (30-3408(4)).
- A living will made in another state is valid if it complies with that state's law or Nebraska's (20-414).
- POLST-type orders: no statute.

## Also in this state
- Advance Mental Health Care Directives Act, Neb. Rev. Stat. 30-4401 et seq. (2020): a separate advance mental health care directive, signed before two disinterested adults or a notary who is not the named agent. The DHHS template is at the FAQ link below. Where it conflicts with a health care power of attorney on mental health care, the mental health directive controls.
- LB66 (2025), which would have adopted the Uniform Health-Care Decisions Act, was indefinitely postponed on April 17, 2026. The laws above remain in force.
