# Florida (FL)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Designation of Health Care Surrogate", Fla. Stat. § 765.202. The suggested form is in § 765.203, and the statute says the designation "may, but need not, be in the following form." The Agency for Health Care Administration (AHCA) posts the form, a living will, a "Designation of Health Care Surrogate for a Minor", a Uniform Donor Form and a wallet card at https://quality.healthfinder.fl.gov/report-guides/advance-directives.
- **Signing:** the person signs in front of two adult witnesses. No notary is needed. The named surrogate may not be a witness. At least one witness must be neither the person's spouse nor a blood relative (§ 765.202(1)-(2)).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the statute names no excluded group.
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **When it takes effect:** the person can say in the document that the surrogate's authority starts right away, while the person still has capacity (§ 765.202). Otherwise it starts when the person is found incapacitated.
- **Mental health:** the person may name a separate surrogate for mental health decisions. Unless the document says otherwise, the health care surrogate is presumed to cover mental health decisions too (§ 765.202(5)).
- **Default decision-maker:** § 765.401(1) sets the proxy order. Each class acts only if no one in an earlier class is reasonably available, willing or competent: a court-appointed guardian or guardian advocate with authority; the spouse; an adult child, or a majority of the reasonably available adult children; a parent; an adult sibling, or a majority of the siblings; an adult relative who has shown special care and kept regular contact; a close friend (an adult who gives the facility or physician an affidavit of the friendship and regular contact, § 765.101); then a clinical social worker chosen by the provider's bioethics committee.

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Living Will", Fla. Stat. §§ 765.302-765.303. The suggested form in § 765.303 is optional. It is separate from the surrogate form, but it has an optional line to name a surrogate. It covers a terminal condition, an end-stage condition and a persistent vegetative state.
- **Signing:** the same rule applies: two witnesses, and at least one is not a spouse or blood relative (§ 765.302(1)).
- **Limits:** a surrogate or proxy may not consent to abortion, sterilization, electroshock therapy, psychosurgery, some experimental treatments, or voluntary admission to a mental health facility. A surrogate also may not consent to withholding life-prolonging procedures from a pregnant patient before viability. Each of these is allowed only if the person expressly gave that authority in writing, or a court approves (§ 765.113).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "POLST Florida" is run by the Florida Medical Directors Association (https://www.fmda.org/polstfl). It is voluntary and is not in statute. Its page describes the form as signed by the provider and the patient or decision-maker. The form posted there is dated July 10, 2015.
- Not confirmed: which clinician types may sign it in Florida, and whether copies are valid. The FMDA page does not say. Florida EMS law (§ 401.45) refers only to the DNRO below, so a person who wants EMS to withhold CPR at home should also have the yellow DNRO.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Form:** "Do Not Resuscitate Order" (DNRO), form DH 1896, under Fla. Stat. § 401.45 and Fla. Admin. Code r. 64J-2.018. The patient or the patient's representative signs, and so does a Florida-licensed physician, osteopathic physician, advanced practice registered nurse registered for autonomous practice or physician assistant. No witnesses or notary. The patient or representative should sign at the same time as the clinician.
- It must be printed on yellow paper, or have a full-page yellow background. EMS is required to honor it only if it is yellow. Copies are valid if they are legible, show the signatures, and are on yellow.
- A reduced-size "DNRO device" at the bottom of the form can be cut off, laminated, and carried with a photo ID or pinned to clothing or bedding.
- Anyone can download it in English, Spanish or Haitian Creole from https://www.floridahealth.gov/about-us/resources/do-not-resuscitate-order/.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none. Bills to create a database died in 2026.
- POLST registry: none.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- Fla. Stat. § 765.112: an advance directive made in another state is valid in Florida if it complied with that state's law or with Florida law. AHCA says the same. Not confirmed: any Florida rule on out-of-state POLST forms. EMS honors the Florida DNRO.

## Also in this state
- The AHCA page offers a separate "Designation of Health Care Surrogate for a Minor" (§ 765.2035) for parents. It also offers the Uniform Donor Form, and it links the Joshua Abbott Organ and Tissue Donor Registry (https://www.DonateLifeFlorida.org).
