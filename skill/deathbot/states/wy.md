# Wyoming (WY)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Power of Attorney for Health Care," part of an "advance health care directive" under the Wyoming Health Care Decisions Act, Wyo. Stat. §§ 35-22-401 to 35-22-416. The statutory form (§ 35-22-405) was repealed in 2007, so there is no statutory or state-published form; any written document that meets § 35-22-403 works. The Wyoming Department of Health (WDH) POLST page refers people to Legal Aid of Wyoming for living wills: https://www.lawyoming.org/ Not confirmed: a state agency or bar copy of a current form.
- **Signing:** in writing, signed by the person (or by another person in their presence at their direction), and either acknowledged before a notary or signed by at least two witnesses. Each witness declares under penalty of perjury that they know the person and saw them sign or acknowledge it (§ 35-22-403(b)). These people may not witness: a treating health care provider or their employee; the named agent; the operator or an employee of a community care facility; the operator or an employee of a residential care facility (§ 35-22-403(c)). Relatives and heirs may witness.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** an owner, operator or employee of a residential or community care facility where the person gets care, unless related by blood, marriage or adoption (§ 35-22-403(b)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **When it takes effect:** when the primary physician (or treating primary health care provider if the physician is unavailable) determines the person lacks capacity, unless the document says otherwise (§ 35-22-403(d)–(e)).
- **Default decision-maker:** § 35-22-406. First, anyone the person named by telling their primary health care provider. Then, "suggested" in this order: spouse unless legally separated; adult child; parent; grandparent; adult sibling; adult grandchild; then an adult who has shown special care and concern and knows the person's values. If members of one class disagree, the provider follows the majority. A provider may require a sworn written declaration of authority. Facility owners, operators and staff may not serve unless related.

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Individual instruction," § 35-22-403(a). It may be oral or written and may be part of the same document as the power of attorney. The statute sets no witness requirement for an instruction on its own. The former provisions in §§ 35-22-101 to 109 were repealed in 2005; documents valid under the law in force when signed remain valid (§ 35-22-403(j)).
- Not confirmed: any Wyoming pregnancy or tube-feeding limit in the current act. None appears in §§ 35-22-401 to 416.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "WyoPOLST" (Provider Orders for Life Sustaining Treatment), Wyo. Stat. §§ 35-22-501 to 35-22-509. It is signed by the patient (or their agent, guardian or surrogate) and by a provider who can write medical orders: MD, PA, FNP or APRN. The official form is printed on gold paper, but white copier-paper copies are valid. WDH posts it for download by patients and providers: https://health.wyo.gov/aging/communityliving/polst/
- If a WyoPOLST and an advance directive conflict, the most recently signed one controls (§ 35-22-505(g)).

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- WyoPOLST replaced the Comfort One program (the old CPR directive statutes, §§ 35-22-201 to 208, were repealed in 2015). Comfort One bracelets issued before June 30, 2016 are still recognized. An optional WyoPOLST bracelet is sold by StickyJ Medical ID; WDH says only StickyJ bracelets are officially recognized, and a completed WyoPOLST is required to order one. EMS must comply with a WyoPOLST (§ 35-22-505(a)).

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none.
- POLST registry: none. WDH says "We do not have a central registry for Wyoming." Forms are kept by the patient, providers and local EMS.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- An advance health care directive is valid if it complied with the applicable law when it was made (§ 35-22-403(j)). The act does not name out-of-state documents separately.
- A POLST form from another state is presumed valid and is effective in Wyoming (§ 35-22-505(d)).
- Copies of an advance directive, revocation or surrogate designation have the same effect as the original (§ 35-22-413).

## Also in this state
- Psychiatric advance directive, Wyo. Stat. §§ 35-22-301 to 35-22-308. Under the Department of Health protocols the directive is countersigned by the attending physician or psychiatric personnel, and a named agent signs to accept the appointment (§ 35-22-303(b)). It covers "psychiatric restabilization" measures such as medication, restraint, seclusion and crisis counseling (§ 35-22-301).
- Revoking the designation of an agent requires a signed writing (§ 35-22-404(a)).
