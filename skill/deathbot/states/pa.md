# Pennsylvania (PA)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Health Care Power of Attorney", Health Care Agents and Representatives Act, 20 Pa.C.S. 5451 to 5465. Any written form that names the agent and grants health care authority works (5465). The statute prints an example combined form in 20 Pa.C.S. 5471: "Durable Health Care Power of Attorney and Health Care Treatment Instructions (Living Will)". Using it is optional, and no state agency may require a particular form (5433). Official text: Pennsylvania General Assembly, Title 20, Chapter 54 (https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute?txtType=HTM&ttl=20&div=0&chapter=54).
- **Signing:** dated and signed (or marked) by the person, and witnessed by two people aged 18 or older (5452). Someone who signs for the person at their direction may not witness, and the person's own health care provider may not sign on their behalf. No notary is required. The example form says it is preferable that witnesses are not heirs, creditors or employees of the person's health care providers, and that notarizing as well helps in other states.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** unless related by blood, marriage or adoption, the person's attending physician or other health care provider, or an owner, operator or employee of a health care provider where the person receives care (5455(b)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** a "health care representative" (5461(d)). A person of sound mind can name one by a signed writing or simply by telling the attending physician or provider. Otherwise, in order: spouse (unless a divorce action is pending) together with adult children who are not the spouse's children; adult child; parent; adult sibling; adult grandchild; an adult who knows the person's values and preferences. A signed writing may set a different order.

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Living Will", 20 Pa.C.S. 5441 to 5447; any written form (5447). Part of the same example combined form in 5471 or a separate document. Same signing rule: two witnesses aged 18 or older (5442).
- It becomes operative only once a copy reaches the attending physician and the physician finds the person incompetent and either in an end-stage medical condition or permanently unconscious (5443).
- **Tube feeding:** without a specific written direction, an agent must presume the person would not want tube or IV nutrition and hydration withdrawn. The agent can overcome the presumption only from clearly expressed wishes or a clear judgment of the person's values (5456). The example form has a nutrition choice.
- **Pregnancy:** life-sustaining treatment, nutrition and hydration continue for an incompetent pregnant woman, despite any living will or agent's decision, unless the attending physician and an examining obstetrician certify that they will not lead to a live birth, will physically harm her, or will cause pain that medication cannot relieve (5429).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "Pennsylvania Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment" (PA POLST), coordinated by the Coalition for Quality at the End of Life. No statute; the Department of Health publishes the form in English and Spanish on its EMS resources page and says to print it on 110# Pulsar Pink card stock. Signed by a physician, PA or CRNP and by the patient or surrogate. Not confirmed: that EMS needs an order from medical command to follow a POLST; the DOH EMS page does not say so. Clinicians usually supply it; the public can download it from the DOH page.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "Pennsylvania Out-of-Hospital Do-Not-Resuscitate Order", 20 Pa.C.S. 5481 to 5488. The attending physician issues it at the request of the patient or an authorized surrogate when the patient has an end-stage medical condition, or is permanently unconscious with a living will refusing CPR (5484). The patient signs, or the surrogate signs if the patient cannot make informed decisions. The physician may also issue a Department of Health bracelet or necklace. EMS must honor the order, bracelet or necklace (5487). Prior versions of the sample order remain valid.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none.
- POLST registry: none.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- A living will or health care power of attorney made in another state under that state's law is valid, except where it would direct something inconsistent with Pennsylvania law (5446(b), 5464). EMS and out-of-hospital DNR orders, bracelets and necklaces from other states are recognized to the extent consistent with Pennsylvania law (5487(d)). Not confirmed: a program statement on out-of-state POLST forms.

## Also in this state
- "Mental Health Declaration" and "Mental Health Power of Attorney", 20 Pa.C.S. Chapter 58 (Mental Health Care), separate from Chapter 54 and combinable with each other (5808). Dated, signed and witnessed by two adults (5822).
- The example combined form includes a notice about anatomical donation.
