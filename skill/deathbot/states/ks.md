# Kansas (KS)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Durable Power of Attorney for Health Care Decisions", K.S.A. 58-625 to 58-632. The document must contain durable-power wording, such as "shall not be affected by subsequent disability or incapacity of the principal" (58-625). The form in K.S.A. 58-632 must be followed "in substantially the following form". Its full text is at https://www.ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch58/058_006_0032.html. The agent may decide about organ donation, autopsy and disposition of the body, and may nominate a guardian or conservator (58-627, 58-629(a)).
- **Signing:** the document must be dated and signed in front of two witnesses at least 18 years old, or acknowledged before a notary public (58-629(e)).
- **Who cannot witness:** the agent; anyone related to the person by blood, marriage or adoption; anyone entitled to part of the estate by will or intestacy; anyone directly financially responsible for the person's health care (58-629(e)). These limits apply to witnesses, not to a notary.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the treating health care provider; an employee of the treating provider; an employee, owner, director or officer of a hospital, psychiatric facility, hospice, nursing home or similar institution. The exceptions are someone related by blood, marriage or adoption, or a member of the same religious community bound by vows (58-629(d)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required. Unless the document says otherwise, the agent's power starts when the attending physician determines the person is impaired (58-629(b)).
- **Default decision-maker:** Kansas has no general surrogate-consent statute for treatment decisions. The only statutory family order is for consent to IRB-approved research (K.S.A. 65-4974): the spouse unless legally separated, an adult child, a parent, then an adult relative. Without a named agent, a guardian is the formal route.

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Declaration", Kansas Natural Death Act, K.S.A. 65-28,101 and following. The form in 65-28,103(c) is "substantially in the following form" and may add specific directions. It is separate from the power of attorney. It applies to a terminal condition certified by two physicians.
- **Signing:** two witnesses at least 18, or a notary. The witnesses may not be the person who signed for the declarant, a relative by blood or marriage, an heir or beneficiary, or anyone directly financially responsible for the person's medical care.
- **Pregnancy:** the declaration "shall have no effect during the course of" a diagnosed pregnancy (65-28,103(a)).
- An agent under a power of attorney cannot revoke the person's living will declaration (58-629(b)).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** TPOPP (Transportable Physician Orders for Patient Preferences), the Kansas-Missouri form also known as POLST. The Kansas Medical Society hosts it at https://kmsonline.org/resources/tpopp-polst. The patient or recognized decision maker signs, and so does the authorized provider; verbal orders are accepted with a follow-up signature, and e-signatures are valid. KMS says that in Kansas it must be signed by a physician or PA; the form's own instructions say "a physician, APRN, or PA in compliance with state law". Not confirmed: whether an APRN may sign it in Kansas. Photocopies, faxes and electronic versions of a signed form are valid (form instructions). The form may be downloaded, but not modified. The Center for Practical Bioethics also hosts it.
- A 2015 Kansas Attorney General opinion (2015-1) says TPOPP cannot override statutory limits on what a guardian may decide.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Form:** "Pre-Hospital DNR Request Form", K.S.A. 65-4941 to 65-4943, in the wording of 65-4942. The person signs and dates it, and one adult witness signs. The witness may not have signed for the person, and may not be a relative by blood or marriage, an heir, or anyone financially responsible for the person's care. The attending physician or PA signs to affirm it. A physician signature is not needed for members of a church that relies on prayer alone.
- **Bracelet:** an optional "DNR identifier", a medallion or bracelet marked "DNR" or "do not resuscitate", distributed by an entity certified by the Emergency Medical Services Board (65-4941(e)).

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none.
- POLST registry: none.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- K.S.A. 58-630: a health care power of attorney that was valid under the law of the person's state of residence when signed is valid in Kansas. Not confirmed: any Kansas statute on out-of-state living wills or POLST forms.
