# Mississippi (MS)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Power of Attorney for Health Care", under the Uniform Health-Care Decisions Act (Miss. Code Ann. §§ 41-41-201 to 41-41-229).
- **Statutory form:** Miss. Code Ann. § 41-41-209 has an optional "Advance Health-Care Directive" form. Part 1 is the power of attorney and Part 2 is instructions. The form's text is in the statute. Not confirmed: no state agency page hosting a fillable copy was found.
- **Signing** (§ 41-41-205): the power must be in writing, dated and signed by the person. It must also either be signed by two witnesses, each of whom signs a declaration, or be acknowledged before a notary public. Either option is enough.
- **Who may not witness** (§ 41-41-205):
  - a health-care provider
  - an employee of a health-care provider or facility
  - the agent
  - At least one witness must be neither related to the person by blood, marriage or adoption nor entitled to any part of their estate.
  - Each witness signs a declaration under penalty of perjury that the person is known to them, signed or acknowledged in their presence, and appeared of sound mind and free of duress.
- **Signing in prison:** for someone in Department of Corrections custody, no offender and no department employee may witness (§ 47-5-180).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** an owner, operator or employee of a residential long-term health-care institution where the person is receiving care, unless related by blood, marriage or adoption (§ 41-41-205).
- **When it takes effect:** unless the document says otherwise, the agent acts when the primary physician determines the person lacks capacity.
- **Agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker** (§ 41-41-211): the person may name a surrogate by personally telling the supervising health-care provider. Otherwise the first reasonably available person in this order serves:
  1. the spouse, unless legally separated
  2. an adult child
  3. a parent
  4. an adult brother or sister
  5. if none of these is available, an adult who has shown special care and concern and knows the person's values

  When members of a class disagree, the majority of that class governs. An evenly divided class is disqualified, along with every lower class. A person may disqualify anyone from serving, in a signed writing or by telling the provider.

## Written instructions (living will)
- **Form:** "individual instruction", Part 2 of the same § 41-41-209 form (§ 41-41-205(1)). It may be oral or written, and it may be conditional.
- **Signing:** the statute sets no witness or notary rule for an individual instruction; the witness-or-notary rule in § 41-41-205 is written for the power of attorney (checked against the 1997 bill text that became the Act). Signing the whole form with two qualified witnesses or a notary covers both parts.
- **Mental health institutions:** an agent or surrogate may not consent to admission to a mental health-care institution unless the written directive expressly allows it (§ 41-41-227(5)).
- **Pregnancy:** no pregnancy restriction was found in §§ 41-41-201 to 41-41-229 or in the bill text that enacted them. Not confirmed: the current § 41-41-209 form text was not opened.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** POST, "Physician Orders for Sustaining Treatment" (Miss. Code Ann. § 41-41-302). The State Board of Medical Licensure issues the standardized form, and the statute spells out its sections.
- **Who signs:**
  - the patient's primary physician; the form says "POST must be reviewed and signed by a physician to be valid"
  - the patient, or the agent, guardian or surrogate if the patient lacks capacity; this signature is required
  - the health care professional who prepared the form, if not the physician
  - If the representative is not available to sign the original, a copy signed by the representative goes in the medical record and "on file" is written on the form.
- **Getting the form:** clinicians get it. Not confirmed: no State Board of Medical Licensure download page was found.
- **Copies:** the form says "Use of original form is required" and that the original should travel with the patient. The form must be reviewed at least annually.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- Not confirmed: no Mississippi State Department of Health EMS DNR form or bracelet program was found. Section A of the POST form ("Attempt Resuscitation (CPR)" or "Do Not Attempt Resuscitation (DNR)") is the portable DNR order in use.

## Documents from other states
- An advance health-care directive that complies with §§ 41-41-201 to 41-41-229 is valid "regardless of when or where executed" (§ 41-41-205).
- Not confirmed: whether a directive valid only under the law of the state where it was signed is honored. No Mississippi statement on out-of-state POLST forms was found.

## Also in this state
- **Minors and POST:** the form warns that someone deciding for a minor may not direct denial of treatment that would make the minor a "neglected child" (§ 43-21-105). They also may not withhold medically indicated treatment from a disabled infant (§ 41-41-302).
