# Iowa (IA)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Durable Power of Attorney for Health Care", Iowa Code ch. 144B. The agent is called the "attorney in fact". The statutory form in § 144B.5 "may, but need not," be used, and the full text is in the Iowa Code. Iowa has no state agency form page.
- **Signing:** the document must be dated and either signed by two witnesses who watch the signing in each other's presence and the person's, or acknowledged before a notarial officer in Iowa (§ 144B.3(1)).
- **Who cannot witness:** a health care provider attending the person on the date of signing; an employee of that provider; the attorney in fact; anyone under 18. At least one witness may not be a relative by blood, marriage or adoption within the third degree (§ 144B.3(2)-(3)).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** a health care provider attending the person on the date of signing. Also an employee of that provider, unless related to the person within the third degree (§ 144B.4).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** Iowa Code § 144A.7 lists who may agree with the attending physician, in writing, to withhold or withdraw life-sustaining procedures from a terminal patient who left no declaration. In order: the attorney in fact under ch. 144B; a guardian, with court approval; the spouse; an adult child, or a majority of the reasonably available adult children; a parent, or both parents; an adult sibling. A witness must be present at that consultation. This list covers only life-sustaining procedures in a terminal condition. No broader Iowa default-consent statute was found.

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Declaration" relating to use of life-sustaining procedures, Iowa Code § 144A.3. The optional form is in § 144A.3(5). It is separate from the power of attorney. It takes effect only when the condition is terminal and the person cannot make decisions.
- **Signing:** two witnesses, or acknowledgment before an Iowa notarial officer. At least one witness may not be a relative within the third degree. The attending provider, that provider's employees, and anyone under 18 may not witness (§ 144A.3(2)).
- **Pregnancy:** the declaration has no effect while the fetus could develop to live birth with continued treatment (§ 144A.6(2)). The same rule limits surrogate decisions (§ 144A.7(3)).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** IPOST (Iowa Physician Orders for Scope of Treatment), Iowa Code ch. 144D. The patient or legal representative signs and dates it. So does the patient's physician, advanced registered nurse practitioner or PA. A facilitator who helped prepare it also signs (§ 144D.2). Blank sections mean full treatment.
- **Where to get it:** Iowa HHS posts the form at https://hhs.iowa.gov/ipost, and the public can download it.
- An IPOST does not override an out-of-hospital DNR order already issued under § 144A.7A (§ 144D.4). Not confirmed: whether copies or faxes are valid. The statute and the HHS page do not say.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Form:** out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order, Iowa Code § 144A.7A, on the uniform state form with a "uniform personal identifier". The attending physician or PA signs, and the patient must be in a terminal condition. A copy goes to the patient or the authorized person. The order does not apply to sudden injuries outside the terminal condition, such as a car crash or fire.
- Not confirmed: the current identifier. A 2024 HHS rule notice describes a MedicAlert identifier, but it was not confirmed whether the rule was adopted as proposed.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none.
- POLST registry: none.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- A durable power of attorney for health care, or a living will declaration, made in another state under that state's law is valid in Iowa to the extent it is consistent with Iowa law. So is one that meets federal Department of Veterans Affairs advance directive requirements (§§ 144B.3(4), 144A.3(4)). An IPOST executed in another state under that state's law is valid to the same extent (§ 144D.3(1)).
