# Tennessee (TN)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Advance Directive for Health Care (Tennessee)", the state model form under the Tennessee Health Care Decisions Act, Tenn. Code Ann. 68-11-1801 to 68-11-1815. Since May 9, 2017 this one form combines what used to be the Living Will / Advance Care Plan and the Medical Power of Attorney / Appointment of Health Care Agent. Older forms that were properly signed are still honored. Using the state form is optional. Official copy from the Tennessee Health Facilities Commission: https://www.tennessee.gov/content/dam/tn/hfc/documents/Advance_Directive_for_Health_Care.pdf (tn.gov blocked automated access on the check date; the CaringInfo copy of the form matches the rules below).
- **Signing:** in writing and signed by the person, then either notarized or signed by two competent adult witnesses (68-11-1803(b)). Neither witness may be the agent. At least one witness must be unrelated by blood, marriage or adoption and not entitled to any part of the estate by will or by law. The directive must include an attestation clause in which the witnesses confirm this. The form offers Option A (witnesses) and Option B (notary).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** Not confirmed: 68-11-1803 sets no limits on who may be the agent. Witnesses cannot be the agent.
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** a surrogate (68-11-1806). A person may name a surrogate by personally telling the supervising health care provider. Otherwise, once a patient lacks capacity and has no available agent or guardian, the supervising provider picks an adult who has shown special care and concern, knows the patient's values, and is available and willing. Preference, in order, goes to: spouse (unless legally separated); adult child; parent; adult sibling; other adult relative; any other qualifying adult. Unless related, the treating provider, the provider's employee, or an operator or employee of the health care institution cannot be the surrogate (68-11-1806(g)). A surrogate the patient did not designate may withdraw artificial nutrition and hydration only if two physicians certify that it is merely prolonging dying and the patient is highly unlikely to regain capacity (68-11-1806(e)).

## Written instructions (living will)
- Pages two and three of the same Advance Directive for Health Care ("Individual Instruction"). For each of CPR, life support, treatment of new conditions, and artificially provided nourishment and fluids, the person checks yes or no. The signing rules are the same as above.
- **Pregnancy:** the agent is bound by Tennessee law on pregnancy and termination of pregnancy.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "Physician Orders for Scope of Treatment" (POST), also called the Universal Do Not Resuscitate Order (UDNR) form, Tenn. Code Ann. 68-11-224. Published by the Health Facilities Commission in English and Spanish. A physician with a bona fide physician-patient relationship issues it (68-11-224(b)). A PA, nurse practitioner or clinical nurse specialist may issue one only if no such physician is available, their protocols allow it, and the patient is being discharged or transferred from a nursing home, ICF/IID or hospital (68-11-224(c)). The form says a nurse or social worker may fill in sections A to E after a verbal physician order. The signature of the patient or a parent or guardian should be obtained if possible, but it is not required. The POST stays in effect until revoked and travels with the patient on transfers. Clinicians supply it.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- No separate state EMS DNR form. Qualified EMS personnel must follow a POST on the commission-approved form (68-11-224(e)(1)); the form tells EMS to honor it when it has a physician signature on the front page. Not confirmed: whether Tennessee has a state DNR bracelet or wallet card.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none.
- POLST registry: none.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- An advance directive signed outside Tennessee by a nonresident is given effect if it complies with Part 18 or with the law of the person's state of residence (68-11-1803(h)). Not confirmed: a Tennessee rule on out-of-state POLST forms.

## Also in this state
- The Advance Directive for Health Care has an optional organ donation section. The person can decline, donate any organs or tissue, donate the entire body, or list specific organs.
