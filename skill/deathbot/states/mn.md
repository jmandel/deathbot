# Minnesota (MN)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Health Care Directive". Minnesota uses one document for both naming a "health care agent" (a health care power of attorney) and writing instructions (Minn. Stat. ch. 145C; § 145C.02).
- **Suggested form:** Minn. Stat. § 145C.16 has an optional suggested form. Any writing that meets § 145C.03 is valid. Official copy: https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/145C.16
- **Legal requirements** (§ 145C.03, subd. 1): the directive must be in writing and dated. It must state the person's name and be signed by the person, or by someone they authorize. It must contain a health care instruction, a health care power of attorney, or both.
- **Signing:** the signature must be verified either by a notary public or "by witnesses as provided under this chapter" (§ 145C.03, subd. 1(5)). The statutory suggested form (§ 145C.16) spells this out as a notary (Option 1) or two witnesses who are at least 18 (Option 2). A directive can also be revoked by saying so in front of two witnesses (§ 145C.09).
- **Who may not witness or notarize** (§ 145C.03, subd. 3):
  - A named agent or alternate agent may not witness or notarize.
  - At least one witness must not be a health care provider, or a provider's employee, giving direct care to the person that day.
  - A notary may be such an employee.
- **Who cannot be the agent** (§ 145C.03, subd. 2):
  - A health care provider attending the person, or that provider's employee, either when the directive is signed or when decisions must be made. This does not apply if they are related by blood, marriage, registered domestic partnership or adoption, or if the directive says otherwise.
  - The person named to judge the person's decision-making capacity.
- **When it takes effect:** the agent acts when the attending physician, APRN or PA judges that the person lacks decision-making capacity, unless the directive says otherwise (§ 145C.02).
- **Agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** Minnesota has no statutory surrogate priority list. Chapter 145C keeps any existing right of a person to consent on behalf of another when there is no directive (§ 145C.10(e)). In practice, clinicians turn to close family, and a court-appointed guardian is the formal route.

## Written instructions (living will)
- **Form:** "health care instructions" go in the same Health Care Directive (§ 145C.05). The signing rules are the same as above.
- **Older documents:** living wills made under the old Minn. Stat. ch. 145B are still recognized (§ 145C.01, subd. 1a).
- **Pregnancy:** if a woman lacks capacity and is pregnant, and the fetus could survive to live birth if her life is sustained, providers must presume she would want life-sustaining care (§ 145C.10(g)). This applies unless her directive says otherwise, or there is clear and convincing evidence of contrary wishes. The directive may include her own pregnancy instructions (§ 145C.05, subd. 2(a)(10)).
- **Tube feeding:** the directive may include instructions on artificially administered nutrition and hydration (§ 145C.05, subd. 2(a)(11)). If an agent directs life-sustaining care, nutrition or hydration, providers must take reasonable steps to provide it or transfer the person, unless the directive says otherwise (§ 145C.15).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** POLST Minnesota (Provider Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment), run by the Minnesota Medical Association. No POLST statute.
- **Who signs:** a physician (MD/DO), APRN or PA must sign it for it to be a valid order.
- **Patient signature:** the patient or surrogate is "strongly encouraged" to sign, but the form is valid without that signature. A surrogate who signs may be a health care agent or a guardian. If neither is available, it may be a person the provider believes will follow the patient's wishes, such as a spouse, domestic partner, adult child or sibling.
- **Getting the form:** the public can download it at https://www.mnmed.org/POLST. Patients should not fill it out alone. Earlier versions remain valid.
- **Copies:** photocopied, faxed and electronic versions are all valid.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- Minnesota has no separate state EMS DNR form or bracelet program. EMS follows a POLST.
- Not confirmed: no Minnesota EMS Regulatory Board rule on out-of-hospital DNR was opened.

## Registries
- **Advance directive:** none.
- **POLST:** none. A registry was studied in 2023, and 2025 bills did not pass. A POLST is valid whether or not it is in a registry.
- **EMS DNR:** none.

## Documents from other states
- A health care directive or similar document made in another state is legally sufficient if it complies with that state's law or with § 145C.03 (§ 145C.04).
- A copy has the same effect as the original (§ 145C.10(f)).
- Not confirmed: no Minnesota statement on out-of-state POLST forms was found.

## Also in this state
- **Mental health:** a "declaration regarding intrusive mental health treatment" (Minn. Stat. § 253B.03, subd. 6d) is separate and is recognized alongside the health care directive. An agent can be given authority over intrusive mental health treatment.
- **Opioids:** a directive may bar opioids (§ 145C.05, subd. 2(a)(12); § 145C.17). Minn. Stat. § 145C.18 also provides a separate "nonopioid directive".
- **Anatomical gifts:** the suggested form includes an anatomical gift section.
