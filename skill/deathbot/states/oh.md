# Ohio (OH)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Durable Power of Attorney for Health Care" (often titled "Health Care Power of Attorney"), Ohio Revised Code 1337.11 to 1337.17. Ohio has no mandatory form. Any printed form sold or handed out for people without a lawyer must carry the "Notice to Adult Executing This Document" set out in R.C. 1337.17. The standard free packet ("State of Ohio Health Care Power of Attorney", "Living Will Declaration" and Donor Registry Enrollment Form) is prepared by the Ohio State Bar Association and distributed by the Ohio Hospital Association and Ohio hospitals.
- **Signing:** signed at the end and dated by the person, then either two adult witnesses or a notary (R.C. 1337.12(A)(1)). Witnesses may not be related by blood, marriage or adoption, may not be the named agent or alternate, the attending physician, or the administrator of a nursing home where the person receives care. The notary attests the person appears of sound mind and free of duress.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the attending physician, a nursing home administrator, or an employee or agent of the attending physician or treating facility, unless that person is a competent adult relative or a member of the same religious order (R.C. 1337.12(A)(2)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** for withholding or withdrawing life-sustaining treatment with no living will or agent, R.C. 2133.08 sets the order: guardian; spouse; majority of available adult children; parents; majority of available adult siblings; nearest adult relative by blood or adoption. Consent must be in writing, witnessed by two people, and needs two physicians to find a terminal condition or permanent unconsciousness lasting at least 12 months.

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Declaration" (Living Will Declaration), R.C. 2133.01 to 2133.15. Separate document from the health care power of attorney; same signing rule: two adult witnesses (not relatives, attending physician or nursing home administrator) or a notary (R.C. 2133.02(B)). It must use the terms "terminal condition", "permanently unconscious state" or both.
- **Tube feeding:** to let nutrition and hydration be withdrawn when permanently unconscious, the person must initial or sign next to a conspicuous statement saying so, and two physicians must find it no longer gives comfort (R.C. 2133.02(A)(3)). Without those initials it cannot be withdrawn in that state. The same rule binds the agent (R.C. 1337.13(E)).
- **Pregnancy:** life-sustaining treatment is not withheld or withdrawn under a living will if that would end the pregnancy, unless the attending physician and one other examining physician find the fetus would not be born alive (R.C. 2133.06(B)). An agent has the same limit, with an added exception when the pregnancy or the care substantially risks the person's life (R.C. 1337.13(D)).
- An agent can refuse life-sustaining treatment only if the person is terminally ill or permanently unconscious (R.C. 1337.13(B)).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- None statewide. Ohio has not adopted POLST or MOLST; SB 165 passed the Senate in 2016 and died in the House. Some regional systems (TriHealth, Bon Secours Mercy Health) run pilots. The Ohio DNR order below is the portable order EMS uses.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- State of Ohio DNR Order form, two levels: "DNR Comfort Care" and "DNR Comfort Care-Arrest" (R.C. 2133.21 to 2133.26; Ohio Adm. Code 3701-62). Signed by a physician, or by an APRN or PA under their collaboration or supervision agreements (R.C. 2133.211). Get it from a clinician or facility; the Ohio Department of Health posts the form, and copies may be reproduced.
- Valid DNR identification also includes a hospital-type bracelet insert, a necklace or bracelet with the DNR logo ("arrest" added for Comfort Care-Arrest), a wallet card, or an operative living will that refuses CPR (Ohio Adm. Code 3701-62-04).

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none.
- POLST registry: none (no statewide program).
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- A living will made in another state is valid if it complies with that state's law or substantially complies with Ohio's (R.C. 2133.14). Not confirmed: an equivalent reciprocity section for out-of-state health care powers of attorney in R.C. 1337.11 to 1337.17. Out-of-state POLST forms have no Ohio program to map to.

## Also in this state
- Declaration for Mental Health Treatment, R.C. Chapter 2135: names a mental health proxy and states treatment preferences. Two adult witnesses or a notary; each proxy must also sign. Witnesses may not be the person's mental health provider or its relative or employee, a facility owner or operator or its relative or employee, a relative of the person, or a named proxy (R.C. 2135.06).
- The Ohio State Bar Association packet includes a Donor Registry Enrollment Form.
