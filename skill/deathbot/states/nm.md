# New Mexico (NM)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Power of Attorney for Health Care", Part 1 of the "Optional Advance Health-Care Directive" set out in the Uniform Health-Care Decisions Act, NMSA 1978, Sections 24-7A-1 to 24-7A-18 (form at 24-7A-4). The form is optional; any writing signed by the person works (24-7A-2). The form has Part 1 (power of attorney for health care), Part 2 (instructions for health care), Part 3 (primary care practitioner) and an anatomical gift designation.
- **Signing:** in writing and signed by the person while they have capacity (24-7A-2). New Mexico requires no witnesses and no notary. The form has an "(Optional) Signatures of Witnesses" block and says two witnesses are "recommended but not required".
- **Who cannot be the agent:** an owner, operator or employee of a health-care institution where the person is receiving care, unless related by blood, marriage or adoption (24-7A-2).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** NMSA 24-7A-5. If there is no agent or guardian and the person has named no surrogate, these family members act in order: spouse (not if legally separated or if an annulment, divorce, dissolution or separation petition is pending); an individual in a long-term relationship of indefinite duration with a commitment similar to a spouse's; adult child; parent; adult sibling; grandparent. If none is available, an adult who has shown special care and concern and knows the person's values may act. An evenly divided class is disqualified, along with lower classes. A person can disqualify anyone in a signed writing or by telling a provider. An unrelated owner, operator or employee of the treating institution may not be surrogate.

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Instructions for Health Care", Part 2 of the same optional form (24-7A-4); an "individual instruction" under 24-7A-2. Same signing rule (signature only).
- The form has separate initial boxes for artificial nutrition and for artificial hydration (item 7), and lets the person choose not to prolong life in stated conditions. The Act has no pregnancy clause.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "New Mexico Medical Orders for Scope of Treatment" (MOST). There is no MOST statute; the form is recognized by Department of Health EMS rule 7.27.6 NMAC (7.27.6.7(M)). It is signed and dated by a physician (MD or DO), APN or PA, and by the patient or their health-care decision maker. End of Life Options New Mexico distributes the 2-page form; anyone can download it from the group's MOST page, including a Spanish version. Green card stock is recommended but any color is honored. Photocopies and faxes are valid. A registered nurse may sign on a verbal order from a physician, APN or PA (7.27.6.8). EMS follow EMS DNR orders, MOST instructions and durable powers of attorney; where a person has an EMS DNR order and also a MOST or durable power of attorney, the most recent document controls for EMS treatment (7.27.6.9(E) NMAC).

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "EMS DNR order", 7.27.6 NMAC (effective December 12, 2017). It is issued by a physician, APN or PA and signed by the person or their health-care decision maker on a bureau-approved form (7.27.6.7(J), 7.27.6.8). An optional EMS DNR bracelet exists, and EMS treat an intact bracelet as a valid order (7.27.6.9). The Department of Health lists its EMS DNR form and EMS MOST form as "archived"; MOST is the order in current use. Not confirmed: whether a current EMS DNR form is still distributed.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none.
- POLST registry: none (NM MOST has no registry).
- EMS DNR registry: none; EMS DNR (archived form) or MOST is used.

## Documents from other states
- A directive or similar document made in another state in compliance with that state's law is valid in New Mexico to the same extent as one made there (24-7A-16(C)).
- EMS may honor out-of-state DNR orders and durable powers of attorney that comply with that state's law or New Mexico's, if consistent with New Mexico public policy (7.27.6.10 NMAC).

## Also in this state
- Advance directive for mental health treatment, Mental Health Care Treatment Decisions Act, NMSA 24-7B-1 et seq. It is valid only if signed by the person and one adult witness (24-7B-4(I)). The witness may not be the person's agent, a relative by blood or marriage, an heir or creditor, the attending qualified health care professional, or an owner, operator or employee of the mental health treatment facility treating the person (24-7B-4(J)).
- The optional advance directive form includes an anatomical gift designation (item 9).
