# Idaho (ID)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** Idaho calls these documents an "advance care planning document" (ACPD), also called an advance directive or health care directive. The law is the Medical Consent and Natural Death Act, Idaho Code §§ 39-4502(1), 39-4510. A health care agent is named in the ACPD (§ 39-4502(10)). The Department of Health and Welfare (DHW) "may create and promulgate an optional form", and that form "is not mandatory" (§ 39-4510). DHW posts its Idaho Advance Directive form, a decision worksheet and a wallet card at https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/services-programs/advance-directives-and-registry-services.
- **Signing:** no witnesses or notary are required. A valid ACPD needs the person's name, date of birth, phone number and mailing address; the signature of the person or their authorized agent; and the date (§ 39-4510). Witness names and notarization are optional items. DHW says the advance directive "does not need to be notarized or signed by a medical provider."
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the statute names no excluded group.
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** Idaho Code § 39-4504(1), in order: a court-appointed guardian; the health care agent in an ACPD whose conditions have been met; the spouse; an adult child; a parent; a person named in a delegation of parental authority; any relative; then any other competent person who says they are responsible for the person's care. A surrogate may not decide against the person's ACPD or wishes the person stated while capable.

## Written instructions (living will)
- The same ACPD holds treatment instructions and the agent appointment (§ 39-4510). The same minimal signing rule applies.
- **Pregnancy:** an ACPD may include instructions about pregnancy (§ 39-4510). Idaho's old rule that a directive "shall have no force during the course of" a pregnancy was challenged in federal court in 2021. In 2022 the state settled and issued a new form without that exclusion; the Secretary of State posted a notice of the change.
- **Tube feeding:** artificial nutrition and hydration may not be withdrawn or denied when the patient, a valid ACPD or the surrogate asks for it (§ 39-4514(3)).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** Idaho POST (Physician Orders for Scope of Treatment), Idaho Code § 39-4512A. It is signed by a "licensed independent practitioner" and by the person or the person's surrogate. A licensed independent practitioner is a physician, PA or APRN (§ 39-4502(13)).
- **Where to get it:** clinicians have it. DHW does not post it on its page, but sends a copy on request to ihdr@dhw.idaho.gov.
- **Copies:** "A photostatic, facsimile, or electronic copy of a valid POST form may be treated as an original" (§ 39-4514(9)(b)). The POST goes with the person across care settings.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- POST replaced the Comfort One EMS DNR program in 2007. A person with a POST may wear a "POST identification device", which is jewelry on the wrist, neck or ankle (§§ 39-4502(19), 39-4512A). CPR is presumed unless an exception applies, such as a valid DNR order or a POST identification device (§ 39-4514(5)).

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: the Idaho Healthcare Directive Registry, run by DHW (it moved there from the Secretary of State). It is active and self-service online at https://idaho-acp.vyncahealth.com/patientportal/user/login. No fee is listed. Registering is optional and does not affect validity (§ 39-4514(10)).
- POLST registry: POST forms are stored in the same registry.
- EMS DNR registry: the same registry.

## Documents from other states
- Not confirmed: Idaho has no explicit out-of-state clause in ch. 39-45. An ACPD includes any document that is a competent person's "authentic expression" of health care wishes (§ 39-4502(1)), which covers directives made elsewhere.

## Also in this state
- Declaration for mental health treatment, Idaho Code title 66, ch. 6 (§ 66-601 and following).
