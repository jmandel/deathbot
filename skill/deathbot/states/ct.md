# Connecticut (CT)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Appointment of Health Care Representative", Conn. Gen. Stat. § 19a-576, with a suggested form in § 19a-577 ("may, but need not be, in substantially the following form"). The Attorney General publishes the official forms at https://portal.ct.gov/ag/health-issues/connecticuts-living-will-laws:
  - the standalone appointment;
  - an "Advance Directives Combined Form", which covers a health care representative, living will, conservator of the person and anatomical gift, and lets the person skip any section by initialing it;
  - the booklet "Your Rights to Make Health Care Decisions".
- **Signing:** signed and dated in the presence of two adult witnesses, who also sign. The named health care representative may not witness or sign the document (§ 19a-576(a)). No notary is required.
  - **Mental health facility residents:** at least one witness must be unaffiliated with the facility, and at least one must be a physician, APRN or licensed clinical psychologist with specialized training in treating mental illness (§ 19a-576(b)).
  - **Developmental services facility residents:** the same structure applies, with training in developmental disabilities (§ 19a-576(c)).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** for a patient, resident or applicant of a hospital, residential care home, rest home or nursing home, the operator, administrator or an employee of that facility, unless related. Also barred: a government agency administrator or employee financially responsible for the person's care. The attending physician or nurse practitioner cannot serve as both representative and attending clinician (§ 19a-576(d)-(e)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** Connecticut has no general surrogate consent statute.
  - For decisions about withholding life support, § 19a-571 has the attending physician or APRN determine the patient's wishes. Sources include the health care representative, next of kin, a guardian or conservator, and a person designated under § 1-56r. This applies only if the patient is terminal, or permanently unconscious after neurological consultation.
  - "Next of kin" is defined in order in § 19a-570(10): spouse, adult child, either parent, adult sibling, grandparent, and so on.

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Living Will" or "Health Care Instructions", Conn. Gen. Stat. § 19a-575a. It is signed and dated with at least two witnesses. It can be standalone, or part of the AG's combined form.
- § 19a-571: if the patient is neither terminal nor permanently unconscious, beneficial treatment, including nutrition and hydration, must be provided.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** Connecticut MOLST, "Medical Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment", Conn. Gen. Stat. § 19a-580h. The form is at https://portal.ct.gov/dph/-/media/dph/dph-2025/molst/molst-digital-form.pdf, with a fillable version on the DPH MOLST page (https://portal.ct.gov/dph/about/molst).
- **Who signs:** the patient (or LAR), and an MD/DO, APRN or PA who has completed the DPH MOLST training required by § 19a-580h: "Only certified MOLST providers may sign off on an individual's MOLST form." Under § 19a-580h, as the DPH public FAQ (July 2026) states it, a legally authorized representative (LAR) is a minor patient's parent, a guardian appointed by a Connecticut Probate Court, or a health care representative the patient designated in writing. Conservators and next of kin cannot sign a MOLST unless the patient also designated them as health care representative before incapacity. (The older DPH consumer FAQ web page lists a conservator as a possible LAR; the public FAQ PDF is the more specific statement.)
- **Where to get it:** a certified provider completes it. The public can view and print it from DPH.
- **Copies:** DPH says a person may print a copy or ask the provider for one. Not confirmed: an explicit statement that copies and faxes are valid orders.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **DNR bracelet:** Conn. Gen. Stat. § 19a-580d; Regs. Conn. State Agencies § 19a-580d-1 et seq. The DPH public FAQ points to the Connecticut College of Emergency Physicians DNR program for bracelets.
  - A physician or APRN writes the DNR order. The patient or representative then gets the bracelet from a designated agency, which keeps a copy of the order.
  - The bracelet is worn on the wrist or ankle, shows the patient's and physician's names, and must not be cut or broken.
  - Outside a health care institution it is "the only valid indication recognized by EMS providers" of a DNR order under the rule.
  - DPH says orange plastic bracelets and metal bracelets from the Connecticut College of Emergency Physicians remain valid.
- EMS also honors a signed MOLST.

## Registries
- **Advance directive registry:** unclear. There is no statutory registry. The DPH FAQ mentions possible viewing through the state health information exchange (Connie) and MyDirectives.
- **POLST registry:** none. Connecticut MOLST is signed by certified providers only.
- **EMS DNR registry:** none. Connecticut uses a DNR bracelet (Regs. § 19a-580d), and EMS also honors MOLST.

## Documents from other states
- Not confirmed: a Connecticut statute recognizing out-of-state advance directives was not found.
- DPH says a Connecticut MOLST may or may not be honored in other states. It does not state whether Connecticut honors out-of-state POLST forms.

## Also in this state
- The AG combined form lets the person name a conservator of the person in advance and make an anatomical gift.
- The special witness rules above apply to residents of mental health and developmental services facilities.
