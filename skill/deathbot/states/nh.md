# New Hampshire (NH)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "New Hampshire Advance Directive Form", whose Part I is the "Durable Power of Attorney for Health Care" and Part II the "Living Will", RSA 137-J (Written Directives for Medical Decision Making). An advance directive signed now must be "substantially in the form" of RSA 137-J:20, and the person must first be given the disclosure statement in RSA 137-J:19 (RSA 137-J:13). The Foundation for Healthy Communities (healthynh.org), the state's POLST and advance care planning program, posts a fillable copy. It must be printed and signed in person.
- **Signing:** signed before either two or more witnesses or a notary public or justice of the peace (RSA 137-J:14). A witness may not be the agent, the person's spouse or heir at law, anyone entitled to part of the estate under a will, trust or deed or by law, the attending practitioner, or someone under that practitioner's direction. No more than one witness may be the person's health or residential care provider or its employee. Witnesses affirm the person appeared of sound mind and free from duress. Someone else may sign for a person who is physically unable, in their presence and at their direction.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the attending practitioner or someone acting under their direct authority, and a nonrelative who is an employee of the person's health care or residential care provider (RSA 137-J:8).
- **Agent limits:** an agent or surrogate cannot consent to voluntary admission to a state institution, voluntary sterilization, psychosurgery or electroconvulsive therapy (RSA 137-J:5, V).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** RSA 137-J:35. The order is: spouse, civil union partner or common law spouse (not if there is a divorce proceeding, separation agreement or restraining order); adult child; parent; adult sibling; adult grandchild; grandparent; adult aunt, uncle, niece or nephew; close friend; financial power of attorney agent or conservator; guardian of the estate. The attending practitioner names the surrogate in the medical record. Surrogate authority lasts up to 180 days unless the patient is actively dying, and it ends if the patient objects (RSA 137-J:37).

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Living Will", Part II of the same statutory form (RSA 137-J:20); same signing rules. The person initials choice A (all life-sustaining treatment) or B (no treatment that would be excessively burdensome). Under B, statements 1 to 4 list such situations (actively dying, permanently unconscious, advanced incurable progressive condition, other); the person crosses out and initials any they disagree with. The form says that stopping medically administered nutrition and hydration may be part of comfort care.
- **Pregnancy:** an agent or surrogate cannot consent to withholding life-sustaining treatment from a pregnant person, unless the attending practitioner and an examining obstetrician certify that treatment will not allow live birth, or will harm the person or prolong severe pain that medication cannot relieve (RSA 137-J:5, V(c)).
- A living will or health care power of attorney is not a DNR order for EMS (RSA 137-J:25 to 137-J:27).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "POLST" (Provider Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment), recognized in RSA 137-J (RSA 137-J:26, 137-J:27). The New Hampshire POLST form (2023 version, bright yellow) is signed by a physician, PA or APRN, and by the patient or patient representative; electronic signatures are valid, and verbal orders are accepted with a follow-up signature. Copies, faxes and electronic versions are valid, and it does not expire. Clinicians order forms from the Foundation for Healthy Communities; the public can view a sample. A POLST counts as a DNR only if it says "This will constitute a DNR Order" (RSA 137-J:26, V).

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "Portable DNR" (P-DNR), a bright pink form, RSA 137-J:25 to 137-J:33. An attending practitioner (MD, DO, APRN or PA) issues it with the consent of the patient, agent or surrogate. It has signature lines for the provider and for the patient, parent, agent (DPOAH) or guardian, and a wallet card. It is valid throughout the state, including in ambulances and homes (RSA 137-J:26, VI; 137-J:27). A DNR bracelet or necklace reading "NH DNR" or "NH Do Not Resuscitate" may be issued only on a valid DNR order (RSA 137-J:33). EMS also honors a POLST that marks Do Not Resuscitate.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none.
- POLST registry: the New Hampshire POLST Registry Act (RSA 137-L, amended 2025) creates one "subject to sufficient funding"; not found operating.
- EMS DNR registry: none; the P-DNR is a paper form.

## Documents from other states
- A DNR, POLST, durable power of attorney for health care, living will or similar document valid where it was signed is as effective as a New Hampshire one (RSA 137-J:17).
