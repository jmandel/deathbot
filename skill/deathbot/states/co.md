# Colorado (CO)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Medical Durable Power of Attorney" (MDPOA), Colorado Patient Autonomy Act, C.R.S. §§ 15-14-503 to -509, with the main section at § 15-14-506. Colorado has no statutory or required form. The Colorado Bar Association page (https://cobar.org/advanceMedicalDirectives) does not post an MDPOA form. Hospital systems and counties publish forms; for example, Larimer County posts one at https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/mdpoa_form_in_word.docx. The agent may consent to or refuse any treatment, including artificial nourishment and hydration.
- **Signing:** Not confirmed in statute text: the statute sets no witness or notary requirement, and only the person's signature is needed. Secondary guides and the UCHealth form say so, and the § 15-14-506 text opened lists no formalities. Many forms add optional witness and notary lines so the document is accepted in other states.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** no restriction found in §§ 15-14-503 to -509.
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required for an MDPOA.
- **Other rules:** divorce, annulment or legal separation revokes a spouse's appointment as agent unless the document says otherwise (§ 15-14-506(5)). An agent cannot override the person's own objection.
- **Default decision-maker:** C.R.S. § 15-18.5-103 has no ranked list. The "interested persons" are the spouse, either parent, any adult child, sibling or grandchild, and any close friend. They must try to reach consensus on one proxy, choosing the person who has a close relationship with the patient and "is most likely to be currently informed of the patient's wishes." If anyone disagrees, any interested person may go to court for a guardianship.
  - The attending physician cannot be the proxy, except a willing physician chosen under § 15-18.5-103(4)(c) with the concurrence of a medical ethics committee.
  - A proxy may have artificial nutrition and hydration withheld only if the attending physician and a second physician trained in neurology or neurosurgery certify that it merely prolongs dying and is unlikely to restore independent neurological function.

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Declaration as to Medical or Surgical Treatment", Colorado Medical Treatment Decision Act, C.R.S. § 15-18-101 et seq., with the form in § 15-18-104. It may be combined with an MDPOA. The Colorado Bar's living will form: https://www.cobar.org/portals/cobar/repository/livingwill1003.pdf.
- **Signing:** signed in the presence of two witnesses, or acknowledged before a notary (§ 15-18-106(1)). The people listed in § 15-18-105, who also may not sign for a declarant unable to sign, may not be a witness or the notary (§ 15-18-106(2)):
  - any physician, including the attending physician;
  - an employee of the attending physician or of the treating facility;
  - anyone with a claim against the estate;
  - anyone who expects to inherit under a will or as an heir.
- It applies to a terminal condition or a persistent vegetative state. For artificial nutrition and hydration, the person picks one: stop it; continue it for a set period; or continue it indefinitely. A physician or APRN may continue it for comfort if stopping causes pain.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** Colorado MOST, "Medical Orders for Scope of Treatment" (C.R.S. § 15-18.7-101 et seq.). The current form is the 2015 version.
- **Who signs:** the adult or an authorized surrogate decision-maker, and a physician, APRN or PA (§§ 15-18.7-103, -104).
- **Where to get it:** clinicians complete it. The public can download it at https://cuanschutz.edu/centers/bioethicshumanities/comost.
- **Copies:** photocopies, faxes and electronic copies have the same effect as the original. Bright green paper is recommended, not required. An oral order from the clinician must be countersigned within 30 days.
- EMS and facilities must comply with it. Without a form declining CPR, consent to CPR is presumed.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Form:** "CPR Directive" (C.R.S. § 15-18.6-101 et seq.). An adult may execute it, or a surrogate for an adult who lacks capacity. The person, or a person authorized to sign for them, signs, and the attending physician countersigns (§ 15-18.6-103(f), (h)). It is obtained from a doctor's office or the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE), and the doctor signs after a consultation.
- The Colorado Bar says the person may receive an identification bracelet. Not confirmed: the current CDPHE rule text on "No CPR" bracelets and pendants.
- EMS also honors a MOST form.

## Registries
- **Advance directive registry:** unclear. C.R.S. 25-54-102 calls for an electronic system, but no launch was found. Only providers can upload.
- **POLST registry:** none. The Colorado MOST program has no registry "yet".
- **EMS DNR registry:** none found. Colorado uses the CPR Directive, which has a bracelet option.

## Documents from other states
- An MDPOA or similar instrument signed in another state is presumed to comply with Colorado law, and providers may rely on it in good faith (§ 15-14-509).
- A MOST-type form executed in another state is honored if it reasonably satisfies § 15-18.7-103 (§ 15-18.7-104).
- Not confirmed: the rule for out-of-state living wills and CPR directives.

## Also in this state
- An MDPOA may include a separate anatomical gift statement (§ 15-14-506(3.5)).
