# Oklahoma (OK)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** two current routes.
  - The statutory "Advance Directive for Health Care" under the Oklahoma Advance Directive Act, 63 O.S. 3101.1 to 3101.16 (form in 3101.4). Part I is the Living Will, Part II names a health care agent (the statute's term is "health care proxy"), Part III covers anatomical gifts, Part IV general provisions, Part V signatures. Any part may be used; Part V is required. Only this route can give an agent power to withhold or withdraw life-sustaining treatment, nutrition or hydration.
  - "Health Care Power of Attorney" under the Oklahoma Health Care Agent Act, 63 O.S. 3111.1 to 3111.13. Oklahoma DHS publishes it as OKDHS Pub. 99-63. It covers ordinary care and lets the agent sign a do-not-resuscitate consent, but says it does not authorize withholding life-sustaining treatment, nutrition or hydration.
  - Neither form is mandatory. Not confirmed: a state-agency copy of the 3101.4 form; the Oklahoma State Department of Health page links only to MyDirectives. CaringInfo (National Alliance for Care at Home) publishes the 3101.4 form.
- **Signing:** both forms need two witnesses, at least 18, not related to the person and not heirs or beneficiaries (63 O.S. 3101.4; Pub. 99-63). No notary is needed.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** unless related by blood, marriage or adoption, an owner, operator or employee of a residential long-term care facility where the person receives care.
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** 63 O.S. 3102.4 sets the order: guardian; health care proxy; attorney-in-fact with health authority; spouse; adult children; parents; adult siblings; other adult relatives by kinship; close friends with regular contact (an affidavit is prima facie evidence). A majority decides within a class. Before deciding, the surrogate must sign a statement that they have no abuse, neglect or exploitation convictions or findings (3102.4(B), 3102.5).
- **Coming change:** HB 1687, the Uniform Health Care Decisions Act, was signed in May 2026 and takes effect July 1, 2027. It repeals 3101.1 to 3101.16, 3102.4, 3102.5 and the Health Care Agent Act and adds a new optional form at 63 O.S. 3100.11. Directives signed before then stay valid if they complied with the law when made (Sec. 30).

## Written instructions (living will)
- Part I of the same statutory Advance Directive for Health Care (63 O.S. 3101.4). It applies when the person is terminally ill, persistently unconscious or has an end-stage condition, with separate choices for life-sustaining treatment and for artificially administered nutrition and hydration in each.
- **Pregnancy:** if the attending physician knows of a pregnancy, treatment, nutrition and hydration are given unless the person has authorized withholding during pregnancy in their own words (form Part IV(c)).
- **Tube feeding:** the Hydration and Nutrition for Incompetent Patients Act presumes a person wants nutrition and hydration unless a directive specifically refuses it.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "Oklahoma Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment (POLST)" (OkPOLST), Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment Act, 63 O.S. 3105.1 to 3105.5. The Attorney General set the format and the State Board of Medical Licensure and Supervision publishes it. Signed by the attending physician and by the patient or representative; any other preparing professional also signs. A photocopy or fax is legal and valid. The form must be reviewed at least annually. Clinicians supply it.
- An out-of-state POLST-type form loses validity 10 days after admission to an Oklahoma medical care facility, and an Oklahoma-format POLST replaces it (3105.3(C)).

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "Oklahoma Do-Not-Resuscitate (DNR) Consent Form", Oklahoma Do-Not-Resuscitate Act, 63 O.S. 3131.1 to 3131.14 (form in 3131.5, preferred but not required). Signed by the person or their representative (an attorney-in-fact, health care proxy or guardian), with two witnesses. EMS honors it. A Certification of Physician section covers an incapacitated person with no representative. DHS publishes it as OKDHS Pub. 97-20. The form refers to "do-not-resuscitate identification" worn on the person. Not confirmed: a state-issued bracelet.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: unclear. The state health department page links only to MyDirectives (a private service) despite a statutory registry.
- POLST registry: none.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- An advance directive made in another state is valid if it complies with that state's law or Oklahoma's, to the extent it does not exceed what Oklahoma allows. It does not authorize withholding nutrition or hydration unless it says so specifically (63 O.S. 3101.14; carried into HB 1687 Sec. 19). For POLST, see the 10-day rule above.

## Also in this state
- From July 1, 2027, HB 1687 allows advance directives covering mental health care.
