# New York (NY)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Health Care Proxy", Public Health Law Article 29-C (PHL 2980 to 2994). The statute gives a form that "may, but need not," be used (PHL 2981(5)(d)). The Department of Health form with instructions is publication 1430 (https://www.health.ny.gov/publications/1430.pdf), with 12 translations (Spanish 1431, Chinese 1401, Russian 1402 and others). It includes an optional organ, eye and tissue donation section.
- **Signing:** signed and dated before two adult witnesses, who also sign and state that the person appeared to sign willingly and free from duress. Someone else may sign for the person in their presence and at their direction. The named agent or alternate may not witness. No notary is needed or provided for (PHL 2981(2)(a)). Either or both witnesses may witness by live audio-video if the person shows photo ID to a remote witness who does not know them, a legible copy reaches the remote witness within 24 hours, and the remote witness signs and returns it (PHL 2981(2-a)). For residents of an Office of Mental Health facility, one witness must be unaffiliated with the facility, and if the facility is a hospital, one must be a qualified psychiatrist or psychiatric nurse practitioner. For residents of an OPWDD facility, one witness must be unaffiliated and one must be a physician, NP, PA or clinical psychologist meeting the statute's criteria (PHL 2981(2)(b)-(c)).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** an operator, administrator or employee of a hospital where the person is a patient or resident, or has applied for admission, unless related by blood, marriage or adoption. A physician, PA or NP may be agent but then may not act as the attending practitioner. A person already serving as agent for ten principals may not be named, unless they are the person's spouse, child, parent, sibling or grandparent, or that relative's child or spouse (PHL 2981(3)(d)). A physician or NP affiliated with a mental hygiene facility or psychiatric unit may not be agent for a patient there unless related (PHL 2981(3)(b)).
- **Tube feeding:** the agent may decide about artificial nutrition and hydration only if the person's wishes about it are reasonably known (PHL 2982(2)). The form and DOH instructions tell people to write those wishes down.
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** Family Health Care Decisions Act, PHL Article 29-CC (2994-a to 2994-u). It applies to decisions in hospitals and nursing homes, and to hospice care wherever given (PHL 2994-b). The order is: court-appointed Article 81 guardian; spouse (not legally separated) or domestic partner; adult child; parent; adult sibling; close friend (PHL 2994-d(1)). A surrogate may refuse life-sustaining treatment only if, with a second practitioner's independent concurrence, treatment would be an extraordinary burden and the person is expected to die within six months or is permanently unconscious, or treatment would be inhumane or extraordinarily burdensome and the condition is irreversible or incurable. In nursing homes the second ground (other than a CPR decision) also needs ethics committee or court review (PHL 2994-d(5)).

## Written instructions (living will)
- New York has no living will statute and no statutory form. Courts accept a written living will as clear and convincing evidence of the person's wishes (Matter of Westchester County Medical Center (O'Connor), 72 N.Y.2d 517 (1988)). DOH publication 1430 lets the person write instructions and limits on the proxy form itself; that is the usual place to state tube-feeding wishes. Two adult witnesses are customary.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "Medical Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment" (MOLST), form DOH-5003 (revised June 2025; earlier completed forms stay valid). Signed by a physician, NP or PA after discussion with the patient or their health care agent or surrogate. The decision-maker signs, or gives verbal consent observed by two witnesses. For a patient with an intellectual or developmental disability, only a physician may sign, and one witness must be the treating physician. Anyone can download the form at https://www.health.ny.gov/forms/doh-5003.pdf. It may be printed on any color; "Pulsar Pink" is recommended. All health care professionals, including EMS, must follow it. MOLST is the only authorized form for a combined nonhospital DNR and DNI order (PHL Article 29-CCC). For a person with I/DD who lacks capacity and has no proxy, SCPA 1750-b and the OPWDD checklist also apply.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "Nonhospital Order Not to Resuscitate" (DOH-3474, one page), PHL Article 29-CCC (2994-aa to 2994-gg). It is signed by a physician, NP or PA (only a physician for a person with I/DD), with consent from the patient or their agent or surrogate. It stays valid even if the 90-day review is missed. A standard bracelet may be worn, but no one may require it (PHL 2994-dd(2)). EMS also honors a MOLST.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none.
- POLST registry: eMOLST (run through Excellus), closed to new provider enrollment since May 2025.
- EMS DNR registry: none; Nonhospital DNR (DOH-3474) or MOLST on paper.

## Documents from other states
- A health care proxy or similar document made in another state in compliance with that state's law is valid (PHL 2990).
- Not confirmed: a statute or DOH statement on honoring out-of-state POLST forms or nonhospital DNR orders.
