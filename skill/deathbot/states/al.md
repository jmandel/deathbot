# Alabama (AL)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Advance Directive for Health Care (Living Will and Health Care Proxy)", Alabama Natural Death Act, Ala. Code § 22-8A-4. The directive "shall be substantially in the following form" (§ 22-8A-4(h)) and may add other directions. Section 2 of the form names a health care proxy and a second choice. The Alabama Hospital Association posts the form in English (https://www.alaha.org/app/uploads/2026/07/advdirective.pdf) and Spanish from https://www.alaha.org/advance-directives/. A durable power of attorney for health care under Ala. Code § 26-1A-404 is also allowed.
- **Signing:** the person must be at least 19 and of sound mind. Two witnesses, each at least 19, sign (§ 22-8A-4(c)(4)). No notary is required. A witness may not be the person who signed for the declarant, the health care proxy, related to the person by blood, adoption or marriage, entitled to any part of the estate, or directly responsible for paying for the person's medical care.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** "Under no circumstances" may the patient's health care provider or a nonrelative employee of that provider act as health care proxy (§ 22-8A-4(b)(4); the same bar applies to a health care power of attorney under § 26-1A-404).
- **The agent's acceptance:** the statutory form has Section 6, "Signature of Proxy", where the proxy and the second choice sign that they are willing to serve.
- **Default decision-maker:** Ala. Code § 22-8A-11(d). In order: a judicially appointed guardian whose appointment covers these decisions; the spouse, unless legally separated or in a divorce proceeding; an adult child; a parent; an adult sibling; the surviving adult relative of the next closest degree of kinship; then, if no relative can be found, a committee of the primary treating physician and the facility's ethics committee acting unanimously. The surrogate must contact everyone in the same and higher classes and sign the "Certification of Health Care Decision Surrogate" (Ala. Admin. Code r. 420-5-19, Appendix I, amended effective Oct 15, 2022) under penalty of perjury. Since 2022 (HB191) the certification is signed in front of two witnesses, each at least 19; the patient's health care provider and a nonrelative employee of the provider may not witness. The older rule text and the ADPH page still say "duly notarized"; the current Appendix I form has the two witness lines.

## Written instructions (living will)
- The living will is Section 1 of the same statutory form (§ 22-8A-4), so the signing rules are the same. It covers two conditions: terminally ill or injured, and permanently unconscious. For each, the person initials yes or no to life-sustaining treatment and to food and water through a tube or IV.
- **Pregnancy:** the form says that if the person is or becomes pregnant, its choices "will not be followed until after the birth of the baby."
- **Tube feeding:** the person must separately initial whether the proxy may decide about food and water through a tube or IV.
- The person also chooses how much freedom the proxy has: follow the form only; follow it and decide what it does not cover; or decide even against the form.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- Alabama has no POLST statute. The Alabama Department of Public Health (ADPH) palliative care page links the National POLST form, and polst.org lists the Alabama Coalition for Advanced Care Planning as the state contact. It cannot substitute for the Alabama Portable DNAR (see below).
- For minors under 19 who are terminally ill or injured, there is the "Order for Pediatric Palliative and End of Life (PPEL) Care" (Ala. Code § 22-8A-15; r. 420-5-19-.03). A parent or guardian signs, and two physicians sign.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Form:** "Alabama Portable Physician Do Not Attempt Resuscitation Order" (Act 2016-96; Ala. Admin. Code r. 420-5-19-.02, Appendix II), at https://www.alabamapublichealth.gov/ems/assets/dnar.form.010317.pdf. Consent comes from one of four sections: the patient; an advance directive with life-sustaining-treatment instructions plus an attestation that the patient lacks capacity (staff such as a nurse, PA, CRNP or social worker may attest); the proxy or attorney-in-fact; or a certified surrogate. Then a physician signs Section V. A PA or CRNP cannot sign the order. An Alabama facility may accept one signed by a physician licensed in another state after verifying the license (ADPH).
- EMS and facility staff are authorized to follow it (r. 420-5-19-.02(3)). An electronic version with the same content and copies on colored paper are valid; pink is recommended. The wording may not be changed. It is not for minors under 19 (use PPEL) and does not cover artificial nutrition and hydration.
- Not confirmed: any state DNR bracelet or wallet card. The rule and ADPH pages do not mention one.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none.
- POLST registry: none.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- Ala. Code § 22-8A-12: an advance directive made in another state is valid in Alabama if it was executed in compliance with that state's law or Alabama law. It does not authorize care that Alabama law otherwise prohibits.
- ADPH says a POLST from another state, or one downloaded from the internet, cannot substitute for the Alabama Portable DNAR form.
