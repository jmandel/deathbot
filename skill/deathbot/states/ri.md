# Rhode Island (RI)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Statutory Form Durable Power of Attorney for Health Care", R.I. Gen. Laws 23-4.10-2 (Health Care Power of Attorney, chapter 23-4.10). The full form is printed in the statute: https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE23/23-4.10/23-4.10-2.htm . Not confirmed: whether a non-statutory form is valid; the statute says the document is valid only if the witnessing rules are followed. Not confirmed: a Department of Health page offering the form (health.ri.gov blocked automated access).
- **Signing:** one notary public, or two qualified witnesses present when the person signs or acknowledges the signature. Witnesses declare under penalty of perjury that they know the person, that the person appears of sound mind, and that they are not the agent or a health care provider. At least one witness, or the notary, must also declare they are not related by blood, marriage or adoption and not entitled to part of the estate. Any added pages must be dated and signed at the same time.
- **Who may not witness:** the agent or alternate agent; a health care provider or its employee; the operator of a community care facility or its employee.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the treating health care provider; a nonrelative employee of the treating provider; the operator of a community care facility; a nonrelative employee of such an operator.
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** Not confirmed: Rhode Island has no general default surrogate statute ranking spouse, children or parents. The MOLST law lets a "recognized healthcare decision maker" (someone authorized by law or by the patient) sign for a patient who lacks capacity (23-4.11-3.1).

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Declaration" under the Rights of the Terminally Ill Act, R.I. Gen. Laws 23-4.11-3; the model form is optional. Separate from the health care power of attorney. Signed by the person (or another at their direction) in front of two witnesses who are not related by blood or marriage. No notary.
- **Tube feeding:** the model form has a check box: the authorization "includes" or "does not include" the withholding or withdrawal of artificial feeding.
- **Pregnancy:** the declaration has no effect while the attending physician knows the patient is pregnant and it is probable the fetus could develop to live birth (23-4.11-6(c)).
- The statutory health care power of attorney form lets the agent stop life-sustaining treatment unless the person says otherwise, but not over the person's objection at the time.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "Medical Orders for Life Sustaining Treatment" (MOLST), R.I. Gen. Laws 23-4.11-3.1; rules at 216-RICR-20-15-4. Signed by a physician, registered nurse practitioner or physician assistant and by the patient, or by the recognized health care decision maker if the patient lacks capacity. Only the RIDOH-approved form may be used, unaltered. Providers may freely copy, store electronically, print or reproduce it. Valid in and outside hospitals. Clinicians supply it. MOLST can supplement a DNR or Comfort One bracelet.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "Comfort One" pre-hospital EMS DNR program, run by the RIDOH Center for Emergency Medical Services. The legal basis is the instructional bracelet law, R.I. Gen. Laws 23-4.11-14: at a physician's request, RIDOH issues a "DNR" bracelet showing the patient's name and address and the physician's name, address, license number and signature. It must not be removed; if tampered with or removed, EMS may resuscitate. The bracelet must be registered with the fire department of the patient's city or town.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: unclear. The Know My Health upload pilot (2019) no longer resolves.
- POLST registry: none for MOLST.
- EMS DNR registry: no state registry was found. The statute does require each Comfort One bracelet to be registered with the local fire department (23-4.11-14), which acts as a local record.

## Documents from other states
- A health care power of attorney made in another state under that state's law is valid (23-4.10-11). A declaration (living will) made in another state under that state's law is valid (23-4.11-12). Not confirmed: a RIDOH statement on out-of-state POLST forms or bracelets.

## Also in this state
- The statutory health care power of attorney includes an optional box asking the agent to tell family of the person's wish to be an organ and tissue donor.
