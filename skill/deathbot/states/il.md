# Illinois (IL)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Illinois Statutory Short Form Power of Attorney for Health Care", 755 ILCS 45/4-10 (Illinois Power of Attorney Act, Article IV). Other forms that meet Article IV are also valid. The Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH) posts the form in English and Spanish at https://dph.illinois.gov/topics-services/health-care-regulation/nursing-homes/advance-directives.html.
- **Signing:** one adult witness, at least 18, signs. No notary is required (755 ILCS 45/4-5.1).
- **Who cannot witness:** the person's attending physician, APRN, PA, dentist, podiatrist, optometrist, psychologist or other treating professional, or a relative of one of them. Also excluded: an owner or operator of the facility where the person is a patient or resident, a relative of that owner or operator, and the directors and executive officers of a corporate operator. Also excluded are a parent, sibling or descendant, or the spouse of one, of the person or of any agent or successor agent, by blood, marriage or adoption, and the agent or successor agent themselves (§ 4-5.1).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** IDPH says the person may choose anyone except their health care professional or other health care provider.
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** Health Care Surrogate Act, 755 ILCS 40/25. In order: a guardian of the person; the spouse (a civil union partner counts as a spouse, 750 ILCS 75/20); adult children; parents; adult siblings; adult grandchildren; a close friend; then the guardian of the estate. Public Act 104-0820 recently amended § 25, including the rules on disputes over priority. Not confirmed: the current wording of § 25(a), because ilga.gov could not be opened.

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Living Will Declaration", Illinois Living Will Act, 755 ILCS 35. It is a separate form from the health care power of attorney, also posted by IDPH. It needs two adult witnesses, and IDPH says the person's health care professional cannot be one. The statutory form's witness statement also says the witness does not inherit from the person and is not directly financially responsible for their medical care. Not confirmed: the exact text of 755 ILCS 35/3 (ilga.gov certificate error). It applies only to a terminal condition. Food and water cannot be withdrawn if that would be the only cause of death (IDPH summary). If a person has both documents, the agent decides unless unavailable.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** "IDPH Uniform Practitioner Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment (POLST)" form, under 20 ILCS 2310/2310-600 and 755 ILCS 40/65. The current form (POLST_220926) is posted in English and seven other languages on the IDPH advance directives page, and the public can download it.
- **Who signs:** an "attending health care practitioner", meaning an Illinois-licensed physician, APRN, PA, or a licensed resident who has finished one year of training. The patient or the patient's legal representative (guardian, health care agent or surrogate) also signs. Since Public Act 102-0140 took effect on Jan 1, 2022, no witness is needed. Consent can be given verbally and documented.
- **Copies:** "Digital copies and photocopies, including faxes, on ANY COLOR paper are legal and valid" (IDPH).

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- The IDPH POLST form is the state's out-of-hospital DNR form. Under 755 ILCS 40/65, providers may presume valid a POLST, or an out-of-hospital DNR order sanctioned by any U.S. state. Not confirmed: whether there is a state DNR bracelet or wallet card.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: unclear. The Secretary of State registry required by law (15 ILCS 305/34) is due by Jan 1, 2027. It will take POLST forms only, deposited by residents. It is not live yet.
- POLST registry: the same Secretary of State registry, not yet live.
- EMS DNR registry: none. A POLST or a state-sanctioned out-of-hospital DNR is presumed valid.

## Documents from other states
- Powers of attorney: 755 ILCS 45/2-10.6. A power of attorney signed outside Illinois is valid if it complied with the law where it was signed, Illinois law, or the law of the person's domicile at the time.
- POLST: IDPH tells providers to honor the National POLST Form and out-of-state forms (MOST, MOLST, POST, POLST) that a state or territory formally authorized.

## Also in this state
- "Declaration for Mental Health Treatment", Mental Health Treatment Preference Declaration Act (755 ILCS 43). Two witnesses sign. The person's health care professional, employees of the facility where the person lives, and relatives cannot witness. The mental health attorney-in-fact must accept in writing. The declaration expires three years after signing. IDPH posts the form.
