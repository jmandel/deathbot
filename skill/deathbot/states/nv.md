# Nevada (NV)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Power of Attorney for Health Care" (durable power of attorney for health care decisions), NRS 162A.700 to 162A.870. The statutory "Advance Health-Care Directive" form (NRS 162A.855, added 2023) combines naming an agent, health care instructions, optional special powers and organ donation. Using it is optional. The official text is in NRS chapter 162A on the Legislature's site.
- **Signing:** signed by the person, and the signature either acknowledged before a notary or witnessed by two adult witnesses (NRS 162A.790(2)). If the person lives in a nursing home, neither witness may be an owner, operator or employee of that nursing home (NRS 162A.790(3)). The statutory form also tells the person that the witnesses cannot be the agent.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the person's health care provider or its employee, or an operator or employee of a health care facility, unless that person is the spouse, legal guardian or next of kin (NRS 162A.840).
- **Agent limits:** an agent may not consent to mental-health commitment, convulsive treatment, psychosurgery, sterilization, abortion, aversive intervention, or experimental treatment or research (NRS 162A.850).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** Nevada has no general surrogate statute. For withholding or withdrawing life-sustaining treatment from a terminally ill patient with no declaration, written consent attested by two witnesses may come from, in order: spouse; majority of available adult children; parents; majority of available adult siblings; nearest other adult relative by blood or adoption. An evenly split class does not pass authority down (NRS 449A.454). The same order defines who may sign a POLST as surrogate (NRS 449A.551(7)).

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Declaration" under the Uniform Act on Rights of the Terminally Ill, NRS 449A.400 to 449A.481; forms at NRS 449A.436 (directing the physician) and NRS 449A.439 (naming someone to decide). Signed by the person (or another at their direction) and attested by two witnesses; the statute makes no notary option (NRS 449A.433). A properly executed health care power of attorney that covers life-sustaining treatment counts as a declaration (NRS 449A.433(3)). Part 2 of the 162A.855 form also holds treatment instructions.
- **Tube feeding:** under a declaration, artificial nutrition and hydration by the gastrointestinal tract is life-sustaining treatment and is withheld unless the person writes otherwise. With no declaration it is not withheld unless the representative or family decision-maker says so in writing (NRS 449A.451(3)).
- **Pregnancy:** treatment is not withheld while it is probable the fetus will develop to live birth (NRS 449A.451(4)).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "Provider Order for Life-Sustaining Treatment" (POLST), NRS 449A.500 to 449A.581; the State Board of Health prescribes the form (NRS 449A.548). Completed and signed by a physician, PA or APRN, and signed by the patient, or, if the patient lacks capacity, by the representative or surrogate (NRS 449A.551). It is honored in any setting, including at home and by EMS. Clinicians order forms from Nevada POLST (nevadapolst.org); patients and families may request forms free from the state health division. Printed copies are valid; hot-pink double-sided paper is recommended but not required.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- Nevada "Out-of-Hospital DNR" and do-not-resuscitate identification, NRS 450B.400 to 450B.590. A physician or APRN may issue a DNR order only to a patient in a terminal condition, and the patient must agree in writing (NRS 450B.510). The patient, or the physician or APRN on the patient's behalf, applies to the Division of Public and Behavioral Health on the DNR Application for Adult (or for Minor), signed by the patient or agent and the attending physician or APRN. The patient must certify they told close family. The application form goes by mail to the Division; the fee is $5 (state registry research). A bracelet or medallion may be issued in addition (NRS 450B.505). An adult in a terminal condition is a "qualified patient" if they have a declaration, a POLST that refuses CPR, or a DNR order (NRS 450B.470); the application must include the attending physician's or APRN's certification of the terminal condition (NRS 450B.520(2)). Clark County residents apply through the Southern Nevada Health District.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: Nevada Lockbox (Secretary of State). Active, free; the resident files a copy themselves.
- POLST registry: the Nevada Lockbox accepts POLST forms.
- EMS DNR registry: DNR identification is issued by application to the state health division ($5).

## Documents from other states
- A power of attorney made elsewhere is valid if its execution complied with that jurisdiction's law, or with the federal military power of attorney rules (NRS 162A.790(4)).
- A living will made in another state is valid if it complies with that state's law or Nevada's (NRS 449A.481).
- An out-of-state POLST is valid on the same test (NRS 449A.578). DNR identification issued under another state's law counts (NRS 450B.410(2)).

## Also in this state
- Advance directive for psychiatric care, NRS 449A.600 to 449A.645 (form at 449A.621). Two witnesses, who may not be the attending physician or provider or their employee, an owner or operator of the facility where the person lives, or the named attorney-in-fact. It lapses 2 years after signing unless revoked (NRS 449A.618).
- Special statutory health care power of attorney forms exist for an adult with an intellectual disability (NRS 162A.865) and for an adult with dementia (NRS 162A.870).
- The 162A.855 form has an organ donation part (Part 4).
