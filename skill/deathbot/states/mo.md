# Missouri (MO)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Durable Power of Attorney for Health Care", under the Durable Power of Attorney for Health Care Act (RSMo §§ 404.800–404.872). The agent is called the "attorney in fact." Missouri has no statutory form.
- **Signing:** the general durable power of attorney rules apply (RSMo § 404.810, applying § 404.705). The document must:
  - be titled "Durable Power of Attorney"
  - state in substance that the attorney in fact's authority does not end if the person becomes disabled or incapacitated
  - be signed, dated, and acknowledged by the person "in the manner prescribed by law for conveyances of real estate," which means before a notary. No witnesses are required.
- **Tube feeding:** to let the attorney in fact refuse artificially supplied nutrition and hydration, the document must grant that authority specifically (RSMo § 404.820). No attorney in fact may refuse food or water the person can take by mouth.
- **Who cannot be the agent** (RSMo § 404.815): the person's attending physician or that physician's employee, or an owner, operator or employee of a health care facility where the person lives. The exceptions are someone related within the second degree, or a member of the same religious community.
- **When it takes effect:** unless the document says otherwise, when two physicians certify after examination that the person is incapacitated (RSMo § 404.825).
- **Agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** Missouri has no general statutory surrogate consent list. Without a durable power of attorney for health care, families act by custom. Contested or end-of-life decisions can require a guardian.

## Written instructions (living will)
- **Form:** "Declaration", under RSMo §§ 459.010–459.055. A sample form is in § 459.015.3, and using it is optional.
- **Signing:** the declaration must be in writing, signed (or signed for the person at their direction) and dated. If it is not wholly in the person's handwriting, it must be signed before two witnesses aged 18 or older. A witness cannot be the person who signed for the declarant. The statute lists no other witness disqualifications.
- **Separate documents:** the declaration and the durable power of attorney for health care are separate documents under separate statutes. Many forms combine them, but the notary rule applies to the power of attorney part.
- **Limits:**
  - A declaration applies only to a terminal condition, with death expected "within a short time" (§§ 459.010, 459.025).
  - Missouri's definition of "death-prolonging procedure" excludes nutrition and hydration (§ 459.010(3)). A living will alone cannot refuse tube feeding; the power of attorney grant under § 404.820 is needed.
  - A declaration "shall have no effect during the course of the declarant's pregnancy" (§ 459.025).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** TPOPP (Transportable Physician Orders for Patient Preferences), Missouri and Kansas's POLST form. It is run by the Missouri State Medical Association at https://www.msma.org/TPOPP. No TPOPP statute was found.
- **Who signs:** a Missouri physician or "authorized practitioner" acting within scope of practice, and the patient or patient representative (or a minor's parent or guardian); both signatures are required. Stamped signatures are not accepted. Not confirmed: which license types beyond physicians count as authorized practitioners in Missouri.
- **Getting the form:** downloadable from MSMA for clinicians.
- **Copies:** the form says a copied, faxed or electronic version is valid, and the original does not need to be present in an emergency.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Form:** "Outside the Hospital Do-Not-Resuscitate Order" (the purple OHDNR form), under the Outside the Hospital Do-Not-Resuscitate Act (RSMo §§ 190.600–190.621). It must be on the Department of Health and Senior Services form.
- **Who signs:** the patient, or the patient's representative, and the attending physician (§§ 190.600(8), 190.603).
- **Identification:** a standardized DHSS identification card, bracelet or necklace (§ 190.600(7)).
- **EMS:** EMS may follow the outside the hospital DNR protocol when shown the order or the identification. EMS does not follow it if the patient or representative asks for resuscitation (§ 190.612).
- **Getting the form:** DHSS EMS forms page: https://health.mo.gov/providers/emergency-medical-services/applications-forms
- **TPOPP instead:** the MSMA TPOPP Clinical Guide says that, effective December 30, 2024, the Department of Health and Senior Services treats a validly executed TPOPP/POLST form as equivalent to the purple Outside the Hospital DNR form, under the same statutes (§§ 190.600–190.621) and immunity. Not confirmed: no DHSS rule or notice stating this was opened.

## Registries
- **Advance directive:** unclear. RSMo § 459.250 orders DHSS to contract for an online "Advance Health Care Directives Registry," with filing at a health care provider or attorney intake point for up to $10. No operating registry or vendor was found, so do not tell a person to file there.
- **POLST:** none.
- **EMS DNR:** none.

## Documents from other states
- Not confirmed: no Missouri statute was found that expressly validates health care powers of attorney, living wills or POLST forms made in another state.

## Also in this state
- **Comfort care:** an attorney in fact must consider comfort measures (§ 404.820.3).
- **Patient communication:** before artificial nutrition is withdrawn, the physician must try to explain this to the patient, or certify that the patient cannot understand (§ 404.820.4).
