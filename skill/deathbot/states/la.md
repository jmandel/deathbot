# Louisiana (LA)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** Louisiana has no separate statutory health care proxy form. A person names an agent in one of two ways:
  - a mandate (power of attorney) that expressly authorizes health care decisions. La. Civ. Code art. 2997(6) says authority to "make health care decisions, such as surgery, medical expenses, nursing home residency, and medication" must be given expressly. A general power of attorney without that wording is not enough.
  - a designation inside the living will "Declaration". La. R.S. 40:1151.2(C)(1) lets the Declaration name "another person to make the treatment decision" about life-sustaining procedures. The Secretary of State's form has no blank for this, so the person has to add a sentence naming the person.
- **Signing:** a mandate needs no particular form (La. Civ. Code art. 2993). A designation of someone to make the life-sustaining decision must be "signed by the patient in the presence of at least two witnesses" (La. R.S. 40:1151.4(A)(2)(b)). A witness must be a competent adult who is not related to the person by blood or marriage and who would not inherit any part of the person's estate (La. R.S. 40:1151.1(15)). Not confirmed: no statute requiring a notary or witnesses for a health care mandate was found.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the statute names no one.
- **Agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker** (Louisiana Medical Consent Law, La. R.S. 40:1159.4). This is the order for consent to medical treatment:
  1. A court-appointed tutor or curator.
  2. An agent under a mandate that specifically authorizes health care decisions.
  3. The spouse, unless judicially separated.
  4. An adult child.
  5. A parent.
  6. A sibling.
  7. Other ascendants or descendants.
  8. An "adult friend" who signs the facility's acknowledgment form.

  For withholding life-sustaining procedures from a certified qualified patient with no Declaration, La. R.S. 40:1151.4(A)(2) sets its own order: tutor or curator; a person the patient named in a writing signed before two witnesses; spouse; adult child; parents; sibling; other ascendants or descendants. Within the adult-child through other-relatives classes, a majority of the available class decides (40:1151.4(A)(3)). At least two witnesses must be present when anyone other than a tutor or curator makes that declaration (40:1151.4(B)).

## Written instructions (living will)
- **Form:** "Declaration" (called a "Living Will" Declaration), under La. R.S. 40:1151–1151.9. The illustrative form is in La. R.S. 40:1151.2(C)(1), and using it is optional. The Secretary of State publishes it as the "Living Will Declaration Form": https://www.sos.la.gov/our-office/end-of-life-registry-programs
- **Signing:** the person signs in the presence of two witnesses, who must meet the rules above. No notary is needed. An oral or nonverbal declaration before two witnesses is allowed only after a terminal and irreversible condition has been diagnosed.
- **When it applies:** two physicians must certify a terminal and irreversible condition or a continual profound coma. One of them must be the attending physician.
- **Tube feeding:** the person initials one choice. Either all life-sustaining procedures, including nutrition and hydration, are withheld so that food and water are "not administered invasively", or nutrition and hydration are kept.
- **Pregnancy:** La. R.S. 40:1151.9(E) says an ambiguity must be read to preserve the life of the unborn child once an obstetrician finds the pregnancy is at 20 or more weeks after fertilization and the woman's life can be maintained until a live birth.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** LaPOST (Louisiana Physician Order for Scope of Treatment), under La. R.S. 40:1155.1–1155.6, with rules at La. Admin. Code tit. 48, Part I, §§ 201–211. The Louisiana Health Care Quality Forum runs it: https://la-post.org
- **Who signs:** a physician, and also the patient or the patient's personal health care representative. Not confirmed: no provision letting nurse practitioners or physician assistants sign was found. The program says it "must be signed by a physician."
- **Who it is for:** patients with a life-limiting and irreversible condition.
- **Getting the form:** clinicians use the official form from la-post.org.
- **Copies:** the form says photocopies and faxes of a signed LaPOST are legal and valid.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- Louisiana has no separate EMS DNR form. Two things serve:
  - **LaPOST:** EMS follows its CPR section.
  - **Registry bracelet:** the Secretary of State sends an engraved "Do Not Resuscitate" bracelet and a wallet card when a Declaration is registered (the statute, 40:1151.2(D)(1)(b), speaks of bracelets for qualified patients in the registry). EMS practitioners must make a reasonable effort to look for the bracelet (40:1151.6(E)).

## Registries
- **Advance directive:** the Secretary of State's Living Will Declaration Registry. The person or their attorney mails the original, a multiple original or a certified copy. Registration is $20, which includes a wallet card and the DNR bracelet. A revocation costs $5.
- **POLST:** the LaPOST eRegistry was disabled in June 2024. No POLST registry is operating.
- **EMS DNR:** none apart from the Secretary of State's bracelet.

## Documents from other states
- A declaration properly executed in and under the laws of another state is valid in Louisiana (La. R.S. 40:1151.9(D)).
- Not confirmed: no statute or program statement on out-of-state POLST forms was found.

## Also in this state
- **Behavioral health directive:** the "advance directive for behavioral health treatment" (La. R.S. 28:221–237, statutory form at 28:236). It must be signed by the person and two competent witnesses. It must also come with a written psychiatric examination by a physician or psychologist confirming the person's capacity. Some people may not serve as witnesses (28:234).
- **Military directive:** a military advance medical directive under 10 U.S.C. 1044c is valid without Louisiana formalities (La. R.S. 40:1153.1–1153.5).
