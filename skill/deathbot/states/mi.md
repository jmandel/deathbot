# Michigan (MI)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Patient Advocate Designation", under the Estates and Protected Individuals Code (MCL 700.5506–700.5515). The person is the "patient" and the agent is the "patient advocate."
- **Statutory form:** none. Any writing that meets the statute works. It must state that the authority can be used only when the patient is unable to take part in medical (or mental health) treatment decisions (MCL 700.5506(3)).
- **Successor:** a successor patient advocate may be named (§ 5507(3)).
- **Signing:** the document must be written, signed, dated and executed voluntarily, in the presence of 2 witnesses who also sign (§ 5506(4)). There is no notary option for new designations. Michigan's remote-witnessing law (§ 1202) applied only from April 30, 2020 to July 1, 2021.
- **Who may not witness** (§ 5506(4)):
  - the patient's spouse, parent, child, grandchild or sibling
  - a presumptive heir or known devisee
  - the patient's physician
  - the patient advocate
  - an employee of the patient's life or health insurer
  - an employee of a health facility treating the patient
  - an employee of a home for the aged where the patient lives
  - an employee of a community mental health services program or hospital providing mental health services to the patient
- **Who cannot be the patient advocate:** the statute names no category. The advocate must be 18 or older.
- **Agent's acceptance:** required. Before acting, the patient advocate must sign an acceptance containing substantially the statements in MCL 700.5507(5). The acceptance can be signed later, but it must exist before the advocate acts. Before the designation is used, it must go into the medical record (§ 5506(3)) and a copy must be given to the patient advocate (and to a successor before the successor acts) (§ 5507(4)).
- **When it takes effect:** when the attending physician and another physician or a licensed psychologist examine the patient and put in writing that the patient cannot participate in medical treatment decisions (§ 5508(1)).
- **Default decision-maker:** Michigan has no general statutory surrogate consent list. The Michigan Dignified Death Act defines a "patient surrogate" as "a member of the immediate family, the next of kin, or the legal guardian" (MCL 333.5653(1)(g)), but sets no priority order. Without a patient advocate, families act by custom, and contested or major decisions can require a guardian.

## Written instructions (living will)
- Michigan has no living will statute. A patient advocate designation may include a statement of the patient's wishes on care and treatment (MCL 700.5507(1)).
- **Withholding treatment that allows death:** the patient advocate may make this decision only if the patient has said "in a clear and convincing manner" that the advocate may do so, and acknowledged that the decision could or would allow the patient's death (§ 5509(1)(e)). Without that sentence, the advocate cannot refuse life-sustaining treatment.
- **Pregnancy:** the designation cannot be used to withhold or withdraw treatment from a pregnant patient when that would result in her death (§§ 5509(1)(d), 5512(1)).
- **Patient's current wishes:** a patient's current wish to receive specific life-extending treatment binds the advocate, even if the patient is incompetent (§ 5511(1)).

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** MI-POST (Michigan Physician Orders for Scope of Treatment), under Part 56B of the Public Health Code (MCL 333.5671 et seq.).
  - The form is MDHHS-5836, with a patient and family information sheet, MDHHS-5837. A new version came out in June 2023.
  - It is for adults with an advanced illness or a condition that makes death within a year foreseeable (MCL 333.5674(2)).
- **Who signs:**
  - The "attending health professional": a physician, physician assistant or certified nurse practitioner (MCL 333.5672(4)).
  - The patient also signs. If the patient cannot, a patient advocate signs, or a guardian with court authority (MCL 333.5677). The patient or representative also signs the information form.
- **Review:** the form is reviewed at least yearly and on changes in condition or setting.
- **Getting the form:** clinicians get it from MDHHS.
- **Copies:** Not confirmed: no openable rule on photocopies or digital copies was found. The statute told the department to set those circumstances (MCL 333.5675(2)(e)).
- **EMS:** EMS must follow a POST form, with limited exceptions (MCL 333.5679(2)).

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Form:** the "Do-Not-Resuscitate Order" under the Michigan Do-Not-Resuscitate Procedure Act (MCL 333.1051–333.1067). Its required wording is in MCL 333.1054.
- **Who signs** (MCL 333.1053):
  - the person, or their patient advocate (or someone signing at the person's direction)
  - the attending physician
  - 2 adult witnesses, at least 1 of whom is not the person's spouse, parent, child, grandchild, sibling or presumptive heir
- **Bracelet:** an identification bracelet may be applied after signing. The person keeps the order accessible at home.
- **POLST:** EMS also honors MI-POST. A DNR order signed after a POST form overrides the POST form's CPR order (MCL 333.5679(2)(c)).

## Registries
- **Advance directive:** none operating. The Peace of Mind Registry is now discretionary under 2023 PA 189, and its website is defunct. Do not send people to mipeaceofmind.org.
- **POLST:** none.
- **EMS DNR:** none.

## Documents from other states
- Not confirmed: no Michigan statute was found that expressly validates health care powers of attorney or POLST forms executed in another state.

## Also in this state
- **Mental health:** a patient advocate designation can cover mental health treatment (§§ 5506, 5515).
  - Using that power requires written certification by a physician and a mental health practitioner.
  - The patient may waive the right to revoke the mental health powers. Treatment over the patient's objection is then capped at 30 days.
  - Forced medication or inpatient hospitalization needs clear and convincing authorization (§ 5509(1)(h)).
- **Anatomical gifts:** the designation may authorize the advocate to make an anatomical gift (§§ 5506(1), 5507(1)).
