# California (CA)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Advance Health Care Directive", Health Care Decisions Law, Probate Code § 4600 et seq. The optional statutory form is Probate Code § 4701. Part 1 is the "Power of Attorney for Health Care". No particular form is required; the Attorney General says "there is no required form" (https://oag.ca.gov/consumers/general/adv_hc_dir). Statute text: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (Probate Code §§ 4670-4701).
- **Signing:** Probate Code § 4673. The directive must be dated and signed by the person, or by another adult in the person's name, in their presence and at their direction. Then either a notary acknowledges it, or two adult witnesses sign. An electronically signed directive must be notarized (§ 4673(b)).
  - Witnesses may not be the agent, the patient's health care provider or the provider's employee, or the operator or an employee of a community care facility or residential care facility for the elderly.
  - At least one witness must not be related by blood, marriage or adoption, and must not be entitled to any part of the estate, and signs an extra declaration saying so (§ 4674).
- **Skilled nursing facility:** a directive signed by a skilled nursing facility patient is not effective unless a patient advocate or ombudsman also witnesses it. That person can be one of the two witnesses, or sign in addition to the notary (§ 4675).
- **Who cannot be the agent** (§ 4659): the supervising health care provider; an employee of the health care institution where the patient is receiving care; an operator or employee of a community care facility or residential care facility where the patient is receiving care. The employee bar does not apply to an employee (other than the supervising provider) who is related by blood, marriage or adoption, is a registered domestic partner, or works for the same institution or facility as the patient. A Lanterman-Petris-Short conservator may be named only with a lawyer's certificate (§ 4659(c)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** Probate Code §§ 4711-4712.
  - A patient can name a surrogate by personally telling the supervising provider. The designation lasts for the current illness or stay, or 60 days, whichever ends first. That surrogate ranks first, ahead of an agent and then a conservator.
  - If none of these exists, the provider or a facility designee may choose an adult who has shown special care and concern and knows the patient's values. The choice is from: the spouse or domestic partner, an adult child, a parent, an adult sibling, an adult grandchild, or an adult relative or close personal friend.
  - The statute lists these categories without stating a ranking (§ 4712(b), added by AB 2338, effective Jan 1, 2023).

## Written instructions (living will)
- Part 2, "Instructions for Health Care", is in the same § 4701 form, with the same signing rules. The person chooses "Choice Not to Prolong Life" or "Choice to Prolong Life", and gives instructions on relief from pain. The form can also be used for instructions only.
- California's statutory form has no pregnancy clause and no separate tube-feeding rule.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** California POLST, "Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment" (Probate Code §§ 4780-4786; form approved by the Emergency Medical Services Authority (EMSA)). SB 1088 (2026), signed by the Governor in September 2026, renames it "Portable Orders Listing Scope of Treatment" from Jan 1, 2027. Not confirmed: the chapter number on leginfo.
- **Who signs:** a physician, or a nurse practitioner or physician assistant acting under the supervision of the physician, and the patient or legally recognized decisionmaker (§ 4780(c)). A decisionmaker may sign only if the patient lacks capacity or the patient has made the decisionmaker's authority effective immediately under § 4682 (§ 4780(b)).
- **Where to get it:** clinicians complete it. The public can download it at https://capolst.org/polst-for-healthcare-providers/forms/.
- **Copies:** "Faxes and photocopies of the form are both as valid as the original." Scans, PDFs and EHR-completed versions are also valid. Pink paper is recommended but not required.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Form:** the EMSA "Prehospital Do Not Resuscitate (DNR) Form", a "request regarding resuscitative measures" under Probate Code § 4780(a). The patient with capacity or the legally recognized decisionmaker signs, and so does the patient's physician. EMS also honors a POLST.
- **Medallion:** allowed. It is engraved "Do Not Resuscitate" or "DNR", with a patient ID number and a 24-hour toll-free number, and is issued under an agreement with EMSA (§ 4780(e)).
- From Jan 1, 2027, SB 1088 lets a nurse practitioner or physician assistant sign the prehospital DNR form as well as a physician, and allows electronic signatures on POLST (per the sponsor's announcement).

## Registries
- **Advance directive registry:** the Advance Health Care Directive Registry run by the Secretary of State, which is active. Residents file their own directives with form SFL-461. The fee is $10 (not re-verified).
- **POLST registry:** a statewide California POLST eRegistry is in development, with no launch date. Regional registries run in Contra Costa and San Diego.
- **EMS DNR registry:** none. EMS honors the Prehospital DNR form, a POLST, or an approved medallion.

## Documents from other states
- Probate Code § 4676: a directive made in another state is valid in California if it complied with that state's law or California law. Providers may presume it is valid.
- From Jan 1, 2027, SB 1088 requires health care personnel to honor POLST-type forms and prehospital DNRs executed in other states.

## Also in this state
- Part 3 of the § 4701 form is "Donation of Organs, Tissues, and Parts at Death" (optional). Part 4 names a primary physician. Part 6 is the "Special Witness Requirement" for the patient advocate or ombudsman.
