# New Jersey (NJ)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Proxy Directive" (durable power of attorney for health care), which names a "health care representative", under the New Jersey Advance Directives for Health Care Act, N.J.S.A. 26:2H-53 to 26:2H-78 (26:2H-58). The statute sets no mandatory form. The Department of Health posts a Proxy Directive and an Instruction Directive developed by the New Jersey Commission on Legal and Ethical Problems in the Delivery of Health Care: https://www.nj.gov/health/advancedirective/documents/proxy_directive.pdf. One document may hold both (26:2H-55; 26:2H-58(b)).
- **Signing:** signed and dated (or signed at the person's direction) before two adult witnesses, who attest the person is of sound mind and free of duress and undue influence. Alternatively, the person acknowledges it before a notary public, an attorney at law or another person authorized to administer oaths, and then no witnesses are needed (26:2H-56). The named health care representative may not witness. The statute sets no other witness bars.
- **Who cannot be the agent:** an operator, administrator or employee of a health care institution where the person is a patient or resident, unless related by blood, marriage, domestic partnership, civil union or adoption. A physician there may serve only if not also the attending physician (26:2H-58(a)(2)). Naming a spouse, domestic partner or civil union partner is revoked by divorce, legal separation or termination, unless the directive says otherwise (26:2H-57(c)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** New Jersey has no general statute setting a family priority order for treatment decisions. The Act applies only where a directive exists (26:2H-74). Without a directive or guardian, physicians work with close family under case law and institutional policy. For a POLST, a "patient's representative" is anyone "otherwise authorized under law" to decide (26:2H-131).

## Written instructions (living will)
- "Instruction Directive" (living will), N.J.S.A. 26:2H-58(b) and 26:2H-64; DOH form at https://www.nj.gov/health/advancedirective/documents/instruction_directive.pdf. Same signing rules as the proxy directive. It may be signed with, attached to, or combined with a proxy directive. It may be supplemented by a video or audio recording (26:2H-56).
- **Pregnancy:** there is no default pregnancy rule. A woman may write in the directive what effect it should have if she is pregnant (26:2H-56), and should state any pregnancy limits on her representative (26:2H-58(a)(5)).
- Life-sustaining treatment may be withheld or withdrawn, consistent with the directive, if the person is permanently unconscious or terminally ill (each confirmed by a second physician), if treatment is futile or only prolongs dying, or if its burdens outweigh its benefits in a serious irreversible illness (26:2H-67). The Act treats tube feeding no differently from other treatment.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- "Practitioner Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment" (POLST), N.J.S.A. 26:2H-129 to 26:2H-140 (P.L.2011, c.145). The form is valid when the patient (or the patient's representative) signs voluntarily and the attending physician, APN or PA signs and dates it (26:2H-134). Health care professionals, institutions and emergency care providers must follow a completed POLST (26:2H-134(a)). The DOH posts fillable forms for anyone to download (the standard form is bright green; Spanish, Korean, Russian, Hindi and Vietnamese versions exist). The original is preferred; photocopies, faxes and electronic scans are valid. The form has an anatomical gift question. The Institute for Quality & Patient Safety (NJHA) is the lead agency.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- "New Jersey Out-of-Hospital DNR" form, a 1997 Medical Society of New Jersey protocol supported by the state Office of EMS. It is signed by the patient or surrogate and the attending physician, and legible photocopies are acceptable. An optional approved DNR bracelet is accepted by EMS as conclusive evidence. The DOH says existing Out-of-Hospital DNR forms remain valid and must be honored, but POLST is replacing them. NJHA advises that once a POLST is signed, the old DNR form can be destroyed. Not confirmed: a current N.J.A.C. rule citation for the form.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none. A separate psychiatric advance directive registry exists (DMHAS).
- POLST registry: emPOLST (NJHA). Active; clinicians at onboarded facilities submit forms.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- An advance directive made under another state's law is valid if it complies with that state's law or New Jersey's. A foreign-country directive is valid on the same test if it is not contrary to New Jersey public policy (26:2H-76).
- A POLST made in another state that meets New Jersey's POLST requirements is valid (26:2H-134(c)).

## Also in this state
- Advance directive for mental health care, N.J.S.A. 26:2H-102 to 26:2H-125 (separate act; names a "mental health care representative"). It needs at least one adult witness. The witness may not be the named representative or the treating mental health professional. A sole witness may not be a relative, a household member, an heir, or staff of a boarding home or residential facility where the person lives (26:2H-105). An out-of-state mental health directive is addressed in 26:2H-123.
