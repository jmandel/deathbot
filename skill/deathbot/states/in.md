# Indiana (IN)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Advance Directive for Health Care Decisions", Ind. Code ch. 16-36-7 (Senate Enrolled Act 204 of 2021). It names a "health care representative" and can include treatment preferences. There is no official or mandatory state form. The Indiana Department of Health (IDOH) points to two free Indiana Patient Preferences Coalition forms: "Representative Appointment Only" and "Preferences and Representative Appointment". The second is at https://www.indianapost.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Indiana-Advance-Directive-Preferences-and-Representative-Appointment.pdf.
- **Signing:** the person signs in front of two adult witnesses, or in front of a notary (IC 16-36-7-28(b)). At least one witness may not be the person's spouse or another relative. The signing may be on paper or electronic. It may also be done by live video with two witnesses or with a remote online notary under IC 33-42-17. If audiovisual contact is impractical, the person and the witnesses may use telephone contact, but never with a notary, and the document must say that phone contact was used (§ 28(e)-(g)). The coalition form has an "Initial here if the Witnesses participated by phone" line.
- **Who cannot witness:** anyone who signed the person's name at their direction. That signer also cannot be the notary or a health care representative named in the directive (§ 28(b)). A notary may never be used by telephone (§ 28(e)).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** the statute names no group. The person may disqualify named people from serving as representative or proxy (§ 28(a)(4)).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required.
- **Default decision-maker:** IC 16-36-7-42(c). In order: a court-appointed guardian, or a health care representative appointed under IC 16-36-1; the spouse; an adult child; a parent; an adult sibling; a grandparent; an adult grandchild; the nearest other adult relative; an adult friend who has kept regular contact and knows the person's health and beliefs; then a religious superior, for a member of a religious order. People at the same level must try to agree. If they cannot, the majority of those available controls (§ 42(f)).

## Written instructions (living will)
- Preferences go in the same Advance Directive (ch. 16-36-7). The coalition form has an optional statement: quality of life over length of life, or prolonging life as much as possible. The transition period ended Dec 31, 2022. Living will declarations, appointments of health care representative, and health care powers of attorney signed before then remain valid. New documents must use the ch. 16-36-7 advance directive.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** Indiana POST (Physician Orders for Scope of Treatment), IC 16-36-6, State Form 55317. It is for advanced chronic progressive illness, frailty or a terminal condition. Only the treating physician, APRN or PA may execute it; that clinician and the patient, representative or proxy each sign and date it, and it must be in English (IC 16-36-6-8). A patient who cannot sign may direct someone to sign in front of the clinician and the patient. Not confirmed: electronic signatures (reported in search excerpts, not in the § 6-8 text read).
- **Where to get it:** anyone can download it from https://forms.in.gov/Download.aspx?id=11217.
- **Copies:** copies are legal documents, to be honored like the original. Pink paper is recommended but not required.

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- **Form:** "Out of Hospital DNR Declaration and Order", IC 16-36-5, on the form in IC 16-36-5-15. The person, or a guardian, health care agent, health care representative or proxy, signs before two adult witnesses. A witness may not be the person's parent, spouse or child, may not have signed for the person, and may not be entitled to part of the estate or directly financially responsible for the person's medical care. Signing may be in person, by live video, or by telephone. The attending physician, APRN or PA certifies a terminal condition, or a condition in which resuscitation would fail or arrest would recur, and signs the order with a license number. EMS honors it, and a copy is enough.
- An identification device developed by the Emergency Medical Services Commission may be worn (IC 16-36-5-17). EMS also follows Sections A and B of a valid POST.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none.
- POLST registry: none.
- EMS DNR registry: none.

## Documents from other states
- An advance directive signed while the person lived or was outside Indiana is valid if it followed Indiana law or the law of the place of residence or signing (ch. 16-36-7). An out-of-state POST is effective in Indiana if it was prepared under that state's law, signed by the provider and the patient or representative, and is in English (IC 16-36-6).

## Also in this state
- Psychiatric advance directive, IC 16-36-1.7.
