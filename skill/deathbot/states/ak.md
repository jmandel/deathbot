# Alaska (AK)

Checked 2026-11-14.

## Naming someone to speak for you
- **Form:** "Advance Health Care Directive", Alaska Health Care Decisions Act, AS 13.52. Part 1 of the form is the durable power of attorney for health care, with an agent and alternates. The optional statutory form is AS 13.52.300. Using it is not required: any form that meets AS 13.52 is valid. The Alaska Legal Services Corporation booklet reproduces the statutory form, with state funding. It is dated January 2017, and the copy found was hosted by esign.com. The statute is at https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp (Title 13, Chapter 52).
- **Signing:** AS 13.52.010, as restated in item (15) of the statutory form. The person signs and dates the directive. Then either two adult witnesses sign, or it is acknowledged before a notary public in the state. Not confirmed: the official text of AS 13.52.010 (akleg.gov and statute mirrors blocked fetching); the rules below are from the statutory form.
  - The witnesses must know the person personally and be present when the person signs or acknowledges the signature.
  - No witness may be a health care provider employed at the institution or facility where the person receives care, an employee of the person's health care provider, an employee of that institution or facility, or the named agent.
  - At least one of the two witnesses may not be related by blood, marriage or adoption, or entitled to part of the estate under a will or codicil.
  - The form's witness statement is sworn under penalty of perjury (AS 11.56.200).
- **Who cannot be the agent:** an owner, operator or employee of a health care institution where the person is receiving care, unless related to the person (AS 13.52.010).
- **The agent's acceptance:** not required by the statutory form.
- **Default decision-maker:** AS 13.52.030 (from secondary summaries; official text not opened). First, an adult the patient names as surrogate by personally telling the supervising provider. Then, in order: the spouse (unless legally separated), an adult child, a parent, an adult sibling. If none of these is reasonably available, an adult who has shown special care and concern for the patient and knows the patient's values may act.

## Written instructions (living will)
- This is Part 2, "Instructions for Health Care", of the same AS 13.52 form, and it uses the same signing rules. The person picks either "Choice to Prolong Life" or "Choice Not to Prolong Life" for permanent unconsciousness or a terminal condition.
- **Tube feeding:** artificial nutrition and hydration is a separate choice: indefinitely; indefinitely unless it increases suffering; a limited trial; or none.
- **Pregnancy:** the form has a blank line, "Should I become unconscious and I am pregnant, I direct that ...". The person writes their own instruction there.
- A person found "not competent" by a court, by two physicians (one a psychiatrist), or by a physician and a mental health clinician cannot revoke the directive.

## Medical orders for the seriously ill (POLST-type)
- **Program:** Alaska POLST, "Provider Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment" (AS 13.52.065; 7 AAC 16.010-.090; form dated January 4, 2024 is adopted by reference). The current form is "Alaska POLST Form: A Portable Medical Order", V2 08/28/2023.
- **Who signs:** only an MD, DO, APRN or PA-C. Verbal orders are acceptable with a follow-up signature, and electronic signatures are valid. The patient or patient representative signature (Section E) is optional, but encouraged because other states may not honor the form without it.
- **Where to get it:** clinicians complete it. The public can download it at https://www.akpolst.org/forms.
- **Copies:** "A copied, faxed, or electronic version of this form is a legal and valid medical order. This form does not expire."

## Out-of-hospital do-not-resuscitate order
- POLST replaced the Comfort One program; Comfort One forms remain legal medical orders. Under 7 AAC 16.010(d), a health care provider, including EMS, may establish DNR status from any of these: a department-approved DNR form, card, or necklace or bracelet; the Alaska POLST form; a written or verbal DNR order from the attending physician, APRN or PA; or a DNR, POLST or MOLST from another state. If the patient cannot answer identity questions, the provider may rely solely on a department-approved DNR necklace or bracelet the patient is wearing (7 AAC 16.010(c)).
- Not confirmed: where a new department-approved bracelet can be ordered now that Comfort One is closed.

## Registries
- Advance directive registry: none found.
- POLST registry: none. Registry wording appears to have been removed from the rule in 2024.
- EMS DNR registry: none. POLST is replacing Comfort One, and Comfort One forms remain valid.

## Documents from other states
- DNR, POLST and MOLST orders from another state, territory or possession are recognized if they meet AS 13.52.150 (7 AAC 16.010(d)(6)). AS 13.52.150 says such an order is valid if it complies with Alaska law.
- Not confirmed: the rule for out-of-state advance directives. Secondary summaries say AS 13.52.010(k) treats them as valid only if they also comply with AS 13.52. The official statute text could not be opened.

## Also in this state
- **Mental health directive:** Part 4 of the statutory form covers mental health treatment, including consent or refusal of admission to a mental health facility, under AS 13.52.
- **Anatomical gift:** Part 3 of the statutory form is an anatomical gift at death.
- Part 5 of the form names a primary physician.
