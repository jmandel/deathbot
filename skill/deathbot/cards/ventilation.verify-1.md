1. **Correct decision-making authority.** Replace “cannot speak, so the family or proxy decides” with capacity-based wording; explain supported communication and authorized surrogacy. Source legal statements separately from ethics.

2. **Repair denominators and populations.** Label tracheostomy home discharge as conditional on hospital survival; identify nonsurgical patients. Clarify COPD’s original-cohort denominator, dementia’s different subgroups/time origins, and LTACH’s mixed ventilation population.

3. **Remove unsupported generalization and personal prediction.** The opening turns selected prolonged-ventilation outcomes into a broad forecast. Replace “people like you” with “people in this historical cohort”; explicitly prohibit treating cohort percentages as personal probabilities. ProVent requires the specified treatment day and clinician interpretation. Its two-cohort ranges need labels and CIs.

4. **Balance the paths.** All major choices appear, but recovery/rehabilitation and ongoing home ventilation receive much less practical detail than death/disability. No explicit treatment recommendation appears; the opening nevertheless slants pessimistically. Add realistic recovery possibilities and burdens without implying disability determines quality of life.

5. **Correct the declining-treatment narrative.** [NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/respiratory-failure/symptoms) lists variable symptoms depending on oxygen, carbon dioxide and cause; it does not establish the card’s inevitable sequence through confusion, unconsciousness and death. Describe variability. Add who provides comfort care, reassessment, and family workload.
