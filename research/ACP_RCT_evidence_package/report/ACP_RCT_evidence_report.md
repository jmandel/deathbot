# Advance Care Planning: Randomized Trial Evidence on Preference-Sensitive End-of-Life Care and Costs

Nov 13, 2026 · @Josh

## Bottom line

Randomized trials show that advance care planning (ACP) reliably increases documentation and conversations, but seldom changes the care people actually receive near the end of life. Of the 21 trials in the table below, 6 found less burdensome or more preference-concordant care on at least one outcome, 14 found no clear change, and 1 (SHARING Choices, 2025) found more burdensome end-of-life care.

The positive trials share a feature: preferences were turned into orders or care plans that the people making transfer and treatment decisions acted on, mostly in nursing homes (Molloy 2000, Hanson 2017, Martin 2019). Large pragmatic programs built on videos, forms or clinician prompts moved documentation and little downstream (PROVEN, ACP PEACE, Jumpstart).

On costs, one small cluster trial (Molloy 2000, 3 homes per arm) found lower total costs, and a secondary analysis of the Penn nudge trial found lower spending driven by less chemotherapy. Four trials found no difference in costs or resource use.

## Scope

This covers randomized trials of interventions meant to elicit, document or communicate patients' preferences ahead of time, and that measured the care patients later received.

- **Interventions included:** facilitated ACP conversations (for example Respecting Choices), advance directive programs, decision-aid videos, structured goals-of-care meetings, and clinician prompts or communication training aimed at goals-of-care talks.
- **Outcomes included:** any care a stated preference could change. That means hospital transfers and admissions, ED visits, ICU use, ventilation, CPR and DNR orders, feeding tubes, chemotherapy near death, hospice, place of death, care matching stated wishes, and costs.
- **Left out:** specialist palliative care trials, ICU family-meeting trials, and ACP trials that measured only documentation or engagement (for example the PREPARE website trials).
- **Designs:** individually randomized, cluster-randomized and stepped-wedge trials. One patient-preference randomized trial (Sinclair 2020) is flagged as such. Systematic reviews are summarized separately because several pool non-randomized studies.

This is a curated set, not a systematic search: the largest and most-cited trials, the 2025 pragmatic trials, and trials from the 2022 Malhotra review that reported care received. Citations were checked against PubMed and Europe PMC in November 2026.

## Cost evidence

Randomized evidence that ACP lowers costs rests on one small 2000 nursing-home trial and one secondary spending analysis. Four other trials found no difference, and no trial-based cost-effectiveness analysis exists.

| Study | What was measured | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Molloy 2000](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.283.11.1437) | Total health care cost per resident over 18 months | Can $3,490 vs $5,239 (lower); 3 homes per arm |
| [Patel 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/aioa2300228) (Penn nudge trial, secondary analysis) | Mean daily spending, last 6 months of life, 1,187 decedents | $378 vs $450 per day; adjusted $75 per day lower (about $13,700 per decedent), driven by systemic therapy and outpatient care; no difference in acute care or hospice spending |
| [Schneiderman 1992](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-117-7-599) | Charges, last month of life | $19,502 vs $19,700 (no difference) |
| [SUPPORT 1995](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.1995.03530200027032) | Hospital resource use | Adjusted ratio 1.05 (95% CI 0.99-1.12), no reduction |
| [Overbeek 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269216318801751) | Medical, residential and home-care costs over 12 months | Medical care EUR 2,360 vs 2,235 (no difference); program cost EUR 76 per participant |
| [Halpern 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.1742) | Inpatient care costs | No difference |

Reviews explain why the claim of savings persists. A [1999 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10540700/) found savings only in retrospective studies, and none in the three prospective ones. A [secondary SUPPORT analysis](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1532-5415.1997.tb05180.x) found that raising directive documentation did not lower resource use, and called the apparent savings an illusion. A [2015 economic review](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269216315586659) found no cost-effectiveness studies at all. It saw savings only for some groups, such as people with dementia, nursing-home residents and patients in high-spending regions. A [2016 review](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269216315601346) said robust trials were still needed.

## Systematic reviews

No meta-analysis pools randomized trials on ICU use, ventilation or hospital death. The pooled estimates that exist cover documentation, communication and quality of life, or they mix in non-randomized studies. The largest review of RCTs (Malhotra 2022) declined to pool at all because interventions and outcomes were too heterogeneous.

| Review | What it pooled | Finding on care received |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Malhotra 2022](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2021-060201), BMJ Open | 132 RCTs, 1992 to May 2021; vote count, no pooling | Lower health care use or costs in 4 of 22 trials; goal-concordant care in 3 of 12; more home deaths in 1 of 4; documentation improved in 34 of 54 |
| [Houben 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jamda.2014.01.008), JAMDA | Meta-analysis of 55 RCTs | Pooled gains in directive completion and end-of-life discussions; communication-based ACP improved concordance between preferences and care delivered |
| [Ng 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijnurstu.2022.104276), Int J Nurs Stud | Meta-analysis of 9 nursing-home RCTs, 2,905 residents | Documentation OR 1.95 (1.64-2.32); family satisfaction no difference; care received not pooled |
| [Schichtel 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-019-05482-w), J Gen Intern Med | Meta-analysis of 14 heart failure RCTs, 2,924 patients | Quality of life SMD 0.38, satisfaction 0.39, communication 0.29; care received not pooled |
| [Lin 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jamda.2025.105974), JAMDA | Dementia in nursing homes: 5 RCTs plus observational studies | RCTs too heterogeneous to pool; observational place-of-death pool not significant; very low certainty |
| [McMahan 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/jgs.16801), J Am Geriatr Soc | 69 RCTs, 2010 to 2020 (scoping review) | Utilization results mixed |
| [Pimsen 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jamda.2022.07.017), JAMDA | Nursing homes: 9 studies (RCTs, controlled trials, pre-post), 5 pooled | Hospitalization RR 0.54 (0.47-0.63); no effect on ED visits or hospice enrollment |
| [Khandelwal 2015](https://doi.org/10.1097/ccm.0000000000000852), Crit Care Med | 9 RCTs and 13 non-randomized trials of ACP or palliative care | ACP: ICU admission about 37% lower, from 3 studies |
| [Levoy 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpainsymman.2023.02.003), J Pain Symptom Manage | 20 studies in cancer (16 observational, 4 experimental), 33,541 patients | Lower odds of hospital death, ICU, admissions (OR 0.55, 6 studies) and late chemotherapy; communication-based ACP beat document-based ACP; the hospice benefit came from observational studies |
| [Weathers 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.maturitas.2016.06.016), Maturitas | RCTs in adults 65+ | Some reduction in hospitalization in long-term care; called for better economic studies |
| [Crooks 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/02692163251344428), Palliat Med | Meta-review of 39 reviews, 2015 to 2025 | 15 reviews reported less hospital use |

The most cited critique, [Morrison, Meier and Arnold 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2021.16430) in JAMA, read this record as showing ACP does not produce goal-concordant care. They urged a shift toward naming a surrogate and supporting decisions in the moment.

## Patterns across the trials

Where ACP changed care, it changed what clinicians were authorized or prompted to do at the moment a decision was made.

- **Orders beat conversations.** Molloy's directives were signed by a physician and travelled with the resident to hospital. Hanson paired the video with a care-plan meeting. Martin tested medical treatment orders directly against ACP alone, and the orders cut admissions. EVINCE and PROVEN offered video alone and changed nothing.
- **Reach is low at scale.** In PROVEN, 21.9% of residents with advanced illness saw a video. In SHARING Choices, 1,181 of 22,949 intervention patients (about 5%) had a facilitated conversation. Documentation can rise while effects on care are diluted to nothing.
- **Written preferences are soft.** In Halpern 2020, the default wording on a form shifted what patients chose, yet their care did not change. Choices that bend to wording are a weak lever on later treatment.
- **Pushing paperwork may backfire.** SHARING Choices raised burdensome end-of-life care. Its authors suspect the push for forms crowded out deeper, ongoing conversations, possibly amplified by COVID-19. It is one trial, with code-based outcomes.
- **In cancer, the clearest signal is less chemotherapy near death.** The Penn nudge trial cut late systemic therapy but not ICU use, hospital death or hospice.
- **Decedent samples are small.** Several trials judged end-of-life care on few deaths: 54 in Sinclair, 56 in Detering, 64 in the Serious Illness Care Program, 89 in Malhotra. Null results there are weak evidence either way.
- **Design drives the answer.** Retrospective and observational studies show savings and less intensive care. Prospective trials mostly do not, likely because people who complete directives differ from those who do not.

## Are the trials just underpowered?

Some are, but not the biggest ones. The large null trials were precise enough to rule out sizable effects of the programs as delivered. Their problem is dilution: they barely changed how many patients actually had a planning conversation, so they test the program, not the conversation.

| Trial | Size | How much ACP actually differed between arms | Precision on care received | What it can and cannot rule out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SUPPORT 1995 | 4,804 patients | CPR discussed 40% vs 37% | ICU, ventilation or coma before death: ratio 0.97 (0.87-1.07) | Rules out more than a 13% reduction. The intervention barely changed communication either |
| PROVEN 2020 | 12,479 residents | 21.9% of intervention residents saw a video | Transfers: -0.2 (-0.5 to 0.2) per 1,000 person-days, on a base of 3.9 | Rules out more than a 13% facility-wide drop. A cut of roughly half among viewers would still fit |
| Jumpstart 2023 | 2,512 patients | Goals-of-care talks 34.5% vs 30.4% (4.1 points) | ICU within 30 days: -1.0 points (-4.4 to 2.5) | Even if every extra talk had prevented all ICU use, ICU would fall only about 1 point. The trial could not see that |
| Halpern 2020 | 492 patients | Comfort care chosen 34% vs 22% | Hospital-free days: rate ratio 1.05 (0.90-1.23) | Rules out large shifts in days out of hospital |
| SHARING Choices 2025 | 64,915 patients; 1,498 decedents | About 5% had a facilitated talk; new documentation 12.0% vs 6.6% | Burdensome care: aOR 1.40 (1.08-1.81) | Large enough to detect a signal, in the harmful direction |

The small trials are a different problem. The Serious Illness Care Program had 64 evaluable patients, Detering 56 decedents, Sinclair 54, Malhotra 89, and Johnson 116 pairs with complete data. Those trials can only detect very large effects, so their nulls say little.

No one has pooled the randomized trials on hospital transfers, ICU or hospital death (see Systematic reviews above). The nursing-home trials (Molloy, Hanson, Martin, PROVEN, EVINCE) are the most plausible set to pool, but their interventions and outcome definitions differ widely.

## Open questions

- Do portable medical orders (POLST or MOST style) cut unwanted transfers outside nursing homes? Only Martin 2019 tested orders against ACP alone, in 6 facilities.
- Can goal-concordant care be measured well enough to be a primary outcome? The Serious Illness Care Program had 64 evaluable patients, and Overbeek could not assess concordance because goals were rarely recorded.
- Does the SHARING Choices signal of more burdensome care replicate in another health system?
- What is ACP's cost-effectiveness once facilitator time is counted? No trial has reported one; Overbeek priced the program at EUR 76 per participant.
