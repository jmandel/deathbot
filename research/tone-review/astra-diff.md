The revision addresses most v3 failures directly and removes several instructions that encouraged them. I would keep the structure. The remaining issues are mostly narrow ambiguities, plus two protections worth restoring.

1. Would it prevent the v3 failures?

| Treating “Have I got that right?” as sufficient understanding | Strong improvement: now explores outcomes, timeframes and uncertainty; confirmation buttons must allow correction. |

These are predictions about the instructions, not evidence that a model now follows them. Several v3 failures already violated the old text.

2. Protections removed or weakened

Equal scrutiny, sourced numbers, uncertainty, the emergency paths and document fidelity remain. Proxy preparation is substantially stronger overall.

Two deletions deserve restoration:

- The explicit instruction to ask before offering information the person has not requested. The remaining permission checks mainly concern prognosis, pictures and the “go gently” preference.
- Checking whether the proxy can honor the person’s wishes under pressure, including when the proxy personally disagrees. Willingness, understanding and permitted leeway do not fully cover that.

The trial paragraph should also retain an agreed point to reassess. Removing a mechanical stopping rule should not remove the trial’s structure.

3. New ambiguities and residual problems

- “Do not supply … phrases of your own” and “adds nothing of your own” can prohibit faithful paraphrase, explained terminology and useful organization. The intended restriction is on added meaning.
- “Anything that came up but was not fully explored belongs under ‘What I haven't decided’” confuses an expressed preference needing clarification with no preference.
- “Always include an option” when checking a reflection could make buttons mandatory again. It should apply only when buttons are used.
- “Never record a treatment preference” during the passive-wish turn conflicts with keeping accurate working notes. Preserve what was said as unconfirmed; do not announce or finalize it.
- The rigid second-turn safety question could repeat an explicit denial or delay responding to disclosed danger. Splitting the questions is right; requiring the sequence regardless of the answer is not.
- The SKILL table still says “Offer two or three options in a sentence” whenever buttons are unavailable. That fallback should inherit the new restrictions.
- Some scripts remain: the image guide’s quoted “Before” question and several “Right” responses in failure-cases.md. Those are residual examples, not new regressions. Failure case 5 even says “from the card,” contrary to voice.md.
- There is no substantial new word blacklist. “Neat two-branch certainty” is reviewer jargon; “how the conversation is sitting with them” and “one manageable piece” are repeated enough to become another house style.

4. Exact edits I would make

I would make these five small groups of changes.

**Permission and proxy capability — conversation.md**

Insert before “Answer what they ask honestly”:

> Before introducing medical information they have not asked for, find out whether they want it now.

Replace the first proxy exploration bullet with:

> Whether that person knows they have been chosen, is willing to do it, and could follow their wishes even when they personally disagree or others object.

**Time-limited trials — replace that paragraph in conversation.md**

> When a time-limited trial fits, explain that it includes an agreed point to reassess whether treatment is helping. Explore what recovery would need to look like for them, what burdens they would accept while waiting, and how much judgement their proxy should have if the outcome remains uncertain. Do not supply a default duration or imply that reassessment automatically means stopping. Probe accepting and refusing with the same care.

**Fidelity — replace the prohibition on “phrases of your own” in both conversation.md and summary.md**

> You may organize and faithfully paraphrase what they said. Do not add wishes, motives, family judgements or emotional reassurances they did not express.

Replace the template’s “anything that came up but was not fully explored” clause with:

> Distinguish what they have said they want from what still needs clarification; do not present either as more settled than it is.

Replace “check that it adds nothing of your own” with:

> Check that the draft adds no meaning they did not express or specifically confirm.

**Safety — replace the new introductory paragraph under “A passive wish to be dead”**

> This is common in older and seriously ill people and is not a crisis by default. Do not reach for a hotline or move on to planning. Preserve any treatment preference in working notes as needing confirmation, without announcing it as settled. Ask about what is making life hard, listen, then ask the direct safety question in a separate turn if they have not already answered it. If they disclose intent, a plan or an attempt, follow the relevant section immediately.

**Buttons and numerical clarity**

Replace the reflection-button bullet with:

> **Allow correction.** When you use buttons to check a reflection, include a way to qualify or correct it alongside confirming it.

Replace the SKILL table’s fallback for ready-made replies with:

> Offer short spoken options only when the same guidance permits buttons.

Add to information.md’s “Numbers” section:

> Make clear who each number counts and over what period. If the denominator changes, say so.
