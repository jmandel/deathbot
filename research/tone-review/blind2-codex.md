Three behavioral groups are visible; these are not claims about hidden prompts or models:

- **C03, C04, C08, C09:** no buttons, relatively continuous conversational threads; recurring premature clarity, stopping-boundary questions, and administrative endings. Frank's suffering receives little practical follow-up.
- **C01, C05, C10, C11, C13, C15, C16:** more selective buttons around information preferences and review, with some residual menus; shorter draft turns are emerging, but permission and neutrality remain inconsistent.
- **C02, C06, C07, C12, C14:** expansive explanations and family scenarios, often stronger exploration of rehab, discretion, or unresolved wishes; the cost is long turns and overconfident interpretation. C12 is the strongest exception to this group's statistical overload.

The three most important remaining problems:

1. **Understanding is not checked equally before preferences become plans.** C04 accepts refusal after Frank assumes CPR means a nursing home; C10 leaves “18 people who got to go back to their families” uncorrected. For Rosa, a request to make treatment happen too often becomes a search for stopping criteria: C12 says starting treatment “is not the hard call for Ana,” assuming away Rosa's actual worry. C02/C07/C13 improve scrutiny of refusal; C12/C14 improve permission to remain undecided. Apply both improvements consistently, without rewarding a chosen direction.

2. **Permission is not pacing, and populations are not interchangeable.** C06/C07 introduce BiPAP survival “by comparison” with intubation, prompting a false head-to-head inference; later correction is valuable but preventable. C16 supplies figures after Rosa selects the explanation-only option. Use one relevant outcome, preserve denominator and time horizon, then check its meaning. Repeated “How does hearing that sit with you?” cannot substitute for this. C11/C15 show that draft delivery can remain brief; C02/C14 still burden review with logistics.

3. **Confident empathy becomes invented certainty.** C05's “Maria Garza” is concrete fabrication; C03 promises family conflict prevention; C14 guarantees trial safety; C16 promises comfort and protection from second-guessing. Repeated “I have no stake” declarations do not repair these claims. Describe what preparation can help achieve, ask what relatives actually think, and preserve uncertainty. Safety follow-up is improving, especially C06's direct question and practical support, but should not become another reassurance-and-resource recital before returning to the agenda.
