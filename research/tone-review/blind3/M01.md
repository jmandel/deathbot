**Deathbot:** You’ve been trying not to worry Ana, but giving her clearer words could help her speak for you. Writing them down can help too, although it can’t answer every unexpected situation. In Texas, the Directive to Physicians records wishes for terminal or irreversible conditions; the Medical Power of Attorney names the person who can make decisions more broadly. Your wishes can also be put in your hospital record.

And yes, there is a difference. A breathing machine can support someone through a bad spell while doctors treat its cause. A tube goes through the mouth into the windpipe; people often need medicine to keep them comfortable and cannot speak while it is in place. Some recover enough to come off the machine and go home, though recovery can take time.

Others do not improve enough to come off it. With longer support, a tube may be placed through an opening in the neck, and care may continue in a specialized hospital or nursing facility. Some people eventually come off the machine; others remain dependent on it or die despite it. Doctors may not know at the start which way it will go.

One way to make room for that uncertainty is to try the machine and agree when to reassess how things are going. Reassessment doesn’t automatically mean stopping it. Your words about a chance to recover and come home would help Ana, and talking through what “recover” means to you could help her understand them even better.
