**Deathbot:** When the heart stops, CPR means pushing hard and fast on the chest to keep blood moving, giving medicines, using electric shocks if the heart rhythm allows, and often placing a breathing tube. Because the compressions have to be forceful, a 2024 review of 74 studies found they break ribs in a little over half of people who receive them.

In a 2025 US registry of adults whose heart stopped outside the hospital, about 10 out of 100 survived to leave the hospital. When a heart stops inside the hospital, where a team is already nearby, a 2014 review of 29 studies found that among people in their 80s, about 15 out of 100 survived to leave the hospital. Among Medicare patients 65 and older (2000–2008) who did survive in-hospital CPR, about 40 out of 100 went straight home, while more than half went to a rehab or nursing facility first.

**Deathbot:** For fluid on the lungs from heart failure, doctors can often try a tight-fitting breathing mask first while medicines pull the fluid off. You stay awake with the mask, and a 2019 review of studies suggests it may lower hospital deaths from heart-failure fluid, though some people find the tight mask hard to tolerate.

If the mask is not enough, a breathing machine pushes air through a plastic tube placed into your windpipe. While the tube is in, you cannot talk or eat, staff have to suction mucus from the tube, and many people need sedatives or get temporary, frightening confusion. Once the fluid clears, some people come off the tube in a few days.

When it comes to how older adults do, a 2015 Canadian study of 610 patients 80 and older in the ICU found that about 44 out of 100 died within a year, and about 26 out of 100 were alive and back to their usual physical ability a year later. If someone cannot come off the breathing machine after about two weeks, doctors often discuss placing a shorter tube in the front of the neck (a tracheostomy). Across 124 studies of prolonged ventilation (over 14 days), nearly 60 out of 100 died within a year, and only about 19 out of 100 of those who left the hospital went straight home.
