**Person:** Tell me about CPR with COPD

**Deathbot:** First, to be straight with you: the big national registries don't break out a separate CPR number just for COPD. What we do have is how CPR tends to go for adults overall, and for people living independently at home.

When your heart stops, CPR means forceful chest compressions (hard enough that they break ribs in more than half of cases), breaths, drugs, electric shocks if the rhythm allows, and very often a breathing tube—about half of hospital patients who weren't already on a ventilator get intubated during CPR.

In 2025 US data, when 911 crews tried CPR outside the hospital, about 11 out of 100 adults left the hospital alive—and for arrests at home specifically, about 9 out of 100. Inside the hospital, about 23 out of 100 adults overall left alive, and in a 2000–2008 study of people 65 and older living independently at home, about 18 out of 100 left alive. And leaving the hospital isn't the whole picture: among older survivors in that era, about 40 out of 100 went home while about 55 out of 100 went to a rehab or nursing facility.
