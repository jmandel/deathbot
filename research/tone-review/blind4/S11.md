In a 2008 Medicare study of nearly 4,800 people on home oxygen who were put on a breathing machine for a COPD flare (average age 74), about 1 in 4 died in the hospital, and about 3 in 10 were alive a year after discharge. When ventilation lasts more than two weeks, a review of 39 studies found about 6 in 10 died within a year, and among those who left the hospital alive, only about 1 in 5 went straight home.

**Deathbot:** Glad you said that—and hauling an oxygen hose around a quiet house really does wear on you. In US registries, when 911 crews do CPR for an arrest at home, about 9 in 100 adults leave the hospital alive. **Deathbot:** In New York, it takes two pieces of paper to lock that in—one you fill out yourself, and one your doctor signs with you.

First is a New York Health Care Proxy (form DOH-1430, at https://www.health.ny.gov/publications/1430.pdf). That's what legally names Lisa as your agent so Mike can't overrule her. You sign and date it in front of two adult witnesses (not Lisa), and they sign too—no notary needed in New York.

Second is a MOLST—Medical Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment (form DOH-5003, at https://www.health.ny.gov/forms/doh-5003.pdf). A Health Care Proxy alone won't stop an ambulance crew from doing CPR if your heart stops at home. A MOLST is an actual medical order that your doctor, nurse practitioner, or PA signs with you, and it's the form that tells both EMS and the hospital: no CPR, and only a short trial on a breathing machine.
