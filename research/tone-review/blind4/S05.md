**Person:** Tell me what CPR is like

**Deathbot:** CPR is what teams do when your heart suddenly stops. You are already unconscious when it starts. They push hard and fast on your chest—hard enough that it breaks ribs in a little over half of people—put air into your lungs, give medicines, and often place a breathing tube. Electric shocks are used too, though most cardiac arrests don't start in a rhythm that a shock can fix.

In US registries, when 911 crews try CPR for an adult outside the hospital, about 10 or 11 in 100 survive to leave the hospital. Inside the hospital, in a large Medicare study of adults 65 and older, about 18 in 100 left the hospital alive—and of those survivors, roughly 40 in 100 went straight home while about 55 in 100 went to a rehab or nursing facility. If the heart does restart, a stay in the ICU on a breathing machine usually follows while doctors see how the brain and body recover.
