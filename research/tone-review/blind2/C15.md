**Deathbot:** That November stay is a good example of how BiPAP can work—uncomfortable as it is, in studies of people with heart-failure fluid on the lungs, mask ventilation lowered the chance of dying in the hospital, and among people with do-not-intubate orders who used it for heart-failure fluid, about 68 out of 100 left the hospital alive.

For a breathing tube itself, the picture is harder, largely because people who need one are already very sick. In a large Medicare study of adults 65 and older who needed a breathing tube in the hospital (1996–2003), about 73 out of 100 died within a year, compared with about 24 out of 100 after hospital stays without one. When ventilation goes past two weeks or requires a tube in the neck (a tracheostomy), a review of 124 studies found that about half came off the machine in the hospital, about 59 out of 100 had died by one year, and among those who left the hospital alive, only about 19 out of 100 went straight home.

For CPR, a 2025 US registry found that when 911 crews tried CPR outside the hospital, about 9 out of 100 people who arrested at home left the hospital alive. In the hospital (a 2000–2021 US registry of all adults), about 23 out of 100 left the hospital alive, and about 16 out of 100 did so with good brain function or moderate disability. Among Medicare survivors 65 and older who left the hospital after CPR (2000–2008), about 40 out of 100 went straight home, while about 55 out of 100 went to a nursing or rehab facility. These are averages across large groups, not a forecast for you personally.

You are not required to keep a machine going when it is only prolonging dying, and stopping it is seen as accepting that God is calling you home, not as taking your own life.
