Those numbers are from 2008 and from a big group, not a prediction for you—especially since you're still driving and active on your own. Still, coming off a ventilator can mean a long haul, sometimes a tube in the neck or a stay in a rehab facility afterwards.

**Person:** Tell me about CPR

**Deathbot:** When your heart stops, CPR is an all-out emergency attempt to restart it: hard chest compressions (strong enough to break ribs in about half of people), electric shocks if the rhythm allows, drugs, and very often a breathing tube. If your heart restarts, you go straight to the ICU on a ventilator.

The odds are much lower than what you see on TV. In 2025 US data for adults who had a cardiac arrest at home, about 9 in 100 survived to leave the hospital. For arrests inside a hospital among people in their 70s (in studies up to 2012), about 19 in 100 left the hospital alive—and in a study of survivors 65 and older, about 4 in 10 went home while more than half went to a nursing or rehab facility.

Those are group averages, not a prediction for you, and they don't break out COPD specifically. What's worth knowing is that without a medical order saying otherwise, 911 crews and hospitals attempt CPR automatically.
