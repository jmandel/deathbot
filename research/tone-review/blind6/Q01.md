**Person:** Tell me what CPR is really like

**Deathbot:** When your heart stops, you're unconscious right away. CPR is forceful chest compressions—which break ribs in a little over half of people—along with drugs, electric shocks if the rhythm allows, and usually a breathing tube down your windpipe. If your heart restarts, you're in the ICU on life support while they try to fix what caused it.

In a 2025 US registry of adults who got CPR from 911 crews outside the hospital, about 10 or 11 in 100 left the hospital alive, and about 8 in 100 with good or moderate brain function. Inside the hospital, across studies of people in their 70s, about 19 in 100 left alive—and in a study of 65+ survivors who made it out, about 40 in 100 went straight home while about 55 in 100 went to a nursing or rehab facility. Being independent at home puts you in a stronger spot than someone already frail, though a breathing tube and ICU stay almost always follow if your heart restarts.
