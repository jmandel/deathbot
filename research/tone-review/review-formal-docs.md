# Review: "Formal documents", wrap-up sentence, "The path" (SKILL.md, What you produce)

Checked 2026-11-14 against skill/deathbot/states/*.md, cards/documents.md, and primary sources where noted.

## A. Legal accuracy

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Formal documents make wishes count" | Overstated. In several states an unwitnessed written or even oral "individual instruction" is valid (de.md § 2502(14); hi.md § 327E-3(a); ms.md § 41-41-205; wy.md § 35-22-403). Elsewhere, known wishes still bind the agent (ny.md: PHL 2982(2), "reasonably known" wishes on tube feeding; O'Connor accepts a written statement as evidence). The same goes for the earlier line "It carries no legal force": it is not a form or an order, but it can be evidence of wishes. |
| Proxy and living will are "for everyone" | True as a recommendation; National POLST says an advance directive is the preferred tool for healthy people (polst.org/for-patients/about-polst-form). |
| "the state's health care proxy form and living will" | Varies. Most statutory forms are optional ("may, but need not": NY, FL, VA, CT, IA, AK); AR, CO, MO, SD, VT and WA have no required form. AL, TX, SC, NC and NH require substantially the statutory form. NY, MA and MI have no living will statute. Defer to the state file. |
| "which the person signs" | True everywhere, with variations (someone may sign at their direction, e.g. NY PHL 2981). Witness/notary rules vary from none (CO, ID, NM) to witnesses plus notary (NC, WV) or notary only (MO). The skill already says to look these up; fine. |
| POLST for the seriously ill or frail, clinician signs after a conversation | True as stated by National POLST. Incomplete: the patient or surrogate also signs in most states (cards/documents.md [8][10]; NY, NH, SD). Eligibility varies: SD MOST and the NV and SC out-of-hospital DNRs are for terminal conditions only. OH, TX and WI have no POLST program. |
| POLST **and** an out-of-hospital DNR | Wrong as a general rule. In WA, OR, UT, TN, ND, MN, MD, DC and LA the POLST-type form *is* the out-of-hospital DNR. In AZ, EMS law rests on the orange Prehospital Directive, and POLST's force is not confirmed (az.md). In IN, TX, CO and AZ the patient also signs the out-of-hospital DNR. Recommend "the state's portable medical order(s)" and defer. |
| "Without the proxy form, nobody they chose legally speaks for them" | Broadly true for future incapacity. Narrow exceptions: oral designation to the treating clinician for the current illness or stay (Cal. Prob. Code § 4711; hi.md § 327E-5; Vermont lets the person name a surrogate by telling their clinician, for DNR/COLST consent only). Any valid document counts, not just the state form (a lawyer's durable POA, LA mandate). Without one, a default-surrogate statute picks someone, where the state has one. |
| "without portable orders, nothing on paper changes what an ambulance crew or emergency department does" | EMS half is true in most states (National POLST: responders cannot use an advance directive; NH RSA 137-J:25-27; VA). Exceptions: Ohio accepts an operative living will refusing CPR as DNR identification (OAC 3701-62-04(A)(2), verified); a registered Louisiana declaration brings a DNR bracelet EMS must look for (40:1151.6(E)). ED half is wrong: hospital staff can and do act on an advance directive and a reachable agent. |
| Astra: "record decision-making authority and treatment instructions under the applicable rules" | Accurate but blurs the key distinction: a POLST/DNR is a medical *order*, not a "treatment instruction" by the person. |
| Portability (not in text) | Advance directives: many states accept out-of-state documents (NY PHL 2990, FL 765.112, MA 201D § 11, PA), but there is no such statute in MI and MO; CaringInfo says it is "not always". POLST: "most likely" honoured (National POLST); AL refuses out-of-state forms. Defer; never promise. |

## B. Behaviour

- In v4 the slogan surfaced word for word ("Two New York forms make this count legally") in the "What matters to me" turn, which turned it into a form briefing on top of the draft review. The briefing was accurate for NY. The actual closing (turn 18) was short and acceptable: one "still open" sentence. The risk is real but appeared mid-conversation, not at the goodbye.
- "Even if the person is tired" licenses overriding a stop request. Combined with "Say whichever applies", it reads as a mandatory recital. A tired person needs one line plus the written list, not a re-explanation.
- Astra's wrap-up text loses the one fact a person can be hurt by not knowing: the summary and an advance directive will not stop EMS from attempting CPR at home. It also drops the trigger for when a portable order is relevant. Astra's "Using the document" drops "who keeps the original", which matters for POLST (the original stays with the person where crews find it) and for revocation. It does keep completion, sharing, access and updating.

## Recommended text

**1. Formal documents** (replace the paragraph through "...emergency department does."):

> **Formal documents** are what institutions act on. For any adult: an advance directive, which names a health care agent and can give instructions; the person signs it under their state's rules. For someone seriously ill or frail (home oxygen, repeated hospital stays, advanced cancer, dialysis, advanced heart failure, a nursing home, or a doctor who would not be surprised if they died within a year or two): the state's portable medical order (POLST; the name, eligibility and whether a separate out-of-hospital DNR exists vary by state), which a clinician signs after talking with them. Without a signed agent designation, state law decides who speaks for them. In most states only a portable medical order changes what an ambulance crew does at home.

- Slogan replaced: wishes can count without forms, and some states accept informal instructions.
- "State form" replaced with "under their state's rules": most forms are optional, and some states have no living will.
- POLST and out-of-hospital DNR folded together: in many states they are one document, and eligibility varies.
- "Emergency department" dropped: EDs act on directives and agents.
- "In most states" added: OH and LA exceptions.

**2. Wrap-up sentence:**

> Before they finish, say in a sentence which of these applies to them and is not yet done, and put the details on the still-open list rather than explaining them again.

- Keeps the EMS/proxy point reaching the person (Astra's version can lose it) and removes "even if tired", which pushed the model past a stop request.

The following sentence ("Use `guide/forms.md`... `guide/information.md`...") should also say *read the person's state file* (states/INDEX.md requires this), and its parenthetical should read "(signing, witnesses or notary, who else must sign)".

**3. The path** (keep the substance, trim):

> **The path.** When a document is ready, give the person written steps they carry out themselves: how it reaches each person who needs it (printed, sent from their phone, brought to an appointment, uploaded to their patient portal; the agent gets it and talks it through with them); for a formal document, how to complete it under their state's signing rules and who keeps the original; where copies live so they can be found in an emergency; when to look at it again, and that old copies are replaced when it changes. In conversation, cover only the next step they want help with. Write it in the language of the people who will read it, and offer a second copy if they differ. Use what your environment offers for printing and sending, and promise nothing it cannot do. Anything not yet done goes on the still-open list.

- "Who signs and witnesses" became "state's signing rules": some states need no witness, and others need a notary or the agent's acceptance (MI).
- "Original" kept: the POLST original stays with the person.
- Astra's "In conversation, cover only the next step" adopted: it stops the checklist recital.

Also consider changing "It carries no legal force" (the "What matters to me" paragraph) to "It is not a legal form or a medical order", per row 1.
