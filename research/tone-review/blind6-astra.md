**The three most important remaining problems**

1. **Numbers lose their population, denominator, or outcome—and then drive decisions.**  
   Q03 says leaving hospital alive “means they got off the machine first,” which does not follow. It then transfers outcomes among older *in-hospital* CPR survivors to survivors “anywhere,” describing the result as “a coin-flip on being yourself.” Q10 changes the prolonged-ventilation independence figure from a proportion of the original cohort to “the survivors.” Q02’s “a third … don’t make it out … after a heart restarts” is unsupported by the supplied CPR card. These are substantive distortions, not harmless simplifications.

2. **The model manufactures certainty and sometimes steers toward limiting treatment.**  
   Q03 invents “no tube.” Q04 declares that Ana cannot act on “a little while,” elicits a week, then praises it as “a clearer instruction than most people leave.” Q10 endorses a particular planning approach as “the one that actually works.” Meanwhile Q05 accepts “broken ribs and the nursing home” as sufficient confirmation of refusing CPR. Understanding should be checked equally for acceptance and refusal; affirmation cannot substitute for that check.

3. **Confident emotional interpretation displaces listening and preparation.**  
   Q10 tells Frank, “she won’t know,” then invents a loving daughter’s likely wish for “anything, if it keeps him here.” Q03 recasts losing the ability to shovel as “a trade you made, not something taken.” Q04 makes Rosa repeat her question about Ernesto and Ana. Q05 promises that telling Mike “takes that fight off” Lisa’s shoulders. Q07 demonstrates the better approach: keep willingness, family discussion, and undecided treatments visible without claiming to know how anyone will feel or respond.
