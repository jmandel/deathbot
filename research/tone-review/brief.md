# Tone review brief

You are reviewing the tone and conversational shape of Deathbot, an AI that talks with mostly older US adults (many Medicare age, some seriously ill) about end-of-life care: what matters to them, what treatments are really like, who would speak for them, and writing it down. Work read-only; do not edit any project file. Write your answer only where told.

The owner's aim: Deathbot should be the best conversation about dying this person has ever had — clear, honest, warm, neutral about what they choose, and in the hands of the person — using what frontier models do well. The owner dislikes AI-sounding prose ("slop"): stock empathy phrases, slogans, groups of three, set-piece contrasts, lecture-length replies, headers and bold in conversation, invented euphemisms or nicknames for medical things, and caveat padding. He wants general guidance that leads the model to behave well, not piles of special cases or word lists.

Read:
- The skill's tone and conversation guidance: skill/deathbot/SKILL.md (especially "The rules that do not bend"), skill/deathbot/guide/voice.md, skill/deathbot/guide/conversation.md, skill/deathbot/guide/safety.md, skill/deathbot/guide/failure-cases.md.
- How expert human facilitators do this: research/episode-180-cacp-transcript.md (a podcast where Kerri Petrin and Marissa McNichols of Aledade describe their comprehensive advance care planning facilitators: motivational interviewing, open questions instead of yes/no, reflective listening, staying neutral, 90-minute conversations or weeks, not forcing people where they aren't ready, involving the decision-maker, and a COPD patient very like ours), and research/conversation-guides.md (Serious Illness Conversation Guide, VitalTalk REMAP, Elwyn's three-talk model, Sudore & Fried).
- The transcript of the latest demo recording, which shows the AI's CURRENT behaviour and tone and is your main evidence: research/tone-review/transcript-v3.md (Gemini 4 Hydrogen, current skill, simulated patient Frank, 74, severe COPD). Base your findings on it. An earlier recording, research/tone-review/transcript-v2.md (Gemini 3.8 Flash, an earlier version of the skill), is there only for contrast: mention it only where it shows something v3 does not, and do not spend your review on problems that v3 no longer has. In both, lines after "## DEATHBOT" are the AI; text inside <replies> blocks became tappable suggestion buttons. Frank's lines are scripted by us and are not under review.

Answer, plainly and specifically (quote the transcript lines you mean):
1. Where the AI's turns in transcript-v3 fall short of best practice and of what Kerri and Marissa describe. Consider: pacing and readiness, open vs closed questions, reflection, how hard facts are delivered and followed up, neutrality and steering (including through which buttons are offered), whose words end up in the documents, warmth vs sentimentality, length, register for an older adult, and any phrasing that reads as AI-written.

3. How you would shape the tone guidance: the few principles that matter most, what to cut, and what to change. Keep it general, short and in plain language. Where you propose replacement wording, write it out in full, ready to paste.
