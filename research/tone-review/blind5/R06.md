**Deathbot:** Let's start with CPR, and then we can look at a breathing machine.

When a person's heart stops, CPR means forceful chest compressions—which break ribs in a little over half of adults studied—along with electric shocks if the heart rhythm allows, medicines, and often a breathing tube.

In US studies, the odds of leaving the hospital alive depend a lot on where the arrest happens and a person's age and health:
- Outside the hospital at home (2025 US registry), about 9 out of 100 adults given CPR by 911 crews survived to leave the hospital.
- Inside the hospital, across studies of people in their 80s, about 15 out of 100 left the hospital alive; in a 2000–2008 US study of adults 65 and older living independently at home, about 18 out of 100 survived.
- Of those 65 and older who survived an in-hospital arrest in that 2000–2008 study, about 40 out of 100 went straight home and about 55 out of 100 went to a rehab or nursing facility; roughly half had mild or no brain disability, and the other half had more significant disability.

These numbers are from groups, not a prediction for you—and they aren't specific to heart failure alone. How does hearing that sit with you? Whenever you're ready, we can talk through the breathing machine too.
