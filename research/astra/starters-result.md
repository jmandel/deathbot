# Astra's review of the welcome starters (2026-11-14)

Prompt: `starters-prompt.md`. Model: gpt-6-astra via Codex CLI, read-only.

The biggest gap is an ordinary, unhurried reason to be here: planning ahead. I agree with your criteria, with one qualification: five starters cannot represent every circumstance. Cover distinct ways into the conversation without making each button carry several situations.

Neutrality does **not** require pairing “less treatment” with “more treatment” here. These should describe reasons for arriving, before anyone has reached a treatment preference.

The current starters:

1. **“What is this, exactly?”** Strong: short, believable, welcoming to curiosity and skepticism. The introduction does not make it redundant; someone may still want an explanation.
2. **“I've been diagnosed with something serious.”** Useful, but centers the diagnosis event. “Living with” also welcomes someone who has been ill for years.
3. **“I watched someone die badly and I don't want that.”** The weakest. It supplies both a judgment and a conclusion, and exceeds your length limit. A bad death can involve unwanted treatment *or* inadequate treatment; the wording nevertheless primes avoidance before hearing the story.
4. **“I just want to get my wishes written down.”** A legitimate practical request, but “just” sounds defensive and “my wishes” assumes they already know. It misses someone holding a confusing form.

My recommended five, in this order:

1. **“I'm wondering what this is.”** An easy entrance for curiosity or skepticism, without requiring either enthusiasm or disclosure.
2. **“I want to plan ahead.”** Gives the mostly well majority a clear place; does not require declaring themselves healthy.
3. **“I'm living with a serious illness.”** Signals personal stakes without assuming prognosis, fear, or readiness to decide.
4. **“I want to talk about someone who died.”** Opens space for the story without calling the death bad or extracting a lesson from it.
5. **“I need help with a form.”** Gives the brisk or externally prompted visitor a concrete opening; the AI can ask which form and what help they need.

All are nine words or fewer. The fourth deliberately gives grieving visitors a recognizable entrance, at the cost of not explicitly covering a witnessed illness that did not end in death.

Alternates worth considering:

- **“I want to get my wishes written down.”** Better than the current version. Loses narrowly to the form starter because planning ahead already offers an entrance to documenting wishes.
- **“I may need to make decisions for someone.”** The strongest omitted category, and consistent with the skill’s emphasis on preparing decision-makers. It loses because the main invitation concerns the visitor’s own planning; use it instead of the death starter if proxy preparation becomes an explicit welcome-screen promise.
- **“I'm not sure where to start.”** Welcoming and low pressure, but gives the AI less direction and overlaps with curiosity. The visible free-text box supplies the escape route.

For the surrounding copy, I’d soften the heading’s implied control over how death unfolds while keeping the subject explicit:

> **Talk about care at the end of life.**  
> I'm an AI that helps you understand care choices, think about who could speak for you, and write down what matters to you—at your pace.  
> **What brought you here? Pick a starting point, or type your own.**

That last line follows the guide’s opening directly. Treat a tap as permission to explore that topic, never as confirmation of a wish or readiness to decide.
