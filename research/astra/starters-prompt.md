You are helping choose the opening suggestions on the welcome screen of Deathbot, a web app where an AI talks with people (mostly Medicare-age US adults arriving from a social-media post; some very sick, most not) about end-of-life planning: what matters to them, what treatments are really like, who would speak for them, and writing it down. Read skill/deathbot/SKILL.md, skill/deathbot/guide/conversation.md (especially "Offering ready-made answers", "Opening", and "Learning first, deciding later") and skill/deathbot/guide/voice.md for the product's stance. Do not edit any files.

The welcome screen shows a heading ("Talk through how you want the end of your life to go."), one sentence saying it is an AI and what it does, then "Start with one of these, or type your own below." and a short list of tappable starters. Tapping one sends it verbatim as the person's first message; the AI then responds. The current starters are:
1. What is this, exactly?
2. I've been diagnosed with something serious.
3. I watched someone die badly and I don't want that.
4. I just want to get my wishes written down.

The owner feels these are not particularly well considered as a set. Critique them, then propose a better set.

What a good set does, as I see it (challenge this if you disagree):
- Covers the main real reasons people arrive, so most people see themselves in one: curiosity or skepticism; living with a serious illness; healthy but planning ahead; a death or illness they witnessed; a practical task (a form, a DNR, a hospital asked); possibly someone else's situation (a parent, being asked to be someone's decision-maker), if that fits the product's scope.
- Neutral: does not lean toward less treatment or more. "I watched someone die badly and I don't want that" leans one way; consider whether the set needs balance or a neutral wording.
- Written as the person would say it, first person, plain, short enough to scan on a phone at large text (aim for under about 8 words, never more than 12).
- Each gives the AI a useful, distinct opening, and none presumes a conclusion or puts words in the person's mouth.
- Four or five items; the free-text box is always there.
- Works for someone frightened, someone brisk, someone grieving, someone skeptical.

Deliver: (a) a short critique of each current starter; (b) your recommended set of 4-5, with one line of reasoning each; (c) two or three alternates worth considering and why they lost; (d) anything about the surrounding copy (heading, "Start with one of these…") that should change to fit. Be concise and concrete.
