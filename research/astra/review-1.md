1. **The plan grants clinical authority before establishing clinical reliability.** “Full, honest, personalised clinical information,” unrestricted research, and optional clinician review are incompatible with public use by potentially sick strangers. A second model checking numbers will miss inappropriate populations, missing alternatives, and unsupported individual prognosis. Require clinician review of the initial cards and difficult conversation examples before public release. Separate established facts, population estimates, and uncertain application to this person. Unsupported personalization should trigger a precise question for their clinician, while useful education continues.

2. **You risk building a persuasive experience that mistakes agreement for understanding.** “Never recommend” does not prevent steering through scenario selection, repeated challenges, social proof, or emotionally loaded images. Remove unsolicited statements about what similar patients choose. Replace “advocates for deciding” with “supports understanding, including remaining undecided.” Require teach-back, symmetrical challenges to treatment acceptance and refusal, and summaries that preserve uncertainty. Personalized images should leave tranche 1.

3. **There is no credible test of the central product claim.** Simulated patients cannot establish that real older adults understand more, feel less pressured, or communicate better. The transcript’s service includes physician referral, experienced clinicians, and document follow-up; the proposed product inherits none of those protections. Add a small, observed formative pilot before broad social-media release. Compare against an ordinary frontier-model conversation and a good human-facilitated conversation. A clinical outcomes trial can wait; real-user evaluation cannot.

Answers to section 11:

1. **Phases:** Useful as internal coverage checks, poor as a sequence. Use a loop: elicit a concern, offer relevant information, check understanding, explore implications, revisit values. “Who speaks for you?” belongs near the beginning. Allow a useful ten-minute session without completing the map.

2. **Information stance:** Coherent if “faithful” means faithful to evidence and the person’s values. Incoherent if it means confident personalization without adequate information. Equal word counts are not balance; relevance, uncertainty, benefits, burdens, and available alternatives matter. Permit direct correction of false beliefs. Do not turn “I hate hospitals” into an inconsistency with accepting hospitalization: people reasonably trade one value against another.

3. **Balance testing:** Paired personas help, but free-running conversations quickly diverge. Replay identical histories with only the preference changed; compare the immediate response and subsequent trajectory. Check which facts are omitted, which preferences get challenged, and how often the bot seeks reconfirmation. Equal depth should apply to comparable informational needs, not every interaction.

4. **Forms:** Official forms are a starting point, not a legal-validity guarantee. Five states is too much initially; unrestricted search-based guidance for the other states is worse. Start with official links and a preparation checklist. Later support one jurisdiction with reviewed execution rules, versioning, revocation guidance, and explicit confirmation of every consequential field. New York, for example, limits an agent’s authority over artificial nutrition and hydration unless the agent knows the person’s wishes. That is not merely a PDF-mapping problem. [New York Attorney General](https://ag.ny.gov/health-care-proxy)

5. **Packaging:** One content source is realistic; equivalent behavior across hosts is unproven. Start with one supported host and test another as a portability check. Define what must work without browsing or file access. Replace “no save-file mechanism needed” with an explicit, user-reviewed checkpoint that can be exported and reconfirmed on return.

6. **Embarrassments:** The plan overreads both sources. Aledade’s “three times as many died” does not establish facilitator capacity as the bottleneck: 80% were never referred. “POLST is where trials showed care changing” converts a pattern across bundled interventions into a causal conclusion. The evidence summary also labels less utilization “improved” without consistently establishing preference concordance. And favorable ACP evidence does not establish this bot’s effectiveness.

7. **Build order:** Move a small reviewed knowledge set and concrete failure cases into M1. Then build the harness, iterate on conversation quality, conduct observed user sessions, and only afterward expand knowledge and packaging. Forms and images should not delay finding out whether the conversation helps.

Several clinical and ethical details need stronger treatment.

“Both branches” is too binary. Decisions may involve short trials, reassessment, noninvasive support, rehabilitation, and changing goals. Distinguish temporary dependence from permanent outcomes. Explicitly prevent summaries from converting a dislike of prolonged ventilation into refusal of all intubation. Likewise, DNR addresses CPR, not every other treatment. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000473.htm)

Home comfort care must include practical constraints, caregiver willingness, and financial realities. A peaceful home image can imply support that does not exist; Medicare hospice does not generally cover room and board. [Medicare](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/hospice-care)

The image module’s main problem is persuasion, not misplaced tubing. Showing several paths does not equalize emotional impact or communicate their probabilities. Putting someone’s face into a scene suggests personal prediction. Values-first ordering does not prevent later preference shaping. A vision judge cannot validate psychological impact. If revisited, test generic educational visuals first and measure comprehension, distress, and directional preference changes.

Safety needs actions, not “changes how the session proceeds.” Specify responses to urgent symptoms, imminent self-harm, passive wishes for death, coercion, and confusion. Do not equate treatment refusal with suicidality or dementia with incapacity. Establish whose words are being recorded; offer private participation when feasible. Pause consequential form completion when voluntariness or understanding is doubtful. State that nobody is monitoring or dispatching help. Explain host-dependent privacy before accepting records or photos, and never invent retention or deletion guarantees.

The simulated-patient harness will detect obvious rule violations and regressions. It will miss shared model assumptions, acquiescent “patients,” emotional harm, and actual comprehension. Add:

- Hidden, independently authored cases with vague answers, contradictions, fatigue, misunderstanding, and changing preferences.
- Acute symptoms, caregiver impersonation, disability bias, unsupported prognosis, stale sources, malicious uploaded instructions, and interrupted sessions.
- Summary checks for omissions, attribution, conditions, uncertainty, and revisions—not just invented statements. A pressured “yes” is still confirmation.
- Repeated runs and held-out cases; assess severe failures separately so good average scores cannot conceal them.
- Human review calibrated with a clinician and older adults. Check consequential claims against sources, not merely against potentially flawed cards.

Drop open-question counts and patient talk share as primary quality metrics. They reward a conversational style rather than useful reasoning.

For a substantially better thought partner, prioritize remembering exact qualifications, noticing tradeoffs without manufacturing contradictions, explaining uncertainty, and rehearsing what a proxy would do in an unfamiliar scenario. Measure whether users can explain options afterward, including what remains unknown.

Cut state-form filling, personalized images, PDF polish, uploaded-record interpretation, unrestricted clinical research, and four-host release commitments from tranche 1. Keep a focused conversation, a handful of reviewed cards, teach-back, proxy rehearsal, an editable summary, an exportable checkpoint, and serious evaluation.
