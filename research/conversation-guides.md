# How facilitators pace these conversations

Sources behind `skill/deathbot/guide/conversation.md`, "Learning first, deciding later" (added 2026-11-14 after reviewing demo transcripts in which Deathbot asked for a treatment choice immediately after explaining the treatment).

| Source | What it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| Serious Illness Conversation Guide, Ariadne Labs (2016 version as adapted by Fraser Health, 2017; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). [PDF](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/-/media/Project/FraserHealth/FraserHealth/Health-Professionals/Clinical-resources/Advance-Care-Planning---Serious-Illness/Serious_Illness_Conversation_Guide.pdf) | Order of a conversation: ask permission; assess understanding and how much information the person wants; share prognosis, then "allow silence, explore emotion"; explore goals, fears and worries, sources of strength, critical abilities, trade-offs ("how much are you willing to go through for the possibility of gaining more time?") and family ("How much does your family know about your priorities and wishes?"); close by summarising what was heard, then check in ("How does this plan seem to you?"). Treatment planning comes last, built from what was heard. |
| VitalTalk REMAP talking map, via CAPC's goals-of-care script. [CAPC](https://www.capc.org/conversation-script-goals-of-care/), [VitalTalk](https://vitaltalk.org/topics/reset-goals-of-care/) | Reframe, expect emotion, map values, align, plan. Ask what the person knows before telling; ask permission before sharing and before moving to a plan; pause after emotional moments; ask several exploratory questions about values, not one or two; reflect back in their words before planning. "I have to think about that some more" is a signal to slow down, not to push. |
| Elwyn G et al. A three-talk model for shared decision making. BMJ 2017;359:j4891. [PMC5683042](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5683042/) (and the 2012 original, J Gen Intern Med 27:1361, [PMC3445676](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3445676/)) | Team talk (support when options are first raised, elicit goals), option talk (compare alternatives), decision talk ("arriving at decisions that reflect the informed preferences of patients"). The model "depicts conversational steps … before deliberation based on informed preferences": time to deliberate sits between learning the options and deciding. Notes that some patients feel anxious when told about options and fear being left to decide alone. |
| Sudore RL, Fried TR. Redefining the "planning" in advance care planning: preparing for end-of-life decision making. Ann Intern Med 2010;153:256-261. [PubMed 20713793](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20713793/), [PMC2935810](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2935810/) | The objective of advance care planning should be preparing patients and surrogates to take part in the best possible in-the-moment decisions, not fixing treatment choices in advance; preferences change as people adapt to illness. Practical emphasis: choose a surrogate, clarify values, and decide how much leeway the surrogate has. |
| The ACO Show, episode 180 (`episode-180-cacp-transcript.md`) | Aledade's facilitators: open-ended questions instead of yes/no ("What would be important for your family to know about your wishes when it comes to life support?"), reflective listening, about 90 minutes and sometimes weeks, "we don't want to force patients to places that they're not ready to be", include the decision-maker, and a COPD patient who reached his own conclusion once the realities were laid out. |

Not obtained: the Respecting Choices First Steps facilitator guide (paid course material); the task tables in the Elwyn papers (figures not in the open text).
