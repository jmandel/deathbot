# Fact cards: what a human reviewer needs to look at

All five cards have had two rounds of independent verification and fixes (2026-11-13) and are still `draft`. These are the points the authors could not settle. Details of every finding and fix are in each card's `.resolution.md` and `.verify*.md` files under `skill/deathbot/cards/`.

Across all cards: many studies were read as abstracts only, because publisher sites block scripted access. Someone with journal access should spot-check the figures named below.

## CPR (`cpr.md`)

1. Prognosis timing after the heart restarts: confirm the current AHA 2025 wording and decide whether it belongs under "CPR, then reassess". The sentence was removed because the guideline could not be opened.
2. Exceptions to default CPR and what emergency crews honour, including verbal family requests: AHA 2025 ethics guidance and state rules were not read.
3. GO-FAR score: read the 2015 erratum and the 2021 patient-count notice, then decide whether the item list and group results (0.9%, 1.7%, 9.4%, 27.5%) go back in.
4. Currency: most older-adult cohorts are 2000 to 2012 (dialysis, cancer, Medicare, survivors). Should a 2023 in-hospital survival figure of 23.6% replace or sit beside the 22.6% row?
5. Abstract-only figures: age bands (van Gijn), cancer (Bruckel), frailty (Mowbray; Wharton is one UK hospital where 1.8% is about one patient), family-presence trial (Jabre).
6. Abbo paper: read from a copy on a university site; it says 2 to 70 hours in the abstract and 2 to 72 in the text.
7. Unsourced reasoning in "The other paths": that other care continues under a DNR, the burden of declining CPR, the "CPR, then reassess" description.
8. Is pointing to the comfort-care card enough, or does this card need its own account of that path?
9. No data found: advanced dementia; out-of-hospital arrest by older age band or by illness; partial orders such as DNI alone.
10. No vetted patient video. The Canadian decision-aid video (vimeo.com/48147363) has not been watched.

## Mechanical ventilation (`ventilation.md`)

1. Confirm in a browser the figures from six sources the verifier could not open: Rotondi 2002 (two thirds of 150 survivors remembered the tube or ICU); Wunsch 2010 (34.5% died in hospital, 30.8% went straight home); Barnato 2011 (72.5% against 23.9% dead at one year); Heyland 2015 (44% dead at 12 months, 26% of 505 back to baseline); Carson 2012 (ProVent day-21 version); Wilson 2018 (56% and 32% survival and the disease split).
2. Pandharipande row: percentages are of those tested at 12 months, the categories overlap, and they are score comparisons, not diagnoses. Is the wording fair?
3. Teno dementia figures: the paper's text says "greater than 80%" and its table shows 67.5% and 70.0%. Show both, or prefer one?
4. "Not intubating" is the thinnest-sourced path: the symptom description has no time course, and the opioid evidence is not specific to the last days of life. Should the 2024 European Respiratory Society guideline's distinction be added? (It was not read.)
5. High-flow oxygen: nothing on what it is like, its burdens or limits; the one trial cited is Brazilian with a mean age of 64.
6. Mask ventilation: check the plain-language renderings and whether do-not-intubate survival figures are presented fairly.
7. ProVent 14: check the instruction that it applies only from day 14, is scored by the ICU team, and only illustrates for advance planning.
8. Currency: most cohorts are 1996 to 2014. A 2024 Medicare study covering 2010 to 2019 exists and was not read.
9. Decision authority: no state law was read; the "do not intubate" versus "do not resuscitate" distinction is now absent from this card.
10. "7 in 10 get delirium" comes from a patient page with no study behind it.
11. Unsourced reasoning: "much of the harm comes from the illness, not the machine"; "at home family usually give the medicines".

## Comfort-focused care and hospice (`comfort-care-and-hospice.md`)

1. The last-days description and symptom treatment rest on one lay page: check that noisy breathing usually does not distress the dying person and that going without food or water is generally not painful.
2. Morphine and survival: no source read; the card says nothing on whether it shortens life. Does the bot need a sourced statement?
3. Palliative sedation: check the wording and whether Maltoni 2012 and Cochrane 2015 are still the best evidence.
4. Ventilator removal: median 0.93 hours is from an abstract and covers only patients who died in or soon after the ICU. Confirm the full-text "93.2% within 24 hours" and consider whether to say some people survive removal.
5. The "without CPR" sentence is reasoning with no source; check against the CPR and documents cards.
6. Survival paragraph: are Temel 2010, the 2016 pooled analysis and Connor 2007 a fair, current summary? Is "stopping a life-sustaining treatment shortens life" put well?
7. Other paths (time-limited trial, hospital comfort care) are the author's reasoning and thinner than the hospice material.
8. Which treatments hospices make people stop (dialysis, transfusions, chemotherapy): the only data are from 2008 to 2009.
9. Abstract-only: spot-check Teno 2016, Kumar 2017, Wright 2016, Anhang Price 2023, Hui 2015.
10. Benefits specialist: Medicaid room-and-board wording and the respite cap against current CMS text.

## The documents (`documents.md`)

Legal reviewer:
1. Is "not able to make decisions about your care" an acceptable general statement of when a living will or proxy takes effect, given immediately effective proxies and state rules on who determines capacity?
2. The interstate-recognition sentence rests on CaringInfo's summary alone.
3. The emergency-crew statements rest on National POLST, not on any state EMS rule or statute.
4. The 42 CFR 489.102 paraphrases (covered provider types, no conditioning of care, conscience refusal only where state law allows).
5. NIA's statement that a provider who refuses "must" tell the proxy and "consider" transfer: safe as a general statement?
6. The 2016 default-surrogate count (41 of 51 jurisdictions): keep as dated history or replace with the ABA's newer chart, which could not be opened?
7. "Most states also require the patient's or surrogate's signature" on a POLST; "a lawyer is not required".
8. Now omitted and possibly needed: the right to revoke or overrule an agent while capable; that treatment can be stopped by agreement without a living will.

Clinician reviewer:
1. The four burden statements marked "(reasoning, no source)" under "The other paths".
2. Do the treatment-level descriptions from the National POLST guidance match forms in real use?
3. The card now has no figure for how often emergency crews follow POLST orders; Richardson 2014's full text would restore it.
4. Abstract-only: Wendler, Grudzen, Shalowitz, Lee, Vranas. No search for POLST trials after February 2020; no video search.

## Illness trajectories and uncertainty (`trajectories-and-uncertainty.md`)

1. With Murray 2005 removed, the card no longer says that organ-failure flare-ups can each end in death or partial recovery, and no longer describes prolonged dwindling in dementia. Restore from the BMJ 2005 full text (PMID 15860828)?
2. Why prognosis is hardest in organ failure: now only three indirect findings and no explanation. Source it or accept the gap.
3. Lunney 2003: is the later time point for cancer and organ failure the final month or three months before death?
4. Abstract-only: Gill 2010 (67.9% of 53; 50% of 10), Christakis and Lamont, Gupta 2024, Fox 1999, Levenson 2000 (54%), Lee 2006 score bands, Kalogeropoulos 2009 (11.0% vs 9.2%).
5. Tool currency: are the Lee, Schonberg, BODE, Seattle (2006) and Palliative Prognostic Score (1999) figures still the ones to cite? Later validations exist and were not read.
6. Should the bot walk a person through the Lee or Schonberg index at all, and is "the model estimates about N in 100" acceptable wording?
7. "What it is like" and "The other paths" are almost all reasoning with no source.
8. Does the Medicare paragraph keep every condition on the Medicare.gov hospice page?
9. "Hope for the best, and prepare for the worst": only the citation was confirmed.
10. Only three reading links, none explaining trajectories for patients; video playback not tested.
